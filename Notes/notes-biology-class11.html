Chapter 1: The Living World

Diversity in the living world, taxonomic categories, and taxonomical aids.

Key Concepts

  • Characteristics of Living Organisms: Growth, reproduction, metabolism, cellular organization, and consciousness.
  • Binomial Nomenclature: System introduced by Carolus Linnaeus. Two components: Generic name and specific epithet (e.g., Mangifera indica).
  • Taxonomic Hierarchy: Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. (Trick: Keep Pots Clean Or Family Gets Sick).
  • Taxonomical Aids: Herbarium (dried pressed plant specimens), Botanical Gardens (living plants), Museum (preserved plant/animal specimens), Zoological Parks (wild animals in protected environments), Key (analytical tool based on contrasting characters).

Chapter 2: Biological Classification

Five kingdom classification and characteristics of major kingdoms.

Key Concepts

  • Five Kingdom Classification: Proposed by R.H. Whittaker (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) based on cell structure, body organization, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships.
  • Kingdom Monera: Prokaryotes. Includes Archaebacteria (extreme environments) and Eubacteria (true bacteria, cyanobacteria).
  • Kingdom Protista: Single-celled eukaryotes. Includes Chrysophytes (diatoms), Dinoflagellates, Euglenoids, Slime moulds, and Protozoans.
  • Kingdom Fungi: Heterotrophic, absorptive nutrition, cell wall of chitin. Includes Phycomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, Deuteromycetes.
  • Viruses & Viroids: Non-cellular, obligate parasites. Viroids are infectious RNA without a protein coat. Prions are infectious proteins.

Chapter 3: Plant Kingdom

Classification of plants and their life cycles.

Key Concepts

  • Algae: Simple, thalloid, autotrophic. Classes: Chlorophyceae (Green), Phaeophyceae (Brown), Rhodophyceae (Red).
  • Bryophytes: Amphibians of plant kingdom. Require water for fertilization. Includes liverworts and mosses. Main plant body is gametophyte (n).
  • Pteridophytes: First terrestrial plants with vascular tissues (xylem/phloem). Main plant body is sporophyte (2n). E.g., Ferns.
  • Gymnosperms: Plants with naked seeds (ovules not enclosed in ovary wall). E.g., Pinus, Cycas.
  • Angiosperms: Flowering plants. Seeds enclosed in fruits. Divided into Monocots and Dicots.
  • Alternation of Generations: Haplontic (algae), Diplontic (gymnosperms/angiosperms), Haplodiplontic (bryophytes/pteridophytes).

Chapter 4: Animal Kingdom

Basis of animal classification and major phyla.

Key Concepts

  • Basis of Classification: Level of organization (cellular, tissue, organ, organ-system), Symmetry (radial, bilateral), Diploblastic/Triploblastic, Coelom (acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, eucoelomate), Segmentation, Notochord.
  • Non-Chordates: Porifera (sponges), Coelenterata (cnidarians), Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes (flatworms), Aschelminthes (roundworms), Annelida (segmented worms), Arthropoda (jointed appendages, largest phylum), Mollusca (soft bodied), Echinodermata (spiny skinned), Hemichordata.
  • Chordata: Presence of notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits. Subphyla: Urochordata, Cephalochordata, Vertebrata.
  • Vertebrata Classes: Cyclostomata, Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fish), Osteichthyes (bony fish), Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves (birds), Mammalia.

Chapter 5: Morphology of Flowering Plants

External structure of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and fruits.

Key Concepts

  • Root: Tap root (dicots), fibrous root (monocots), adventitious root. Modifications for storage, support, respiration (pneumatophores).
  • Stem: Develops from plumule. Modifications: rhizome, tuber, tendrils, thorns, phylloclade.
  • Leaf: Photosynthetic organ. Venation (reticulate/parallel). Phyllotaxy (alternate/opposite/whorled).
  • Inflorescence: Arrangement of flowers on floral axis (Racemose - indefinite growth, Cymose - definite growth).
  • Flower: Reproductive unit. Parts: Calyx (sepals), Corolla (petals), Androecium (stamens), Gynoecium (carpels).
  • Fruit: Mature/ripened ovary. Parthenocarpic fruit (formed without fertilization).
  • Seed: Structure of dicot and monocot seeds.

Chapter 6: Anatomy of Flowering Plants

Internal structure and functional organization of higher plants.

Key Concepts

  • Meristematic Tissues: Actively dividing cells (Apical, Intercalary, Lateral meristems).
  • Permanent Tissues: Simple (Parenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma) and Complex (Xylem, Phloem).
  • Tissue Systems: Epidermal, Ground/Fundamental, Vascular tissue systems.
  • Dicot vs Monocot Anatomy: Differences in root (vascular bundles, pith), stem (arrangement of vascular bundles - ring vs scattered), and leaf (dorsiventral vs isobilateral).
  • Secondary Growth: Increase in girth in dicots due to vascular cambium and cork cambium. Formation of annual rings.

Chapter 7: Structural Organisation in Animals

Animal tissues and morphology/anatomy of cockroach/frog.

Key Concepts

  • Epithelial Tissue: Covering/lining. Simple (squamous, cuboidal, columnar) and compound (stratified).
  • Connective Tissue: Most abundant. Connects and supports. Includes loose (areolar, adipose), dense (tendons, ligaments), and specialized (cartilage, bone, blood).
  • Muscle Tissue: Skeletal (striated, voluntary), Smooth (non-striated, involuntary), Cardiac (striated, involuntary, intercalated discs).
  • Neural Tissue: Neurons (excitable cells) and neuroglia (supporting cells).
  • Morphology & Anatomy (Cockroach/Frog): Digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, nervous, and reproductive systems.

Chapter 8: Cell: The Unit of Life

Structure and function of cell organelles.

Key Concepts

  • Cell Theory: Schleiden and Schwann. Virchow added "Omnis cellula-e cellula" (cells arise from pre-existing cells).
  • Prokaryotic Cells: Lack membrane-bound nucleus/organelles. Have 70S ribosomes, plasmids, nucleoid.
  • Eukaryotic Cells: Have well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Plant vs Animal cells.
  • Plasma Membrane: Fluid mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson) - lipid bilayer with embedded proteins.
  • Endomembrane System: ER (Rough - protein synthesis, Smooth - lipid synthesis), Golgi apparatus (packaging/secretion), Lysosomes (hydrolytic enzymes), Vacuoles.
  • Mitochondria & Chloroplasts: Double membrane, semi-autonomous (contain circular DNA and 70S ribosomes). Mitochondria (ATP production), Chloroplasts (photosynthesis).
  • Nucleus: Nuclear envelope, chromatin, nucleolus.

Chapter 9: Biomolecules

Chemical constituents of living cells.

Key Concepts

  • Carbohydrates: Monosaccharides (glucose), Disaccharides (sucrose), Polysaccharides (starch, cellulose, glycogen). Glycosidic bonds.
  • Proteins: Polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. Primary, secondary (alpha-helix, beta-sheet), tertiary, and quaternary structures. Denaturation.
  • Lipids: Fatty acids and glycerol. Saturated (no double bonds) vs Unsaturated. Phospholipids in cell membranes.
  • Nucleic Acids: DNA (double helix, Watson-Crick model, A=T, G≡C) and RNA. Phosphodiester bonds.
  • Enzymes: Biological catalysts (mostly proteins). Lower activation energy. Affected by temp, pH, substrate concentration. Competitive vs non-competitive inhibition.

Chapter 10: Cell Cycle and Cell Division

Phases of cell growth and division.

Key Concepts

  • Cell Cycle: Interphase (G1, S, G2) and M phase (Mitosis/Meiosis). DNA replication occurs in S phase.
  • Mitosis (Equational Division): Prophase, Metaphase (chromosomes align at equator), Anaphase (sister chromatids separate), Telophase. Cytokinesis follows. Important for growth and repair.
  • Meiosis (Reductional Division): Occurs in germ cells. Halves chromosome number (2n → n).
    • Meiosis I: Prophase I is long (Leptotene, Zygotene-synapsis, Pachytene-crossing over, Diplotene, Diakinesis). Homologous chromosomes separate in Anaphase I.
    • Meiosis II: Similar to mitosis. Sister chromatids separate.
  • Significance of Meiosis: Maintains constant chromosome number across generations, introduces genetic variation through crossing over.

Chapter 11: Transport in Plants

Movement of water, minerals and food in plants.

Key Concepts

  • Means of Transport: Diffusion, Facilitated diffusion, Active transport.
  • Water Potential (Ψ_w): Measure of free energy of water. Ψ_w = Ψ_s (solute potential) + Ψ_p (pressure potential).
  • Osmosis & Plasmolysis: Movement of water across semipermeable membrane. Plasmolysis occurs in hypertonic solutions.
  • Long Distance Transport: Apoplast (through cell walls) vs Symplast (through cytoplasm/plasmodesmata) pathways.
  • Transpiration Pull: Cohesion-tension-transpiration pull model is the most accepted mechanism for upward movement of water in xylem.
  • Phloem Transport: Mass flow/Pressure flow hypothesis for translocation of sugars from source to sink.

Chapter 12: Mineral Nutrition

Essential elements and their roles in plant growth.

Key Concepts

  • Essential Elements: Macronutrients (C, H, O, N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and Micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo, Zn, B, Cl, Ni).
  • Criteria for Essentiality: Indispensable for normal growth/reproduction, specific requirement, direct involvement in metabolism.
  • Deficiency Symptoms: Chlorosis (loss of chlorophyll), Necrosis (death of tissue), Stunted growth.
  • Nitrogen Cycle: Nitrogen fixation (N₂ → NH₃), Nitrification (NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻), Denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂).
  • Biological Nitrogen Fixation: Symbiotic (e.g., Rhizobium in legumes) and free-living nitrogen fixers. Role of nitrogenase enzyme and leghemoglobin.

Chapter 13: Photosynthesis in Higher Plants

Synthesis of food using light energy.

Key Concepts

  • Site of Photosynthesis: Chloroplasts (grana for light reactions, stroma for dark reactions).
  • Light Reaction (Photochemical phase): Light absorption, water splitting (photolysis releasing O₂), ATP and NADPH formation. Non-cyclic and Cyclic photophosphorylation. Z-scheme.
  • Dark Reaction (Biosynthetic phase): Use of ATP/NADPH to fix CO₂.
    • C3 Pathway (Calvin Cycle): Carboxylation (RuBP + CO₂ → PGA), Reduction, Regeneration.
    • C4 Pathway (Hatch-Slack Pathway): Kranz anatomy. Initial acceptor is PEP. First product is OAA (4-carbon). Avoids photorespiration.
  • Photorespiration: Wasteful process in C3 plants when RuBisCO binds to O₂ instead of CO₂.
  • Factors affecting Photosynthesis: Light intensity, CO₂ concentration, temperature, water (Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors).

Chapter 14: Respiration in Plants

Cellular respiration and ATP production.

Key Concepts

  • Glycolysis: Occurs in cytoplasm. Partial breakdown of Glucose (6C) into two molecules of Pyruvic acid (3C). Net gain: 2 ATP, 2 NADH.
  • Aerobic Respiration:
    • Link Reaction: Pyruvic acid → Acetyl CoA (mitochondrial matrix).
    • Krebs Cycle (TCA Cycle): Occurs in matrix. Acetyl CoA + OAA → Citric acid. Generates NADH, FADH₂, ATP, CO₂.
    • Electron Transport System (ETS) & Oxidative Phosphorylation: Occurs on inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH/FADH₂ oxidized, electrons passed to O₂ (terminal acceptor) forming H₂O. Proton gradient drives ATP synthesis via ATP synthase. 1 Glucose ≈ 36-38 ATP.
  • Anaerobic Respiration (Fermentation): Incomplete oxidation. Lactic acid fermentation (muscles, some bacteria) and Alcoholic fermentation (yeast). Net gain: 2 ATP.
  • Amphibolic Pathway: Respiratory pathway involves both breakdown (catabolism) and synthesis (anabolism).
  • Respiratory Quotient (RQ): Vol CO₂ evolved / Vol O₂ consumed. RQ=1 for carbs, <1 for fats/proteins.

Chapter 15: Plant Growth and Development

Phases of plant growth and plant growth regulators.

Key Concepts

  • Growth Rates: Arithmetic (linear, L_t = L_0 + rt) and Geometric (sigmoidal/S-curve, W_1 = W_0 e^rt).
  • Differentiation, Dedifferentiation, Redifferentiation: Maturation of cells, regaining division capacity, and losing it again to mature.
  • Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs):
    • Auxins: Apical dominance, rooting, parthenocarpy.
    • Gibberellins: Internode elongation, seed germination, bolting.
    • Cytokinins: Cell division, delay leaf senescence.
    • Ethylene: Fruit ripening, senescence (gaseous PGR).
    • Abscisic Acid (ABA): Stress hormone, stomatal closure, seed dormancy.
  • Photoperiodism: Response to relative length of day/night (Long day, Short day, Day-neutral plants). Florigen.
  • Vernalization: Cold treatment required for flowering.

Chapter 16: Digestion and Absorption

Human digestive system and nutrient absorption.

Key Concepts

  • Alimentary Canal: Mouth, buccal cavity (teeth: diphyodont, thecodont, heterodont; dental formula 2123/2123), pharynx, esophagus, stomach (cardiac, fundic, body, pyloric), small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), large intestine, anus.
  • Digestive Glands: Salivary glands (salivary amylase), Liver (largest gland, secretes bile, emulsifies fats), Pancreas (exocrine part secretes pancreatic juice with trypsinogen, lipase, amylase).
  • Digestion Process:
    • Stomach: Pepsin digests proteins in acidic medium (HCl).
    • Small Intestine: Major site of digestion. Pancreatic enzymes and intestinal juice (succus entericus) complete digestion into monosaccharides, amino acids, fatty acids.
  • Absorption: Maximum in small intestine via villi/microvilli. Glucose/amino acids (active transport/facilitated diffusion to blood), Fatty acids/glycerol (form chylomicrons, enter lacteals/lymph).
  • Disorders: Jaundice, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, indigestion, PEM (Kwashiorkor, Marasmus).

Chapter 17: Breathing and Exchange of Gases

Mechanism of breathing and gas transport.

Key Concepts

  • Respiratory Organs: Nostrils → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli (site of gas exchange).
  • Mechanism of Breathing: Inspiration (diaphragm and external intercostals contract, volume increases, pressure decreases) and Expiration (relaxation).
  • Respiratory Volumes & Capacities:
    • Tidal Volume (TV): 500 mL.
    • Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV): 2500-3000 mL.
    • Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV): 1000-1100 mL.
    • Residual Volume (RV): 1100-1200 mL.
    • Vital Capacity (VC): ERV + TV + IRV.
  • Exchange of Gases: Occurs via simple diffusion based on partial pressure gradients (pO₂, pCO₂).
  • Transport of Gases:
    • O₂: 97% by RBCs as oxyhemoglobin, 3% dissolved in plasma. Oxygen dissociation curve is sigmoidal (Bohr effect).
    • CO₂: 70% as bicarbonate, 20-25% bound to hemoglobin (carbaminohemoglobin), 7% dissolved in plasma.
  • Regulation: Respiratory rhythm center in medulla, pneumotaxic center in pons.
  • Disorders: Asthma (inflammation of bronchi), Emphysema (alveolar walls damaged, often from smoking), Occupational respiratory disorders.

Chapter 18: Body Fluids and Circulation

Blood, lymph, and the human circulatory system.

Key Concepts

  • Blood Composition: Plasma (55% - water, proteins like albumin/globulin/fibrinogen) and Formed Elements (45% - Erythrocytes/RBCs, Leucocytes/WBCs, Thrombocytes/Platelets).
  • Blood Groups: ABO grouping (based on antigens A/B) and Rh grouping. Universal donor (O-), Universal recipient (AB+).
  • Coagulation: Cascade mechanism involving thrombokinase, prothrombin → thrombin, fibrinogen → fibrin.
  • Human Heart: 4 chambers (2 atria, 2 ventricles). Valves: Tricuspid (Right), Bicuspid/Mitral (Left), Semilunar.
  • Cardiac Cycle: Joint diastole → Atrial systole → Ventricular systole. Duration 0.8s. Cardiac Output = Stroke Volume (70mL) × Heart Rate (72 beats/min) ≈ 5000 mL/min.
  • ECG: P wave (atrial depolarization), QRS complex (ventricular depolarization), T wave (ventricular repolarization).
  • Double Circulation: Pulmonary circulation (Heart ↔ Lungs) and Systemic circulation (Heart ↔ Body).
  • Disorders: Hypertension (High BP), Coronary Artery Disease (Atherosclerosis), Angina, Heart Failure.

Chapter 19: Excretory Products and their Elimination

Human excretory system and urine formation.

Key Concepts

  • Nitrogenous Wastes: Ammonia (most toxic, ammoniotelic - aquatic animals), Urea (ureotelic - mammals, terrestrial amphibians), Uric acid (least toxic, uricotelic - birds, reptiles, insects).
  • Human Excretory System: Kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, urethra.
  • Nephron Structure: Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule (Malpighian body), PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT, Collecting duct.
  • Urine Formation:
    1. Glomerular Filtration: Ultrafiltration. GFR = 125 mL/min.
    2. Reabsorption: Essential substances (glucose, amino acids, Na⁺, water) reabsorbed mainly in PCT.
    3. Secretion: Active secretion of H⁺, K⁺, NH₃ into filtrate to maintain pH/ionic balance.
  • Concentration Mechanism: Counter-current mechanism involving Loop of Henle and vasa recta maintains medullary osmolarity gradient.
  • Regulation: ADH/Vasopressin (water reabsorption), RAAS mechanism (Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System responds to low BP/blood volume), ANF (vasodilator, lowers BP).
  • Disorders: Uremia, Renal calculi (kidney stones), Glomerulonephritis, Hemodialysis.

Chapter 20: Locomotion and Movement

Types of movement, muscles, and the skeletal system.

Key Concepts

  • Types of Movement: Amoeboid (macrophages), Ciliary (respiratory tract), Muscular.
  • Muscle Types: Skeletal (striated, voluntary), Visceral/Smooth (non-striated, involuntary), Cardiac (striated, involuntary, intercalated discs).
  • Skeletal Muscle Structure: Muscle fiber (cell) contains myofibrils. Sarcomere is the functional unit. Alternating light (I band - Actin) and dark (A band - Myosin) bands.
  • Sliding Filament Theory: Contraction occurs by sliding of thin (actin) filaments over thick (myosin) filaments. Requires Ca²⁺ and ATP.
  • Skeletal System (206 bones):
    • Axial Skeleton (80 bones): Skull, vertebral column, sternum, ribs.
    • Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones): Limbs (forelimbs, hindlimbs) and Girdles (pectoral, pelvic).
  • Joints: Fibrous (immovable - skull sutures), Cartilaginous (limited movement - intervertebral discs), Synovial (freely movable - ball & socket, hinge, pivot, gliding, saddle).
  • Disorders: Myasthenia gravis, Muscular dystrophy, Tetany, Arthritis, Osteoporosis, Gout.

Chapter 21: Neural Control and Coordination

Nervous system, nerve impulses, and sensory organs.

Key Concepts

  • Neuron Structure: Cell body, dendrites, axon. Myelinated vs unmyelinated fibers. Nodes of Ranvier.
  • Nerve Impulse: Resting membrane potential (-70mV, maintained by Na⁺/K⁺ pump). Action potential/Depolarization (influx of Na⁺). Repolarization (efflux of K⁺).
  • Synaptic Transmission: Arrival of impulse at axon terminal → Ca²⁺ influx → release of neurotransmitters (e.g., acetylcholine) into synaptic cleft → binding to receptors on post-synaptic membrane.
  • Central Nervous System (CNS):
    • Forebrain: Cerebrum (memory, intelligence), Thalamus (relay center), Hypothalamus (temperature, hunger, thirst, emotions).
    • Midbrain: Corpora quadrigemina (visual/auditory reflexes).
    • Hindbrain: Pons, Cerebellum (balance/coordination), Medulla (respiration, cardiovascular reflexes).
  • Reflex Action & Arc: Involuntary response to stimulus (Receptor → Sensory neuron → CNS → Motor neuron → Effector).
  • Sensory Organs:
    • Eye: Cornea, iris, lens, retina (rods/cones). Mechanism of vision.
    • Ear: Outer, Middle (ear ossicles: malleus, incus, stapes), Inner (cochlea for hearing, vestibular apparatus for balance). Organ of Corti.

Chapter 22: Chemical Coordination and Integration

Endocrine glands, hormones, and mechanism of action.

Key Concepts

  • Hypothalamus: Produces releasing and inhibiting hormones that control the anterior pituitary.
  • Pituitary Gland:
    • Anterior: GH (Growth Hormone), PRL (Prolactin), TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH.
    • Posterior: Stores and releases Oxytocin (uterine contraction, milk ejection) and ADH/Vasopressin (water reabsorption).
  • Thyroid Gland: Thyroxine (T4) and T3 regulate BMR. Calcitonin lowers blood Ca²⁺. Disorders: Goiter, Cretinism, Graves' disease (hyperthyroidism).
  • Parathyroid Gland: PTH (Parathyroid Hormone) increases blood Ca²⁺ (hypercalcemic).
  • Thymus: Thymosins (T-lymphocyte differentiation, cell-mediated immunity).
  • Adrenal Gland:
    • Cortex: Glucocorticoids (cortisol - stress, glucose metabolism), Mineralocorticoids (aldosterone - Na⁺/water reabsorption).
    • Medulla: Adrenaline (Epinephrine) and Noradrenaline - "Fight or Flight" response.
  • Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans): Insulin (lowers blood glucose) and Glucagon (raises blood glucose). Diabetes Mellitus.
  • Testis/Ovary: Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone.
  • Mechanism of Hormone Action: Peptide hormones bind to membrane receptors (generate second messengers like cAMP). Steroid hormones enter cell and bind to intracellular receptors to regulate gene expression.
