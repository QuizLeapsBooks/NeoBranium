Carbon and its Compounds High Weightage (6-8 Marks)

Why this chapter matters for Boards: Carbon forms the basis of all organic chemistry and living structures. CBSE frequently tests: Why carbon forms covalent bonds instead of ionic, cleansing action of soap & micelle formation, esterification & saponification, and distinguishing between ethanol and ethanoic acid.

1. Bonding in Carbon – The Covalent Bond

Carbon has atomic number 6. Its electronic configuration is 2, 4. To attain noble gas configuration (octet rule), it needs 4 electrons.

Why Carbon Cannot Form C4+ or C4- Ions:
  • It cannot gain 4 electrons (C4-): Carbon nucleus has 6 protons. Holding 10 electrons (6 + 4) would be extremely difficult due to strong inter-electronic repulsion and high nuclear energy requirements.
  • It cannot lose 4 electrons (C4+): Removing 4 electrons requires a tremendous amount of energy to overcome the nuclear attraction of 6 protons.

Solution: Carbon overcomes this problem by sharing its valence electrons with other carbon atoms or with atoms of other elements, forming covalent bonds.

2. Versatile Nature of Carbon

Over 3 million carbon compounds are known today. Two characteristic properties enable carbon to form such an enormous number of compounds:

  1. Catenation: Carbon has the unique ability to form strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms, resulting in long chains, branched chains, or cyclic rings. Carbon-carbon bonds are exceptionally strong and stable because of carbon's small atomic size.
  2. Tetravalency: Having a valency of 4, a carbon atom can bond with four other carbon atoms or monovalent/divalent atoms like Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Chlorine, and Sulphur.

3. Allotropes of Carbon

Allotropes are different physical forms of the same chemical element.

Allotrope Structure Properties & Conduction Uses
Diamond Rigid 3D tetrahedral network; each C bonded to 4 others. Hardest known natural substance; non-conductor of electricity (no free electrons). Glass cutting, rock drilling, jewelry.
Graphite Hexagonal layers held by weak van der Waals forces. Slippery, soft, conductor of electricity (due to free delocalized valence electron). Lubricant, pencil lead, electrodes.
Fullerenes (C-60) Spherical soccer-ball structure of 20 hexagons and 12 pentagons. Dark solid at room temperature; unique electronic and cage properties. Nanotechnology, drug delivery research.

4. Saturated and Unsaturated Carbon Compounds

  • Saturated Hydrocarbons (Alkanes): Carbon atoms joined only by single covalent bonds. General formula: CnH2n+2. Methane (CH4), Ethane (C2H6). Burn with a clean blue flame.
  • Unsaturated Hydrocarbons: Carbon atoms contain double or triple bonds.
    • Alkenes (Double bond C=C): General formula: CnH2n. Ethene (C2H4).
    • Alkynes (Triple bond C≡C): General formula: CnH2n-2. Ethyne/Acetylene (C2H2).
    Unsaturated hydrocarbons burn with a yellow sooty flame due to incomplete combustion.

5. Functional Groups & Homologous Series

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of an organic compound regardless of the length of the carbon chain.

Functional Group Formula Suffix / Prefix Example
Alcohol -OH -ol Ethanol (C2H5OH)
Aldehyde -CHO -al Ethanal (CH3CHO)
Ketone >C=O -one Propanone / Acetone (CH3COCH3)
Carboxylic Acid -COOH -oic acid Ethanoic Acid (CH3COOH)
Homologous Series Characteristics:
  1. All members share the same functional group and general chemical formula.
  2. Any two successive members differ by a -CH2 unit and 14 u molecular mass.
  3. They show similar chemical properties, with a gradual gradation in physical properties (melting point, boiling point, density).

6. Chemical Properties of Carbon Compounds

  1. Combustion: Carbon compounds burn in presence of oxygen to give CO2, H2O, heat, and light.
    CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + Heat & Light
  2. Oxidation: Alcohols are oxidized to carboxylic acids using alkaline KMnO4 or acidified K2Cr2O7.
    CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O
  3. Addition Reaction: Unsaturated hydrocarbons add hydrogen in presence of Nickel or Palladium catalyst to give saturated hydrocarbons (used in hydrogenation of vegetable oils into vanaspati ghee).
    R2C=CR2 + H2 → (Ni catalyst) → R2CH-CHR2
  4. Substitution Reaction: In presence of sunlight, chlorine replaces hydrogen atoms of saturated hydrocarbons one by one.
    CH4 + Cl2 → (Sunlight) → CH3Cl + HCl

7. Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid (Key Board Reactions)

A. Ethanol (C2H5OH)

  • Reaction with Sodium: 2Na + 2C2H5OH → 2C2H5ONa + H2↑ (Effervescence of hydrogen gas confirms alcohol).
  • Dehydration: Heating ethanol at 443 K with excess concentrated H2SO4 gives ethene.
    C2H5OH → (conc. H2SO4, 443K) → CH2=CH2 + H2O

B. Ethanoic Acid (CH3COOH)

  • Esterification Reaction: Reaction between ethanoic acid and ethanol in presence of acid catalyst yields sweet-smelling Ethyl Ethanoate (Ester).
    CH3COOH + C2H5OH → (conc. H2SO4) → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O
  • Saponification: Alkaline hydrolysis of ester with NaOH produces sodium ethanoate (soap) and ethanol.
    CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH
  • Reaction with Carbonates/Bicarbonates: Releases CO2 gas with brisk effervescence (turns lime water milky).
    CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2↑

8. Soaps and Detergents – Micelle Formation

Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids (e.g., sodium stearate, C17H35COO-Na+).

  • Structure of Soap Molecule:
    • Hydrophobic tail: Long hydrocarbon chain; insoluble in water, dissolves in oily dirt.
    • Hydrophilic head: Ionic COO-Na+ end; soluble in water, points outwards.
  • Micelle Formation: In water, soap molecules arrange radially with hydrophobic tails clustered inwards trapping oil/grease droplets, while hydrophilic heads point outwards into water. This emulsion is washed away by rinsing with water.
  • Hard Water Issue: Hard water contains Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions. Soap reacts with these to form an insoluble white precipitate called scum, wasting soap.
  • Detergents: Ammonium or sulphonate salts of long chain carboxylic acids. They do not form insoluble precipitate with Ca2+/Mg2+ ions and lather well even in hard water.

9. Most Repeated CBSE Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

  1. (CBSE 2024, 3M) What is a homologous series? List any two characteristics. Write molecular formula of the third member of alkynes.
  2. (CBSE 2023, 5M) An organic compound 'A' on heating with concentrated H2SO4 forms 'B', which on addition of hydrogen gives 'C'. Identify A, B, and C and write the balanced chemical equations.
  3. (CBSE 2022, 3M) Explain the cleansing action of soap with the help of a labeled diagram of a micelle. Why do soaps fail in hard water?
  4. (CBSE 2020, 2M) How would you distinguish experimentally between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid?
