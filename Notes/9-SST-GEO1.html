India – Size and Location High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? This chapter introduces India's exact location on the globe, its latitudinal and longitudinal extent, and its strategic position in the Indian Ocean. Questions on the Tropic of Cancer, Standard Meridian, and neighbouring countries are frequently asked in periodic assessments [citation:3][citation:7].

Introduction

India is a vast country located entirely in the Northern Hemisphere. It is the seventh-largest country in the world in terms of landmass [citation:6][citation:8]. This chapter explains India's location, size, and its relationship with neighbouring countries and the world.

1. Location of India

India's mainland extends between specific latitudes and longitudes [citation:3][citation:6]:

  • Latitudinal extent: 8°4'N to 37°6'N
  • Longitudinal extent: 68°7'E to 97°25'E

Tropic of Cancer

The Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N) divides India into almost two equal parts. It passes through several states including Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram [citation:3][citation:7].

Standard Meridian

India's longitudinal extent creates a time difference of about two hours between the eastern and western parts. To have a uniform time across the country, 82°30'E has been selected as the Standard Meridian of India. The Indian Standard Time (IST) is based on this meridian, which passes through Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh [citation:3][citation:6].

🎯 Key Point: India's location is strategically important because the Deccan Peninsula protrudes into the Indian Ocean, helping India establish close contact with West Asia, Africa, and Europe from the western coast, and with Southeast and East Asia from the eastern coast [citation:6].

2. Size of India

India's total geographical area is 3.28 million square km, which accounts for about 2.4% of the world's total geographical area [citation:3][citation:6].

📝 Countries Larger Than India

Russia, Canada, USA, China, Brazil, and Australia are larger than India in terms of area [citation:7].

3. India and Its Neighbours

India shares its land boundaries with several countries [citation:3][citation:8]:

  • Northwest: Afghanistan and Pakistan
  • North: China (Tibet), Nepal, and Bhutan
  • East: Myanmar and Bangladesh

Island Neighbours

  • Sri Lanka: Separated from India by the Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar
  • Maldives: Situated to the south of the Lakshadweep Islands [citation:7]

Island Groups of India

  • Lakshadweep: Located in the Arabian Sea, composed of coral islands. Kavaratti is the administrative headquarters [citation:7]
  • Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Located in the Bay of Bengal, southeast of the mainland. These are of great strategic importance [citation:7]

4. India and the World

India's central location at the head of the Indian Ocean has been significant since ancient times [citation:6][citation:8]:

  • Ancient Trade Routes: The famous Silk Route passed through the Indian subcontinent, facilitating exchange of ideas and commodities.
  • Cultural Exchange: Indian scriptures like the Upanishads, Ramayana, Gita, and Panchatantra stories reached other parts of the world.
  • Suez Canal: Opened in 1869, it reduced India's distance from Europe by 7,000 km, boosting trade [citation:3][citation:8].
  • Maritime Trade: About 90% of India's international trade is done through sea routes.
