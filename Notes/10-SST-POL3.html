Gender, Religion and Caste Board Exam Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains how social divisions based on gender, religion, and caste influence politics and democracy. It covers gender inequality, feminist movements, communalism, secularism, and the relationship between caste and politics. Board exams frequently test women's representation, forms of communalism, and the role of caste in elections.

1.1 Social Differences and Divisions

Social differences exist in every society and arise from variations in identity, background, and social position. These differences often lead to divisions and inequalities.

The commonly observed divisions relate to:

  • Gender — unequal roles assigned to men and women
  • Religion — differences based on faith and belief
  • Caste — hierarchical social structure based on birth

These divisions influence social interactions and also shape political debates and demands. When one group is placed above another, it creates unequal opportunities, unequal power, and unequal resources within society.

🎯 Key Point: Social differences become social divisions when they overlap with other differences. For example, the difference between Blacks and Whites in America became a social division when income factors were also observed — Blacks tended to be poor, while Whites were usually rich.

2. Gender and Politics

2.1 Gender Division — Social, Not Biological

The gender division tends to be understood as natural and unchangeable. However, it is not based on biology but on social expectations and stereotypes.

ConceptMeaning
SexBiological differences determined by reproductive organs. This is natural and unchangeable
GenderCharacteristics, behaviours, and roles that society assigns to males and females. These are created and reinforced by social beliefs and cultural practices

2.2 Sexual Division of Labour

Society assigns different roles to men and women. This creates a public–private split:

  • Public life (work outside home, decision-making, politics) is associated with men
  • Private life (household work, caregiving) is associated with women

Household work is largely performed by women but rarely receives recognition or economic value. The same tasks, when performed professionally outside the home, are paid and valued, often performed by men.

2.3 Patriarchy

Patriarchy is a social system in which men are regarded as superior to or possessing greater value than women. It also bestows more political and economic power in the hands of men.

As India is a male-dominated, patriarchal society, women face disadvantage, discrimination, and oppression in various ways:

AspectDiscrimination Faced by Women
LiteracyWomen's literacy rate is lower (54%) compared to men (76%)
WorkWomen work more hours daily, often unpaid. The Equal Remuneration Act of 1976 mandates equal pay for equal work, but inequality persists
EmploymentWomen have fewer high-paying positions; their work is undervalued
Sex RatioSex-selective abortion has led to a decline in child-sex ratio (914 girls per 1000 boys)
SafetyUrban areas have become particularly unsafe for women

2.4 Feminist Movements

Feminist: A woman or a man who believes in equal rights and opportunities for men and women.

Feminist Movements: These are radical women's movements dedicated to achieving gender equality in personal and family life, as well as in public affairs. These movements have organised and agitated to open up avenues for strengthening women's political and legal position, as well as their educational and employment prospects.

💡 Key Point: Women's participation in public life is particularly high in some regions of the world, such as Scandinavian countries like Sweden, Norway, and Finland.

3. Women's Political Representation

3.1 Low Representation in Legislatures

Women's political participation has been significantly lower than men's across the globe. Women's well-being can be improved by providing them with political power and placing them in decision-making and law-making roles.

LevelWomen's Representation
Lok SabhaAbout 10-12% of total strength (reached 12% in 2014)
State AssembliesLess than 5%
Global AverageAbout 19%

3.2 Reservation in Local Bodies

In Panchayats and Municipalities, one-third of seats are reserved for women. This has resulted in more than 10 lakh elected women representatives in rural and urban local bodies.

3.3 Women's Reservation Bill

Efforts to introduce similar reservation in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies have been made, but the law was never passed because political parties were unable to reach an agreement.

🎯 Key Point: Gender division is an example that some form of social division needs to be expressed in politics. This also shows that disadvantaged groups do benefit when social divisions become a political issue.

4. Religion, Communalism and Politics

4.1 Religion in Politics

Religious differences are often expressed in the field of politics. In India, there are followers of different religions. People should be able to express in politics their needs, interests, and demands as members of a religious community.

However, the use of religion in politics becomes problematic when it takes the form of communalism.

4.2 What is Communalism?

Communalism refers to the use of religion in politics when:

  • Beliefs of one religion are presented as superior to those of other religions
  • The demands of one religious group are formed in opposition to another
  • State power is used to establish the domination of one religious group over the rest

4.3 Forms of Communal Politics

FormDescriptionExample
Everyday BeliefsReligious prejudices, stereotypes of religious communities, and belief in the superiority of one's religionMilitant religious groups promoting intolerance
Quest for Political DominanceA communal mind often leads to a desire for political dominance of one's own religious communityMajoritarianism
Political MobilisationUse of sacred symbols, religious leaders, emotional appeal, and fear to bring followers of one religion together in the political arenaMobilising voters along religious lines
Communal ViolenceRiots and massacres between religious communitiesPartition of India riots
⚠️ Threat to Democracy: Communalism divides people based on religion, leading to conflicts. It can result in violence, riots, and hatred among communities. It encourages discrimination against religious minorities and weakens secularism.

5. Secular State

India is a secular state. The key features of India's secular state are:

FeatureExplanation
No Official ReligionThere is no official religion in the Indian state. The Constitution does not give special status to any religion
Freedom of ReligionThe Constitution provides to all individuals and communities the freedom to profess, practice, and propagate any religion or not to follow any
Prohibition of DiscriminationThe Constitution prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion
State InterventionThe Constitution allows the state to intervene in matters of religion in order to ensure equality, such as banning untouchability
🎯 Key Point: Secularism is vital to combat communalism. The Indian Constitution provides all individuals freedom to profess any religion and prohibits discrimination based on religion.

6. Caste and Politics

6.1 The Caste System

The caste system is a social hierarchy found mainly in India. It divides people based on birth and determines their social status and occupation. People from "lower castes" have historically faced discrimination and exclusion. Access to education, jobs, and social respect was often limited to "upper castes".

6.2 Changes in the Caste System

Modern India has witnessed several positive changes:

  • Urbanization and economic growth have reduced caste barriers
  • Education and occupational mobility have changed people's attitudes
  • The Constitution of India prohibits caste-based discrimination and declares untouchability a crime

However, caste-based divisions haven't disappeared completely. Many still marry within their caste, and some castes remain economically weaker due to historical disadvantages.

6.3 Caste in Politics

Caste continues to play a role in Indian politics:

  • Political parties choose candidates considering the caste composition of voters
  • Parties appeal to caste groups to secure votes
  • But since no constituency has only one caste, candidates must seek support from multiple communities

6.4 Why Caste Alone Cannot Determine Elections

Two reasons why caste alone cannot determine election results in India:

  • No parliamentary constituency in India has a clear majority of one single caste
  • No party wins the votes of all the voters of a caste or community

6.5 Positive Role of Caste in Politics

Caste-based organizations have also brought positive changes:

  • They help disadvantaged groups demand equal rights and representation
  • They have increased political awareness among marginalized communities
  • They push for social justice and access to resources like education and land
🧠 Mnemonic: DAME the Caste Politics
  • D — Discrimination (caste-based inequalities persist)
  • A — Awareness (marginalized communities becoming aware)
  • M — Mobilization (parties appeal to caste groups)
  • E — Equality (positive role in demanding rights)

7. Social Divisions and Democracy

7.1 Factors Determining Outcomes of Social Divisions

The factors which are important for deciding the outcomes of politics of social divisions are:

FactorExplanation
How People Perceive Their IdentitiesIf people see their identities in singular and exclusive terms, it becomes difficult to accommodate. It is easier if people see their identities as multiple and complementary with the national identity
How Political Leaders Raise DemandsIt is easier to accommodate demands within the constitutional framework and not at the cost of another community
How Government ReactsIf rulers are willing to share power and accommodate reasonable demands, social divisions become less threatening. If they try to suppress demands, the results may be the opposite

7.2 Balancing Diversity and Unity

India's strength lies in its diversity, and democracy ensures equal representation for all. The Constitution promotes:

  • Equality (no discrimination based on gender, religion, caste)
  • Secularism (state neutrality in religious matters)
  • Social justice (empowering weaker sections)
💡 Key Lesson: Political expression of social divisions is possible and sometimes quite desirable in a democratic system. Disadvantaged groups do benefit when social divisions become a political issue.

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Gender Division: Socially constructed roles based on expectations, not biology
  • Patriarchy: Social system where men are regarded as superior
  • Feminist: A person who believes in equal rights for men and women
  • Communalism: Use of religion in politics to divide people
  • Secular State: State with no official religion
  • Caste System: Social hierarchy based on birth
🎯 Key Comparisons:
  • Sex vs Gender: Sex is biological; Gender is socially constructed
  • Religion in Politics: Acceptable when expressing community needs; Problematic when it becomes communalism
🎯 Key Facts:
  • Women's representation: ~10-12% in Lok Sabha, <5% in State Assemblies
  • 1/3 seats reserved for women in Panchayats and Municipalities
  • More than 10 lakh elected women representatives in local bodies
  • Child sex ratio: 914 girls per 1000 boys
  • Equal Remuneration Act: 1976
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Confusing gender (social) with sex (biological)
  • Forgetting that secularism is about state neutrality, not anti-religion
  • Thinking caste alone determines elections — no constituency has a single caste majority
  • Mixing up the forms of communalism — everyday beliefs, political dominance, mobilisation, violence
  • Not mentioning 1/3 reservation for women in local bodies
  • Forgetting that Equal Remuneration Act was passed in 1976
