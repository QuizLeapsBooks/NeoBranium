Motion High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Motion lays the foundation for all of mechanics. Graphical representation and equations of motion are heavily tested in board exams.

Introduction: What is Motion?

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to time and a reference point. If the position does not change, the object is said to be at rest.

Reference Point and Frame of Reference

To describe the position of an object, we need a reference point (origin). An object may appear to be moving to one observer while being stationary to another. For example, a passenger inside a moving bus sees other passengers at rest, but an observer on the road sees them in motion. This is why a common reference frame is needed for consistent observations.

Types of Motion

  • Translatory Motion: Particles move from one point in space to another. This can be along a straight line (rectilinear) or a curved path (curvilinear). Example: a car on a straight road.
  • Rotatory Motion: Particles describe concentric circles about an axis. Example: Earth's rotation.
  • Vibratory Motion: Particles oscillate back and forth around a fixed point. Example: a pendulum.

1. Distance and Displacement

Distance (Scalar Quantity)

The total path length covered by an object during motion. Distance depends only on how much ground is covered, irrespective of direction. It is always positive and can never be zero for a moving object.

Displacement (Vector Quantity)

The shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final positions, measured in a specific direction. Displacement can be positive, negative, or zero.

🎯 Key Point: Distance is always greater than or equal to displacement. The magnitude of displacement never exceeds the total distance travelled.

📝 Example: Distance vs Displacement

If you walk 8 m towards the west and then 3 m towards the east:

  • Total distance = 8 + 3 = 11 m
  • Displacement = 8 − 3 = 5 m towards the west

📝 Example: Circular Path

A body moves along a circular path of radius r. After half a revolution:

  • Distance covered = π × r
  • Displacement = 2r (the diameter, since the particle is diametrically opposite)

Uniform vs Non-Uniform Motion

Uniform MotionNon-Uniform Motion
Covers equal distances in equal intervals of time Covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time
Speed remains constant Speed or direction changes
Example: Earth revolving around the Sun Example: A car in city traffic

2. Speed and Velocity

Speed (Scalar Quantity)

Speed is the rate at which an object covers distance. It tells us how fast an object is moving but does not specify direction.

Speed = Distance / Time   |   SI Unit: m/s

Velocity (Vector Quantity)

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement. It specifies both speed and direction.

Velocity = Displacement / Time   |   SI Unit: m/s

Average Speed vs Average Velocity

Average SpeedAverage Velocity
Total distance / Total time Total displacement / Total time
Always positive Can be positive, negative, or zero
📌 Important Formula: For uniform acceleration, Average Velocity = (Initial Velocity + Final Velocity) / 2 = (u + v) / 2

📝 Example: Average Speed for Round Trip

A car travels from A to B at 20 km/h and returns at 30 km/h. Let the distance be d.

  • Time going = d/20, Time returning = d/30
  • Total distance = 2d, Total time = d/20 + d/30
  • Average speed = 2d / (d/20 + d/30) = 24 km/h

3. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time. When velocity changes (either in magnitude or direction), the object is accelerating.

a = (v − u) / t

Where: v = final velocity, u = initial velocity, t = time taken. SI unit is m/s².

Key Points about Acceleration

  • Positive acceleration: Velocity increases with time
  • Negative acceleration (retardation): Velocity decreases with time
  • Zero acceleration: Velocity is constant (uniform motion)
⚠️ Important: A body moving with constant speed in a circular path has non-zero acceleration because its direction changes continuously. This is called centripetal acceleration.

📝 Example: Finding Acceleration

A train starting from rest attains a velocity of 72 km/h in 5 minutes. Find its acceleration.

Solution:

  • u = 0 m/s, v = 72 km/h = 72 × (5/18) = 20 m/s
  • t = 5 min = 300 s
  • a = (v − u) / t = (20 − 0) / 300 = 0.067 m/s²

4. Equations of Motion

These three equations apply only when acceleration is uniform (constant):

1. v = u + at    (Velocity–Time relation)

2. s = ut + ½at²    (Position–Time relation)

3. v² = u² + 2as    (Position–Velocity relation)

Graphical Derivation (Exam-Favourite)

These equations can be derived from the velocity-time graph:

Deriving v = u + at

From the v-t graph, slope of line = acceleration

a = (v − u) / t
→ v = u + at
Deriving s = ut + ½at²

Area under v-t graph = displacement

Area = area of rectangle + area of triangle
s = ut + ½ × t × (v − u)
→ s = ut + ½at²

How to Choose the Right Equation

GivenFindUse Equation
u, a, tvv = u + at
u, a, tss = ut + ½at²
u, v, asv² = u² + 2as
💡 Tips:
  • If a body starts from rest, u = 0
  • If a body comes to rest, v = 0
  • For retardation, use a = − (positive value)
  • Always convert km/h to m/s by multiplying with 5/18

📝 Example: Distance with Uniform Acceleration

A motorboat starts from rest with acceleration 3.0 m/s² for 8.0 s. Find the distance travelled.

Solution: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(8)² = 96 m

📝 Example: Two-Stage Motion

A car starts from rest with acceleration 5 m/s² for 8 s, then moves with constant velocity. Find total distance in 12 s.

Solution:

  • Phase 1: s₁ = ½(5)(8)² = 160 m; final velocity v = 5 × 8 = 40 m/s
  • Phase 2: s₂ = 40 × 4 = 160 m
  • Total distance = 320 m

6. Uniform Circular Motion

When an object moves in a circular path with constant speed, its motion is called uniform circular motion.

Key Characteristics

  • Speed is constant, but velocity changes continuously (direction changes)
  • Since velocity changes, there is acceleration (centripetal acceleration)
  • The acceleration is always directed towards the centre of the circle
⚠️ Common Exam Question: "A boy on a merry-go-round moving at constant speed of 10 m/s is in accelerated motion." This is TRUE because the direction of velocity changes continuously.

Formulas for Circular Motion

Speed (v) = 2πr / t

Where r = radius of circular path, t = time period (time for one revolution)

📝 Example: Satellite Speed

An artificial satellite orbits at radius 42,250 km and takes 24 hours for one revolution. Find its speed.

Solution: v = 2πr / t = (2 × 3.14 × 42250) / 24 = 3.069 km/s

Most Repeated Previous Year Questions

Short Answer (1-2 Marks)

  1. Differentiate between distance and displacement with examples. Most Imp
  2. What does the slope of a distance-time graph indicate? (1 Mark)
  3. Can displacement be zero when distance is not zero? Give an example. (1 Mark)
  4. What is the SI unit of acceleration? (1 Mark)

Numericals (3 Marks)

  1. A train starting from rest attains a velocity of 72 km/h in 5 minutes. Find its acceleration and distance travelled. Most Imp
  2. A car accelerates uniformly from 18 km/h to 36 km/h in 5 s. Calculate the acceleration and distance covered.
  3. A body travels along a circular path of radius 70 m. After half a revolution in 20 s, find average velocity and average speed.

Long Answer (5 Marks)

  1. Derive the three equations of motion graphically from a velocity-time graph. Most Imp
  2. Draw distance-time graphs for: (a) stationary body, (b) uniform motion, (c) non-uniform motion. Explain each.

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • Distance is scalar; displacement is vector
  • Speed = distance/time; Velocity = displacement/time
  • Acceleration = rate of change of velocity
  • Three equations of motion (only for uniform acceleration): v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as
  • Slope of v-t graph = acceleration; Area under v-t graph = displacement
  • Uniform circular motion has constant speed but changing velocity (accelerated motion)
  • Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying with 5/18
