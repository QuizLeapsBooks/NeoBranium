Natural Vegetation and Wildlife High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains India's rich biodiversity—the different types of forests, grasslands, and wildlife found across the country. Questions on forest types, their rainfall requirements, and wildlife conservation are frequently asked in periodic assessments [citation:1][citation:2].

Introduction

Natural vegetation refers to a plant community that has grown naturally without human aid and has been left undisturbed by humans for a long time. This is also termed virgin vegetation [citation:13][citation:3].

India is one of the 12 mega bio-diversity countries of the world, with about 47,000 plant species and approximately 90,000 animal species [citation:13].

Flora and Fauna

  • Flora: The plant species of a particular region [citation:4].
  • Fauna: The animal species of a particular region [citation:4].

1. Factors Affecting Natural Vegetation

The growth and type of vegetation depend on several natural factors [citation:1][citation:3]:

FactorHow It Affects Vegetation
Relief (Land)Fertile plains are devoted to agriculture; rough terrains support grasslands and woodlands. Mountainous areas show vegetation changes with altitude [citation:2][citation:3].
SoilSandy soils support cactus and thorny bushes; marshy/deltaic soils support mangroves; hill slopes support conical trees [citation:3].
TemperatureDetermines vegetation type—tropical, subtropical, temperate, alpine. Decrease in temperature with altitude changes vegetation [citation:3].
Photoperiod (Sunlight)Longer sunlight in summer makes trees grow faster [citation:3].
Precipitation (Rainfall)Heavy rainfall areas have dense forests; low rainfall areas have thorny vegetation [citation:2].
🎯 Key Point: Almost all rainfall in India comes from the southwest monsoon (June-September) and retreating monsoon. Areas with more than 200 cm rainfall have evergreen forests, while areas with less than 70 cm have thorn forests [citation:2].

2. Types of Natural Vegetation in India

India has five major types of natural vegetation [citation:1][citation:3]:

Tropical Evergreen Forests

  • Rainfall: More than 200 cm [citation:1][citation:2].
  • Location: Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, upper Assam, Tamil Nadu coast [citation:3][citation:5].
  • Features: Dense, multi-layered forests; trees reach up to 60 metres; no definite time for shedding leaves—appear green all year [citation:3][citation:5].
  • Important trees: Ebony, Mahogany, Rosewood, Rubber, Cinchona [citation:1][citation:3].
  • Animals: Elephants, monkeys, lemurs, deer; one-horned rhinoceros in Assam and West Bengal [citation:3].

Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests)

  • Rainfall: Between 200 cm and 70 cm [citation:3][citation:5].
  • Location: Most widespread forests; northeastern states, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh [citation:1][citation:5].
  • Features: Trees shed leaves for about 6-8 weeks in dry summer [citation:3].
  • Sub-types:
    • Moist Deciduous (100-200 cm): Teak is dominant; also bamboo, sal, shisham, sandalwood [citation:5].
    • Dry Deciduous (70-100 cm): Teak, sal, peepal, neem [citation:5].
  • Animals: Tigers, elephants, deer, lions [citation:1].

Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs

  • Rainfall: Less than 70 cm [citation:1][citation:5].
  • Location: Semi-arid areas of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh [citation:1].
  • Features: Trees are scattered; long roots penetrate deep for moisture; stems are succulent; leaves are thick and small to minimize evaporation [citation:5].
  • Important plants: Acacias, palms, euphorbias, cacti [citation:5].
  • Animals: Camels, rats, mice, rabbits, foxes, wolves, wild ass, horses [citation:5].

Montane Forests (Mountain Forests)

Vegetation changes with altitude due to decrease in temperature [citation:2][citation:4]:

AltitudeVegetation TypeCommon Trees/Features
1000-2000 mWet temperate forestsOaks, Chestnuts (evergreen broad-leaf) [citation:4][citation:5]
1500-3000 mTemperate coniferous forestsPine, Deodar, Silver fir, Spruce, Cedar [citation:4][citation:5]
Above 3600 mAlpine vegetationSilver fir, Junipers, Pines, Birches (stunted near snowline) [citation:4][citation:5]
Higher altitudesTundra vegetationMosses and Lichens [citation:5]

Animals: Kashmir stag, spotted deer, wild sheep, jack rabbit, Tibetan antelope, yak, snow leopard, red panda [citation:4][citation:5].

Mangrove Forests (Tidal Forests)

  • Location: Coastal areas influenced by tides—deltas of Ganga, Mahanadi, Krishna, Godavari, Kaveri [citation:2].
  • Features: Roots submerged under water; mud and silt accumulate [citation:2].
  • Important trees: Sundari (provides durable hard timber), palm, coconut, keora, agar [citation:2].
  • Animals: Royal Bengal Tiger, turtles, crocodiles, gharials, snakes [citation:2].

📝 Quick Comparison: Evergreen vs Deciduous

Evergreen: >200 cm rainfall, no definite leaf-shedding, dense multi-layered.
Deciduous: 70-200 cm rainfall, shed leaves for 6-8 weeks, most widespread [citation:4].

3. Wildlife in India

India has one of the richest wildlife populations in the world [citation:1]:

  • Over 90,000 animal species [citation:1][citation:13].
  • Around 2,000 bird species [citation:1].
  • About 2,546 fish species [citation:13].
  • India shares 5-8% of the world's amphibians, reptiles, and mammals [citation:13].

Notable Wildlife

  • Elephants: Forests of Assam, Karnataka, Kerala [citation:12].
  • One-horned Rhinoceros: Swampy lands of West Bengal and Assam [citation:12].
  • Wild Ass: Rann of Kutch [citation:12].
  • Camels: Thar Desert [citation:12].
  • Lions: Gir Forest, Gujarat [citation:12].
  • Tigers: Madhya Pradesh, Sundarbans (West Bengal), Himalayas [citation:12].
  • Snow Leopard, Yak, Tibetan Antelope: High altitudes of Ladakh [citation:12].

4. Conservation of Forests and Wildlife

Conservation means protecting plants and animals from destruction so that nature remains balanced [citation:1].

Why Conservation is Important

  • Forests provide oxygen, cause rainfall, and prevent soil erosion [citation:1].
  • They supply food, wood, and medicines [citation:1].
  • Wildlife maintains ecological balance [citation:1].
  • Extinction of wildlife can disturb the entire ecosystem [citation:1].

Threats to Wildlife

  • Deforestation: Large-scale cutting of trees for agriculture and urbanization destroys habitats [citation:1].
  • Hunting and Poaching: Illegal hunting for body parts [citation:1].

Conservation Measures

  • Biosphere Reserves: 18 biosphere reserves set up in India; 10 included in the world network (Sundarban, Nanda Devi, Nilgiri, Great Nicobar, Gulf of Mannar, Manas, Norkek, Simlipal, Panchmari, Achanakmar-Amarkantak) [citation:12].
  • National Parks and Sanctuaries: 103 national parks and 535 wildlife sanctuaries [citation:12].
  • Projects: Project Tiger, Project Great Indian Bustard, Project Rhino [citation:12].
🎯 Key Point: A biosphere reserve is a protected area for conserving natural vegetation, wildlife, and the environment. Examples: Sundarbans, Nilgiri, Nanda Devi [citation:4].

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • Natural vegetation: Plants growing naturally without human aid (virgin vegetation).
  • Five types: Tropical Evergreen, Tropical Deciduous, Thorn Forests, Montane, Mangrove.
  • Evergreen: >200 cm rainfall; ebony, mahogany, rosewood; no leaf-shedding.
  • Deciduous: 70-200 cm rainfall; most widespread; teak, sal; shed leaves 6-8 weeks.
  • Thorn: <70 cm rainfall; acacia, cactus; long roots, thick leaves.
  • Montane: Altitude-based succession—oak (1000-2000m), pine (1500-3000m), alpine (>3600m).
  • Mangrove: Tidal coasts; Sundari trees; Royal Bengal Tiger.
  • Biosphere Reserves: 18 in India; 10 in world network.
