Our Environment High Weightage Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering ecosystem (biotic and abiotic components), food chains and webs, trophic levels, 10% energy law, biological magnification, ozone layer depletion (causes, effects, and solutions), and waste management (biodegradable vs non-biodegradable). Includes most repeated board exam questions and quick revision table.

Why this chapter is important? Environment-related questions are compulsory in board exams. Every year, 2-3 marks come from the 10% energy law, ozone depletion, and biomagnification. Case-based questions on food chains and waste management are very common.

1. Ecosystem Most Repeated

Ecosystem: A self-sustaining unit where living organisms interact with each other and their physical environment.

🌿 Components of Ecosystem

Component Description Examples
Biotic (Living) Living organisms Plants, animals, decomposers (bacteria, fungi)
Abiotic (Non-living) Physical & chemical factors Sunlight, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals

📌 Types of Organisms Based on Nutrition

  • Producers (Autotrophs): Make their own food via photosynthesis. Eg: Green plants, algae, phytoplankton.
  • Consumers (Heterotrophs): Depend on others for food.
    • Primary consumers (Herbivores): Eat plants – rabbit, deer, grasshopper.
    • Secondary consumers (Carnivores): Eat herbivores – snake, frog, eagle.
    • Tertiary consumers (Top carnivores): Eat other carnivores – lion, hawk.
    • Omnivores: Eat both plants and animals – human, bear, crow.
  • Decomposers (Saprotrophs): Break down dead organic matter. Eg: Bacteria, fungi. Essential for nutrient recycling!

2. Food Chains & Food Webs

🌱 Grass → 🦗 Grasshopper → 🐸 Frog → 🐍 Snake → 🦅 Eagle

Food chain: A linear sequence of organisms where each is eaten by the next. Energy flows from producers → consumers.

Food web: Interconnected network of multiple food chains. More realistic representation of nature.

📌 Key Points:
• Producers always at the first trophic level.
• Decomposers act at all levels, breaking down dead matter.
• Shorter food chains = more energy available to top consumers.

3. Trophic Levels & 10% Energy Law Board Favourite

Trophic level: Each step in a food chain where energy transfer occurs.

  • T₁: Producers (Plants)
  • T₂: Primary consumers (Herbivores)
  • T₃: Secondary consumers (Small carnivores)
  • T₄: Tertiary consumers (Top carnivores)
10% Law (Lindeman's Law): Only 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next. Remaining 90% is used for metabolic processes (respiration, growth, reproduction) or lost as heat.
📌 Example: If plants have 1000 J of energy → herbivores get only 100 J → primary carnivores get 10 J → top carnivores get 1 J.
Why does the number of trophic levels limited to 4-5? Energy becomes insufficient to support another level after 4-5 transfers.

4. Biological Magnification Most Repeated

Definition: The increase in concentration of harmful non-biodegradable substances (pesticides, heavy metals) at each successive trophic level.

⚠️ How it happens:

Pesticides like DDT are not degradable. These get absorbed by producers (algae/plants) → accumulated in their tissues → when herbivores eat many plants, concentration increases → carnivores eat many herbivores → highest concentration in top predators (eagles, humans).

⭐ Real example: DDT in water → zooplankton → small fish → large fish → osprey (egg shell thinning led to population decline).
Common Mistake: Students often confuse biomagnification with bioaccumulation. Bioaccumulation is buildup within an organism; biomagnification is increase across the food chain.

5. Ozone Layer Depletion HOT

Ozone layer (O₃): A protective layer in the stratosphere that absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation from the sun.

🌍 Causes of Ozone Depletion

  • Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs): Used in refrigerators, air conditioners, aerosol sprays, fire extinguishers.
  • CFCs rise to stratosphere → release chlorine atoms (Cl) → chlorine reacts with ozone (O₃) → breaks it into O₂ + O → each Cl atom destroys up to 100,000 ozone molecules.

💀 Effects of UV Radiation

  • Skin cancer, cataracts in humans
  • Reduced photosynthesis in plants
  • Damage to marine phytoplankton (base of aquatic food webs)
  • Weakening of immune system
📌 Measures taken: Montreal Protocol (1987) – international treaty to phase out CFCs. Success: Ozone hole over Antarctica is gradually healing.

6. Waste Management: Biodegradable vs Non-biodegradable

Type Definition Examples Effect
Biodegradable Broken down by microbes (bacteria/fungi) Fruit peels, paper, wood, cotton, food waste Recycled naturally, less harmful
Non-biodegradable Cannot be broken down naturally Plastic, glass, metals, DDT, styrofoam Accumulate, cause pollution, biomagnification

♻️ Waste Management Strategies

  • Reduce: Minimize waste generation
  • Reuse: Use items multiple times (cloth bags, glass bottles)
  • Recycle: Convert waste into new products (paper, plastic, metals)
  • Composting: Convert biodegradable waste into manure
  • Landfills: Disposal for non-biodegradable waste (but not sustainable)
⭐ Garbage segregation (dry/wet waste) at home is the first step towards effective waste management.

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. What is an ecosystem? List its two main components with examples.
    Answer: Ecosystem = interaction of living (biotic: plants, animals) and non-living (abiotic: light, water, soil).
  2. State the 10% law of energy transfer. Why is only 10% energy transferred to the next trophic level?
    Answer: Only 10% energy passes; 90% used in metabolism, growth, reproduction, or lost as heat. Limits number of trophic levels.
  3. What is biological magnification? Give an example.
    Answer: Increase in toxic substance concentration across trophic levels. Eg: DDT in water → algae → fish → eagle (high concentration).
  4. Why is the ozone layer getting depleted? Name the chemicals responsible.
    Answer: CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) from refrigerators, ACs, aerosols release chlorine that destroys ozone.
  5. Differentiate between biodegradable and non-biodegradable substances. Give two examples of each.
    Answer: Biodegradable: broken by microbes (paper, food waste). Non-biodegradable: not broken (plastic, glass).
  6. Construct a terrestrial food chain with four trophic levels. Label producers and consumers.
    Answer: Grass (producer) → Grasshopper (primary) → Frog (secondary) → Snake (tertiary).
  7. Explain the role of decomposers in an ecosystem.
    Answer: Decomposers break down dead organisms, recycle nutrients back to soil, and clean the environment.

• Always mention "10% energy transfer" in energy flow questions.
• For ozone: "CFCs release chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy O₃."
• Draw a simple food chain (4 levels) for 2-3 marks.
• Remember: Decomposers complete the nutrient cycle.
