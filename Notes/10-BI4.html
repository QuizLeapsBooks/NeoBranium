Heredity High Weightage Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering Mendel's experiments, laws of inheritance, monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, sex determination in humans, acquired vs inherited traits, and most repeated board exam questions with Punnett square explanations.

Why this chapter is important? Every year 4-5 questions appear — monohybrid cross ratio (3:1 and 1:2:1), sex determination (XX-XY), difference between acquired and inherited traits, and Mendel's laws.

1. Genetics – Basic Terminology

🧬 Heredity: Transmission of genetic characters from parents to offspring.
🧬 Variation: Differences between individuals of the same species.
🧬 Gene: Basic unit of inheritance (segment of DNA that codes for a protein/trait).
🧬 Allele: Alternative form of a gene (dominant or recessive).
🧬 Genotype: Genetic makeup of an organism (e.g., TT, Tt, tt).
🧬 Phenotype: Observable physical characteristic (e.g., tall, dwarf).
🧬 Homozygous: Both alleles are same (TT or tt).
🧬 Heterozygous: Alleles are different (Tt).
⭐ Gregor Mendel is known as the "Father of Genetics".

2. Mendel's Experiments on Pea Plants

Mendel chose garden pea (Pisum sativum) for his experiments because:

  • Many contrasting traits (tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled seeds, etc.)
  • Short generation time
  • Self-pollination possible, cross-pollination easy

🌱 Traits Studied by Mendel:

Character Dominant Trait Recessive Trait
Plant height Tall (T) Dwarf (t)
Seed shape Round (R) Wrinkled (r)
Seed color Yellow (Y) Green (y)
Flower color Violet (V) White (v)
Pod shape Inflated (I) Constricted (i)

3. Monohybrid Cross Board Favourite

A cross between two parents differing in a single trait (e.g., tall vs dwarf).

📊 Cross: Pure Tall (TT) × Pure Dwarf (tt)

F1 Generation: All Tt (Tall)
Self-pollination of F1: Tt × Tt

📈 Punnett Square for F2 Generation:

Gametes T t
T TT (Tall) Tt (Tall)
t Tt (Tall) tt (Dwarf)
📌 F2 Generation Ratios:
• Phenotypic Ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
• Genotypic Ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

🧬 Dihybrid Cross (Two traits – Round Yellow × Wrinkled Green)

F2 Phenotypic Ratio: 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green

⭐ Dihybrid cross proves that traits are inherited independently (Law of Independent Assortment).

4. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

🔬 Law of Dominance:
When two opposite traits are present together, only one (dominant) is expressed in F1 generation. The other (recessive) remains hidden.
🔬 Law of Segregation:
During gamete formation, the two alleles for a trait separate (segregate) so that each gamete receives only one allele.
🔬 Law of Independent Assortment:
During gamete formation, alleles for different traits assort independently of each other (inheritance of one trait does not affect another).

5. Sex Determination in Humans Most Repeated

In humans, sex is determined by the combination of sex chromosomes (XX for female, XY for male).

🧬 Chromosome Types:
• Female: 44 autosomes + XX
• Male: 44 autosomes + XY
• All eggs carry X chromosome
• Sperm carry either X or Y chromosome

📊 Sex Determination Cross:

X (Egg) X (Egg)
X (Sperm) XX (Female) XX (Female)
Y (Sperm) XY (Male) XY (Male)
📌 Important Results:
• 50% chance of baby being male, 50% chance female
• Father (male) determines the sex of the child – because mother contributes only X, father contributes X or Y
• Sex determination is a matter of chance
⭐ Sex determination in birds is different: Females are ZW and males are ZZ.

6. Acquired vs Inherited Traits

Acquired Traits Inherited Traits
Developed during lifetime Present from birth
Not passed to offspring Passed from parents to offspring
Not controlled by genes Controlled by genes (DNA)
Example: Learning a language, bodybuilding, pierced ears Example: Eye color, blood group, hair type
Do not cause evolution Cause evolution over generations
📌 Why acquired traits are not inherited?
Acquired traits do not change the DNA of germ cells (sperm/egg). Only changes in germ cell DNA are passed to offspring.

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. How do Mendel's experiments show that traits may be dominant or recessive?
    Answer: When Mendel crossed pure tall and pure dwarf pea plants, all F1 plants were tall. This shows that tallness is dominant over dwarfness, which reappeared in F2 (3:1 ratio).
  2. How is the sex of the child determined in human beings?
    Answer: Females have XX chromosomes, males have XY. The father contributes either X or Y chromosome. If sperm with X fertilizes egg → girl (XX); if sperm with Y fertilizes egg → boy (XY).
  3. What is a monohybrid cross? Give its phenotypic and genotypic ratios.
    Answer: Cross between parents differing in one trait. Phenotypic ratio = 3:1, Genotypic ratio = 1:2:1.
  4. Distinguish between acquired traits and inherited traits with examples.
    Answer: Acquired traits (learned, not genetic) vs Inherited traits (passed through genes). Example: muscular body (acquired) vs eye color (inherited).
  5. What is the Law of Independent Assortment?
    Answer: Genes for different traits assort independently during gamete formation. Proved by dihybrid cross (9:3:3:1 ratio).
  6. A child has blood group O. If father has blood group A and mother has blood group B, work out the possible genotypes of the parents.
    Answer: Father: IAi, Mother: IBi. Child receives i from both → ii (O blood group).
  7. Why did Mendel choose pea plants for his experiments?
    Answer: Easy to grow, short generation time, clear contrasting traits, self and cross pollination possible.

🎯 Quick Exam Tips:
• Pure (homozygous) – TT or tt; Hybrid (heterozygous) – Tt
• F1 always shows dominant trait only
• Recessive trait appears only in homozygous condition (tt)
• Sex determination is a 50% chance – not influenced by previous children
• DNA copying introduces variations that lead to evolution
