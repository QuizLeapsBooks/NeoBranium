Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations High Yield Chapter

A complete, detailed guide covering every important concept, most repeated reactions, and board exam patterns. This chapter forms the foundation of Chemistry in Class 10 and carries high weightage in CBSE, ICSE, and state boards.

Why this chapter is critical? Every year 4-5 questions appear from this chapter — including balancing equations, identifying reaction types, and application-based questions on corrosion/rancidity.

1. Chemical Equations – The Language of Reactions

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using symbols and formulas of reactants and products.

  • Reactants → Left side (substances that react)
  • Products → Right side (new substances formed)
  • Arrow (→) means "yields" or "produces"
📌 Example:
Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium Oxide
Word equation: Mg + O2 → MgO
Balanced equation: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO

Symbols in equations: (g) gas, (l) liquid, (s) solid, (aq) aqueous solution, ↑ gas evolved, ↓ precipitate formed.

2. Balancing Chemical Equations 🔥 Most Repeated in Exams

Balancing is done to obey Law of Conservation of Mass – mass can neither be created nor destroyed.

⚠️ Golden Rule of Balancing

❌ NEVER change the subscript (small numbers) – only change the coefficient (big numbers in front).

Step-by-step method (Hit and Trial)

  1. Write the unbalanced equation with correct formulas.
  2. Start balancing with the compound having maximum atoms.
  3. Balance atoms one by one (except O and H later).
  4. Finally balance oxygen and hydrogen.
  5. Ensure all coefficients are in simplest whole numbers.

📝 Examples of Balanced Equations (Exam Favourites)

  • Formation of water: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
  • Burning of methane: CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
  • Decomposition of ferrous sulphate: 2FeSO4 → Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3
  • Photosynthesis (often asked): 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2

3. Types of Chemical Reactions (with Examples & Identification Tricks)

Board exams ask to identify the type of reaction just by looking at the equation. Learn these 5 types thoroughly.

🔹 1. Combination Reaction (Synthesis)

Two or more reactants combine to form a single product.
General form: A + B → AB

  • Example: CaO (quicklime) + H2O → Ca(OH)2 (slaked lime) [Exothermic]
  • Example: C + O2 → CO2
⭐ Most repeated: Formation of slaked lime from quicklime

🔹 2. Decomposition Reaction

A single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler substances.
General form: AB → A + B

  • Thermal decomposition: CaCO3 (limestone) → CaO + CO2 (Heat)
  • Electrolytic decomposition: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2 (electricity)
  • Photolytic decomposition: 2AgCl (white) → 2Ag (grey) + Cl2 (sunlight) – used in black & white photography

🔹 3. Displacement Reaction

More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from its salt solution.
General form: A + BC → AC + B

  • Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu (Iron nail becomes brownish – copper deposits)
  • Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu

🔹 4. Double Displacement Reaction

Exchange of ions between two reactants to form new compounds (often precipitate or gas).
General form: AB + CD → AD + CB

  • AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO3 (white precipitate – confirmed by yellowing in sunlight)
  • NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O (neutralization – special case)

🔹 5. Redox Reaction (Oxidation and Reduction combined)

Oxidation: Gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen OR loss of electrons.
Reduction: Loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen OR gain of electrons.

🧠 Mnemonic: OIL RIG → Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)
Example: CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O
– CuO loses oxygen → Reduced (Cu)
– H2 gains oxygen → Oxidized (H2O)

4. Redox Reactions – Detailed with Everyday Examples Most Confusing Topic

In many displacement reactions, redox occurs simultaneously. Identify the oxidizing agent (gets reduced) and reducing agent (gets oxidized).

Reaction Substance oxidized Substance reduced
2Mg + O2 → 2MgO Mg (gains oxygen) O2 (gains electrons)
ZnO + C → Zn + CO C (gains oxygen) ZnO (loses oxygen)
2PbO + C → 2Pb + CO2 C → CO2 PbO → Pb
Board Trick: In reaction 2Na + Cl2 → 2NaCl, Na is oxidized (loses e-) and Cl2 is reduced (gains e-). Always check electron transfer in ionic equations.

5. Effects of Oxidation in Daily Life – Corrosion & Rancidity

These application-based questions appear in 3-mark answers every alternate year.

⚙️ Corrosion

Gradual destruction of metals due to reaction with moisture/air. Rusting of iron: 4Fe + 3O2 + xH2O → 2Fe2O3.xH2O

  • Prevention: Galvanization (Zn coating), painting, oiling, alloying (stainless steel).

🥨 Rancidity

Oxidation of fats & oils in food causing unpleasant smell/taste.

  • Prevention methods: Adding antioxidants (BHA/BHT), storing in airtight containers, refrigeration, flushing with nitrogen gas.

Sample PYQ (2023):

Q. List two ways to prevent rancidity. Why do chips packets flush with nitrogen gas?
Ans: (i) Keep food in refrigerator, (ii) Use antioxidants. Nitrogen prevents oxidation by creating inert atmosphere.

6. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

  1. Balance the following equation: Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2 (Answer: 3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2)
  2. Identify the type of reaction: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 (Photodecomposition / Thermal decomposition) – Answer: Photolytic decomposition.
  3. What is a redox reaction? Give an example and identify the oxidizing agent. (Answer: CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O ; Oxidizing agent: CuO)
  4. Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped? (Answer: Displacement reaction – Fe displaces Cu, solution turns pale green due to FeSO4)
  5. Give one example of double displacement reaction where precipitate is formed. (Answer: BaCl2 + Na2SO4 → BaSO4↓ + 2NaCl)

📌 Pro Tips from Toppers

  • ✔️ Always write physical states (s, l, g, aq) – it adds presentation marks.
  • ✔️ For "identify type of reaction" questions, read the equation carefully: if one product is formed → combination; if single reactant splits → decomposition.
  • ✔️ In redox questions, look for gain/loss of oxygen or hydrogen — easiest way to score.
