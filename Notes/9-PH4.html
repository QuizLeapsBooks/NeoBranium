Work and Energy High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Understanding the conservation of energy is crucial. Numerical questions on Kinetic and Potential energy are very common in board exams.

1. Work Done

In physics, work is said to be done when a force acts on an object and the object is displaced in the direction of the force [citation:2].

W = F × s × cos θ

Where:

  • F = Force applied (Newton)
  • s = Displacement (meter)
  • θ = Angle between force and displacement

Special Cases of Work

  • Positive Work: When θ = 0°, force and displacement are in the same direction. Example: Pushing a box forward [citation:1].
  • Negative Work: When θ = 180°, force and displacement are in opposite directions. Example: Friction acting on a moving object [citation:1].
  • Zero Work: When θ = 90° (force perpendicular to displacement) or when there is no displacement. Example: Carrying a bag while walking horizontally [citation:1].
📌 Exam Tip: Work is a scalar quantity — it has magnitude but no direction [citation:12]. The SI unit of work is Joule (J).

Definition of 1 Joule

1 Joule of work is said to be done when a force of 1 Newton displaces an object through a distance of 1 meter in the direction of the force [citation:6].

2. Energy

Energy is defined as the capacity to do work. It is a scalar quantity and its SI unit is Joule (J) [citation:1][citation:9].

Forms of Energy

Energy exists in various forms: Light, Heat, Chemical, Electrical, and Mechanical. In this chapter, we focus on Mechanical Energy, which is the sum of Kinetic and Potential Energy [citation:9][citation:12].

A. Kinetic Energy (K.E.)

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion. Any moving object — a ball, a car, or a running person — has kinetic energy [citation:1].

K.E. = ½ m v²

Where:

  • m = Mass of the object (kg)
  • v = Velocity of the object (m/s)
🔑 Important Relationship: If velocity is doubled, Kinetic Energy becomes 4 times (since v is squared). If velocity is tripled, K.E. becomes 9 times [citation:6].

B. Potential Energy (P.E.)

Potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration (shape) [citation:1][citation:5].

Example: Water stored in a reservoir can rotate a turbine at a lower level. The water possesses energy due to its elevated position [citation:5].

P.E. = m × g × h

Where:

  • m = Mass (kg)
  • g = Acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s²)
  • h = Height above the ground (m)

C. Work-Energy Theorem

The work-energy theorem states that the work done by a force on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy [citation:1].

W = K.E.final − K.E.initial

D. Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system always remains constant [citation:1][citation:5].

Example — A Falling Ball:

  • At the top: Only Potential Energy (P.E. = mgh, K.E. = 0)
  • Midway: P.E. + K.E. (some energy converted)
  • Just before hitting the ground: Maximum K.E., minimum P.E.
  • Total Energy = Constant at every point [citation:1]

3. Power

Power is defined as the rate of doing work or the rate of transfer of energy [citation:6].

P = W / t

Where:

  • W = Work done (Joule)
  • t = Time taken (second)

SI unit of power is Watt (W). 1 Watt = 1 Joule/second [citation:6].

Commercial Unit of Energy

In homes and industries, a larger unit called kilowatt-hour (kWh) is used. It is also known as 1 "Unit" of electricity [citation:3][citation:7].

1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
⚡ Why kWh instead of Joules? Joules are too small for measuring large amounts of household or industrial energy consumption. kWh makes calculations easier [citation:7].

Most Repeated Previous Year Questions

  1. Define 1 Joule of work. (1 Mark) — Answer: When 1 N force displaces an object through 1 m in its direction, 1 J work is done.
  2. What is the kinetic energy of an object of mass m moving with a velocity v? (1 Mark) — Answer: K.E. = ½ m v²
  3. A pair of bullocks exerts a force of 140 N on a plough. The field being ploughed is 15 m long. How much work is done? (2 Marks) — Answer: W = F × s = 140 × 15 = 2100 J
  4. Calculate the electricity bill for a 100W bulb used for 10 hours a day for 30 days if cost is Rs 5 per kWh. (3 Marks) — Answer: Energy = 100W × 10h × 30 = 30000 Wh = 30 kWh. Bill = 30 × 5 = Rs 150.
  5. When a body falls freely towards the earth, what happens to its total energy? (1 Mark) — Answer: Remains constant (Law of Conservation of Energy).
  6. In case of negative work, what is the angle between force and displacement? (1 Mark) — Answer: 180°
  7. A car is accelerated on a levelled road and attains a velocity 4 times its initial velocity. What happens to its potential energy? (1 Mark) — Answer: Does not change (height remains constant).

Quick Revision — At a Glance

🎯 Key Formulas to Memorize:
  • Work: W = F × s
  • Kinetic Energy: K.E. = ½ m v²
  • Potential Energy: P.E. = mgh
  • Work-Energy Theorem: W = ΔK.E.
  • Power: P = W / t
  • Commercial Energy: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
🎯 Key Tips for Exam:
  • Total Mechanical Energy = K.E. + P.E. = Constant (Law of Conservation of Energy).
  • Work can be zero if force and displacement are perpendicular.
  • Work is a scalar quantity — it has no direction.
  • If velocity doubles, Kinetic Energy becomes 4 times.
