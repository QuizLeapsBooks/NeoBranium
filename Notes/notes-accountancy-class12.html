Chapter 1: Accounting for Partnership: Basic Concepts

Meaning of Partnership

Definition
As per Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.

Partnership Deed

It is a written agreement containing the terms and conditions of the partnership. If there is no deed, the provisions of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932 apply: equal profit sharing, no interest on capital/drawings, no salary to partners, and 6% p.a. interest on partner's loan.

Profit and Loss Appropriation Account

An extension of the P&L Account, prepared to distribute profits among partners.

Key Items in P&L Appropriation A/c
Debit: Interest on Capital, Partner's Salary/Commission, Reserve.
Credit: Net Profit (from P&L), Interest on Drawings.

Capital Accounts

  • Fixed Capital Method: Two accounts maintained per partner - Capital A/c (only capital additions/withdrawals) and Current A/c (all other transactions).
  • Fluctuating Capital Method: Only one account (Capital A/c) is maintained for all transactions.

Chapter 2: Reconstitution of a Partnership Firm: Admission of a Partner

Admission of a Partner

When a new partner is admitted, the old partnership is reconstituted. The new partner brings in capital and their share of goodwill (Premium for Goodwill).

New Profit Sharing Ratio and Sacrificing Ratio

Formula
Sacrificing Ratio = Old Ratio - New Ratio

The Premium for Goodwill brought by the new partner is distributed to old partners in their sacrificing ratio.

Revaluation Account

Prepared to record the increase/decrease in the value of assets and liabilities. Profit/Loss on revaluation is transferred to old partners' capital accounts in their old ratio.

  • Debit side: Decrease in assets, Increase in liabilities, Unrecorded liabilities.
  • Credit side: Increase in assets, Decrease in liabilities, Unrecorded assets.

Accumulated Profits/Losses

Distributed to old partners in their old profit sharing ratio before the admission.

Chapter 3: Reconstitution of a Partnership Firm: Retirement/Death of a Partner

Retirement or Death

A partner may retire by giving notice, or the partnership may reconstitute upon a partner's death. The retiring/deceased partner is entitled to their share of capital, revaluation profit, accumulated reserves, and goodwill.

Gaining Ratio

Formula
Gaining Ratio = New Ratio - Old Ratio

The remaining partners compensate the retiring/deceased partner for goodwill in their gaining ratio.

Calculation of Deceased Partner's Share of Profit

If a partner dies during the year, their share of profit till the date of death is calculated based on:

  • Time Basis: Based on previous year's profit or average profit.
  • Turnover/Sales Basis: Based on the sales till the date of death and the profit percentage of the previous year.

Chapter 4: Dissolution of Partnership Firm

Meaning of Dissolution

Dissolution of firm means the business of the firm is closed down, assets are sold, liabilities are paid, and the relation among all partners comes to an end.

Realisation Account

Prepared only once during the lifetime of the firm (at dissolution) to find out profit/loss on realisation of assets and settlement of liabilities.

  • Transfer all assets (except cash/bank) to debit side at book value.
  • Transfer all outside liabilities to credit side at book value.
  • Record sale of assets on credit side (Bank A/c).
  • Record payment of liabilities on debit side (Bank A/c).

Order of Payment of Liabilities

  1. Outside creditors and loans.
  2. Partner's loan.
  3. Partner's capital balances.

Chapter 5: Accounting for Share Capital

Company and Share Capital

A company's capital is divided into small equal units called shares. Types of shares: Equity Shares and Preference Shares.

Categories of Share Capital

  • Authorised Capital: Maximum capital a company can issue (stated in MOA).
  • Issued Capital: Part of authorised capital offered to the public.
  • Subscribed Capital: Part of issued capital applied for by the public.
  • Called-up Capital: Amount demanded by the company to be paid on shares.
  • Paid-up Capital: Amount actually received from shareholders.

Issue of Shares

Shares can be issued at Par (face value) or at a Premium (above face value). The premium amount is credited to Securities Premium Reserve A/c.

Forfeiture of Shares

Cancellation of shares due to non-payment of calls. The amount already paid is transferred to Share Forfeiture A/c.

Reissue of Forfeited Shares

Forfeited shares can be reissued at par, premium, or discount. The discount cannot exceed the forfeited amount. Any remaining balance in Share Forfeiture A/c is transferred to Capital Reserve.

Chapter 6: Issue and Redemption of Debentures

Meaning of Debenture

A debenture is a written instrument acknowledging a debt under the common seal of the company. It carries a fixed rate of interest.

Issue of Debentures

Like shares, debentures can be issued at Par, Premium, or Discount. They can be issued for cash or for consideration other than cash (e.g., buying machinery).

Debentures Issued as Collateral Security

When debentures are given to a bank as a secondary/additional security against a loan.

Redemption of Debentures

Redemption means repayment of the principal amount of debentures to the debenture holders. Can be redeemed at Par or Premium.

Important Note
As per latest CBSE guidelines, detailed methods of redemption (lump sum, draw of lots) might be restricted in scope, focusing mainly on the accounting entries at the time of issue considering the terms of redemption.

Chapter 7: Financial Statements of a Company

Financial Statements

Under Section 129 of the Companies Act 2013, financial statements must be prepared in the prescribed format as per Schedule III.

Major Heads in Balance Sheet (Schedule III, Part I)

  • Equity and Liabilities:
    • Shareholders' Funds (Share Capital, Reserves & Surplus, Money received against share warrants)
    • Share Application money pending allotment
    • Non-Current Liabilities (Long-term borrowings, Deferred tax liabilities, etc.)
    • Current Liabilities (Short-term borrowings, Trade payables, etc.)
  • Assets:
    • Non-Current Assets (Property, Plant and Equipment, Intangible assets, Non-current investments, etc.)
    • Current Assets (Current investments, Inventories, Trade receivables, Cash and cash equivalents, etc.)

Statement of Profit and Loss (Schedule III, Part II)

Shows Revenue from Operations, Other Income, Total Revenue, and Total Expenses (Cost of materials consumed, Purchases of Stock-in-Trade, Changes in inventories, Employee benefits expense, Finance costs, Depreciation, Other expenses), leading to Profit Before Tax and Profit After Tax.

Chapter 8: Analysis of Financial Statements

Meaning and Objectives

Financial Statement Analysis is the systematic process of analyzing the financial information to understand and make decisions regarding operations. Objectives include assessing profitability, liquidity, solvency, and operational efficiency.

Tools of Analysis

  • Comparative Statements: Compare financial data for two or more years side-by-side to find absolute and percentage changes.
  • Common Size Statements: Convert all figures to a percentage of a common base (e.g., Total Assets in Balance Sheet, Revenue from Operations in P&L).
  • Ratio Analysis: Establaling mathematical relationships between items.
  • Cash Flow Statement: Shows inflows and outflows of cash.

Chapter 9: Accounting Ratios

Meaning

An accounting ratio is a mathematical relationship between two interdependent financial variables.

Types of Ratios

  1. Liquidity Ratios: Measure short-term paying capacity.
    • Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities (Ideal 2:1)
    • Quick Ratio / Liquid Ratio = Quick Assets / Current Liabilities (Ideal 1:1)
  2. Solvency Ratios: Measure long-term paying capacity.
    • Debt to Equity Ratio = Debt / Equity
    • Total Assets to Debt Ratio = Total Assets / Debt
    • Proprietary Ratio = Shareholders' Funds / Total Assets
    • Interest Coverage Ratio = Profit before Interest & Tax / Interest on Long Term Debt
  3. Activity / Turnover Ratios: Measure efficiency of resource utilization.
    • Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Revenue from Operations / Average Inventory
    • Trade Receivables Turnover Ratio = Net Credit Sales / Average Trade Receivables
    • Trade Payables Turnover Ratio = Net Credit Purchases / Average Trade Payables
    • Working Capital Turnover Ratio = Revenue from Operations / Working Capital
  4. Profitability Ratios: Measure operational efficiency and profitability.
    • Gross Profit Ratio = (Gross Profit / Revenue from Operations) × 100
    • Operating Ratio = ((Cost of Revenue + Operating Exp) / Revenue) × 100
    • Operating Profit Ratio = (Operating Profit / Revenue) × 100
    • Net Profit Ratio = (Net Profit / Revenue) × 100
    • Return on Investment (ROI) = (Profit before Interest, Tax & Dividend / Capital Employed) × 100

Chapter 10: Cash Flow Statement

Meaning

A Cash Flow Statement shows the inflows (receipts) and outflows (payments) of Cash and Cash Equivalents during a period, prepared as per Accounting Standard (AS) 3 (Revised).

Cash Equivalents

Short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and which are subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value (e.g., current investments, short-term marketable securities).

Classification of Activities

  1. Operating Activities: Principal revenue-producing activities. Cash generated from operations after adjusting non-cash items (depreciation) and non-operating items (interest/dividend received/paid), and changes in working capital.
  2. Investing Activities: Acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments. Includes purchase/sale of fixed assets, interest/dividend received.
  3. Financing Activities: Activities that result in changes in the size and composition of owners' capital and borrowings. Includes issue/redemption of shares/debentures, dividend paid, interest paid.
Net Increase/Decrease
Net Cash Flow from Operating + Investing + Financing Activities + Opening Cash & Cash Equivalents = Closing Cash & Cash Equivalents
