Globalisation and the Indian Economy Core Board Chapter

Chapter Overview: This chapter explores how goods, services, investments, and technology move across national borders. CBSE repeatedly tests: Ways MNCs control production in other countries, Impact of trade barriers and liberalization (New Economic Policy 1991), Role of WTO, and How globalisation has had both positive and negative consequences for different social groups.

1. Production Across Countries & Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

A Multinational Corporation (MNC) is a company that owns or controls production in more than one nation. MNCs set up offices and factories for production in regions where they can obtain cheap labor, abundant resources, and favorable government policies to minimize costs and maximize profit.

Ways MNCs Set Up or Control Production Across Countries:
  1. Joint Ventures: Setting up production jointly with local companies in another nation (e.g., Ford Motors entered India in partnership with Mahindra & Mahindra).
  2. Buying Up Local Companies: The most common route. Large MNCs purchase existing local companies to rapidly expand production (e.g., Cargill Foods, an American MNC, bought Parakh Foods in India).
  3. Placing Orders with Small Producers: Garments, footwear, sports equipment are produced by thousands of small producers worldwide and supplied to MNCs who sell under their brand names.

2. Foreign Trade and Integration of Markets

Foreign trade creates an opportunity for producers to reach beyond domestic markets:

  • For Producers: Can compete not only in domestic markets but in markets around the globe.
  • For Buyers: Import of goods expands choices beyond domestically produced goods and keeps prices competitive (e.g., Chinese toys in the Indian market).
  • Result: Connecting different markets across countries is called market integration.

3. What is Globalisation?

Globalisation is the process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries. It involves greater movement of goods, services, investments, technology, and people across national boundaries.

MNCs are playing a major role in driving the globalisation process by investing in diverse countries and facilitating cross-border trade.

4. Factors That Have Enabled Globalisation

  1. Rapid Improvement in Transportation Technology: Containers for goods transport on ships, railways, and planes have drastically reduced delivery time and shipping costs.
  2. Information and Communication Technology (ICT): Telecommunications, computers, internet, and satellite links allow instant sharing of information, video conferencing, and remote services (e.g., BPO centers and IT support).

5. Liberalisation of Foreign Trade and Investment Policy

A trade barrier is a restriction placed by governments on foreign trade (e.g., import tax/tariff). Governments use trade barriers to protect domestic producers from foreign competition.

  • Around 1991, India initiated the New Economic Policy:
    • Barriers on foreign trade and foreign investment were largely removed.
    • Liberalisation: Removing barriers or restrictions set by the government is known as liberalisation. Goods could be imported and exported easily, and foreign companies could set up factories.

6. World Trade Organisation (WTO)

The World Trade Organisation (WTO) is an international organization whose aim is to liberalise international trade. Founded in 1995, it establishes rules regarding trade between nations.

Criticism of WTO: Although WTO is supposed to allow free trade for all, in practice, developed countries have unfairly retained trade barriers (such as heavy agricultural subsidies in the US and Europe) while forcing developing nations like India to remove theirs.

7. Impact of Globalisation in India

Beneficiaries (Positive Impact) Struggles / Negative Impact
  • Urban Consumers: Greater choice, improved quality, and lower prices on automobiles, electronics, fast food.
  • Indian Top Companies: Emerged as multinationals themselves (e.g., Tata Motors, Infosys, Ranbaxy, Asian Paints).
  • Service Sector (IT/BPO): Boom in jobs for software developers, accountants, call-center employees.
  • Small Scale Producers: Batteries, toys, plastic, tires, dairy faced severe competition and many small units shut down.
  • Uncertain Employment: Workers are increasingly employed on a temporary/contractual basis with long hours and low wages to cut production costs.

8. The Struggle for a Fair Globalisation

Fair globalisation would create opportunities for all, and ensure that the benefits of globalisation are shared more equally.

Government's Role:

  • Ensure labor laws are properly implemented and workers get their rights.
  • Support small producers to improve their performance until they can compete.
  • Negotiate at the WTO for fairer rules alongside other developing countries.

9. Most Repeated CBSE Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

  1. (CBSE 2024, 3M) "Information and communication technology has played a major role in spreading out products and services across countries." Support the statement with examples.
  2. (CBSE 2023, 5M) Analyze any five positive impacts of globalisation on the Indian economy.
  3. (CBSE 2022, 3M) What is liberalisation? Describe any two ways through which the Indian government liberalised foreign trade in 1991.
  4. (CBSE 2020, 5M) "Globalisation has not been uniform across all sections of society in India." Explain with suitable arguments.
