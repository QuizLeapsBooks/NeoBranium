People as Resource High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? This chapter shifts the perspective of population from a liability to an asset. It introduces Human Capital, explains the three sectors of the economy, and discusses unemployment. Concepts like disguised unemployment and the role of education/health are frequently asked in exams [citation:1][citation:13].

What is 'People as Resource'?

'People as Resource' is a way of referring to a country's working people in terms of their existing productive skills and abilities. Instead of viewing a large population as a burden, this chapter explains how investment in people (through education and health) turns them into an asset called Human Capital [citation:1][citation:9].

🎯 Key Point: Human beings are the only active resource. Land and physical capital cannot become useful on their own; they need human effort to produce goods and services [citation:4].

1. Human Capital Formation

When the existing human resource is further developed by becoming more educated and healthy, it is called Human Capital Formation. This investment in people yields a return just like investment in physical capital (like machines or land) [citation:1][citation:13].

Why is Human Capital Superior?

  • Human capital can make use of land and physical capital.
  • Land and capital cannot become useful on their own without human intervention.
  • Investment in education and health gives high returns in the form of higher productivity and income [citation:2][citation:11].

📝 Example: Japan

Japan is a developed country despite having very limited natural resources. It invested heavily in education, health, and skill training. This created a highly productive workforce (human capital), proving that people are the real strength of an economy [citation:1].

2. Role of Education and Health

Education

Education is one of the most important inputs for human capital formation. It improves skills, efficiency, and the ability to earn better [citation:4].

  • Government Initiatives: Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (elementary education for children aged 6-14) and Mid-Day Meal Scheme (to encourage attendance and improve nutrition) [citation:1][citation:8].

Health

Only a healthy person can work to their full potential. Poor health reduces productivity and makes a person a liability for the economy [citation:4][citation:5].

  • Good health increases productivity and earning capacity.
  • An unhealthy person cannot work efficiently and may lose working days due to illness [citation:4].

3. Economic Activities and Sectors

Economic activities are those performed to earn a living. They are classified into three main sectors [citation:1][citation:7]:

SectorDescriptionExamples
Primary SectorActivities directly related to natural resources.Farming, fishing, mining, forestry [citation:1].
Secondary SectorManufacturing and processing raw materials into finished goods.Making clothes from cotton, factory work, construction [citation:1][citation:7].
Tertiary SectorProvides services that support other sectors.Teachers, doctors, transport, banking [citation:1].
⚠️ Important: The Primary Sector (especially agriculture) is the most labour-absorbing sector in India [citation:8].

4. Market vs Non-Market Activities

Market ActivitiesNon-Market Activities
Performed for pay or profit (remuneration). Performed for self-consumption.
Includes production of goods and services, government services. Includes processing of primary products for own use, household chores.
Contributes to national income. Does not contribute to national income directly [citation:7][citation:13].

5. Unemployment

Unemployment exists when people who are willing to work at the prevailing wage rate cannot find jobs .

Types of Unemployment

TypeDescriptionWhere it is found
Seasonal UnemploymentPeople are unemployed during certain months of the year.Agriculture sector [citation:10][citation:14].
Disguised UnemploymentMore people are employed than actually needed. Their removal does not affect productivity.Family-based agriculture [citation:3][citation:10].
Educated UnemploymentEducated individuals with degrees cannot find suitable jobs.Urban areas [citation:10][citation:13].

📝 Example: Disguised Unemployment

If a farm needs only 5 workers but 8 family members are working on it, the extra 3 are disguisedly unemployed. Even if they stop working, the total output will not fall [citation:14].

6. Women in the Workforce

Women are often employed in low-paid work due to several reasons [citation:4][citation:11]:

  • Low Education and Skill: A majority of women have meagre education and low skill formation, leading to lower wages compared to men.
  • Lack of Job Security: Most women work in the unorganised sector where job security is low and benefits like maternity leave are missing.
  • Unpaid Domestic Work: Women's household work is not recognized as an economic activity and is not paid for.

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • People become a resource when invested in education, health, and training (Human Capital).
  • Human capital is superior because it utilizes land and physical capital.
  • Three sectors: Primary (natural resources), Secondary (manufacturing), Tertiary (services).
  • Market activities = for pay/profit; Non-market = for self-consumption.
  • Disguised unemployment = more workers than needed (common in agriculture).
  • Seasonal unemployment = joblessness during off-seasons.
  • Women are low-paid due to low skills and lack of job security.
