Political Parties Board Exam Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Political parties are the backbone of democracy. This chapter explains what political parties are, why they are necessary, how they function, and the challenges they face. Board exams frequently test the functions of parties, national vs state parties, and the reforms needed.

1.1 What is a Political Party?

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on some policies and programmes for society with a view to promote the collective good [citation:20].

1.2 Components of a Political Party

A political party has three components [citation:2]:

ComponentRole
LeadersGuide the party and make major decisions
Active MembersParticipate in party activities and campaigns
FollowersGeneral supporters who vote for the party

1.3 Why Do We Need Political Parties?

Political parties are necessary because [citation:2]:

  • They help in the successful implementation of democratic ideals
  • They bring together people with similar ideas and policies
  • They help voters choose between different programmes
  • They help form stable governments
  • They ensure that elected representatives work together
  • They make the government more accountable to the people
🎯 Key Point: Political parties are easily one of the most visible institutions in a democracy. For most ordinary citizens, political parties are equal to democracy [citation:3].

2. Functions of Political Parties

Political parties perform several crucial functions in a democracy [citation:20]:

FunctionDescription
Contest ElectionsParties select and support candidates to stand for public office and compete in elections. In India, top party leaders choose candidates for contesting elections [citation:20].
Put Forward PoliciesParties present policy proposals and programmes so that voters can choose between alternative plans. A party reduces a vast multitude of opinions into a few basic positions [citation:20].
Make LawsParties play a decisive role in making laws. Members follow the direction of party leadership in the legislature [citation:20].
Form and Run GovernmentThe winning party forms and runs the government. Parties recruit leaders, train them, and make them ministers [citation:20].
Play OppositionParties that lose elections form the opposition, voicing different views and criticising the government for its failures [citation:20].
Shape Public OpinionParties raise and highlight issues through lakhs of members and activists. They sometimes launch movements for resolving problems [citation:20].
Provide Access to GovernmentParties serve as a vital link between citizens and government. Ordinary citizens can approach local party leaders easily [citation:20].
🧠 Mnemonic: CLOP-PSO
  • C — Contest elections
  • L — Laws (make laws)
  • O — Opposition (play opposition)
  • P — Policies (put forward)
  • P — Public opinion (shape)
  • S — Serve as link (government access)
  • O — Organize government (form and run)

3. Necessity of Political Parties

Political parties are necessary for a democracy because [citation:2]:

  • Coordination and Promise: They enable unified policy platforms and commitments
  • Government Formation and Accountability: They facilitate stable government formation
  • Representation and Organization: They aggregate group interests into organized political formations
  • Handling Complex Societies: They gather diverse views for representative government
  • Support and Restraint: They support or restrain the government and ensure varied viewpoints are considered

If political parties were absent, every person would be independent, and no proper policy decision could be made. The elected person would be held responsible for the local area, but no proper government would be present for the whole country [citation:2].

💡 Key Point: A democracy cannot function effectively without political parties. They are a necessary condition for a functioning democracy [citation:7].

4. How Many Parties Should We Have?

There are three types of party systems in the world [citation:2]:

Party SystemDescriptionExamples
One-Party SystemOnly one party is allowed to control and run the government. This is not a democratic option since there is no fair chance for competition [citation:2].China, North Korea, Cuba
Two-Party SystemPower alternates between two major parties. Other parties can be present but are not provided majority share in legislation [citation:2].United States, United Kingdom
Multi-Party SystemMore than two parties have a reasonable chance of coming to power either on their own or in alliance with others [citation:2].India

4.1 Why Does India Follow a Multi-Party System?

  • India has a diverse population with multiple social, cultural, and regional interests [citation:4]
  • A multi-party system allows representation of different communities [citation:4]
  • It makes governance more inclusive [citation:4]

4.2 Coalition Government

When no single party wins a majority, multiple parties join together to form a government. This is called a coalition government [citation:4].

🎯 Key Point: In India, there were three major alliances in the 2004 parliamentary elections — the National Democratic Alliance, the United Progressive Alliance, and the Left Front [citation:2].

5. National and State Parties

5.1 Registration with Election Commission

Every party in the country has to register with the Election Commission. While the Commission treats all parties equally, it offers some special facilities to large and established parties [citation:2].

5.2 Recognition Criteria

TypeCriteria
State PartyA party that secures at least six per cent of the total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and wins at least two seats is recognised as a State party [citation:2].
National PartyA party that secures at least six per cent of total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four States and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha is recognised as a national party [citation:3].

5.3 Recognised National Parties

India now has seven recognised national parties [citation:3]:

PartyKey Facts
Indian National Congress (INC)Founded in 1885; dominated India's freedom movement
Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)Formed in 1980; emphasises cultural nationalism (Hindutva) [citation:3]
Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)Formed in 1984 under Kanshi Ram; represents Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs [citation:3]
Communist Party of India (CPI)Formed in 1925; believes in Marxist-Leninist ideas [citation:9]
Communist Party of India (Marxist) - CPI(M)Founded in 1964; supports socialism, secularism, democratic rights [citation:9]
Nationalist Congress Party (NCP)Formed in 1999; supports democracy, Gandhian secularism [citation:9]
Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)Established 26 November 2012 [citation:11]

5.4 State Parties

  • State parties are also referred to as regional parties [citation:2]
  • Some state parties have national level political organisations with units in several states (e.g., Samajwadi Party, Rashtriya Janata Dal) [citation:2]
  • Since 1996, nearly every one of the State parties has got an opportunity to be a part of one or the other national level coalition government [citation:2]
  • This has contributed to the strengthening of federalism and democracy [citation:3]

6. Challenges to Political Parties

Political parties face several major challenges [citation:6]:

6.1 Lack of Internal Democracy

  • Power concentrated in the hands of a few top leaders [citation:6]
  • Parties do not keep membership registers [citation:3]
  • They do not hold organizational meetings regularly [citation:3]
  • They do not conduct internal elections regularly [citation:3]
  • Ordinary members do not have sufficient information about party affairs [citation:3]
  • Leaders assume greater power to make decisions in the name of the party [citation:3]

6.2 Dynastic Succession

  • Leadership positions are often inherited by family members [citation:6]
  • Top leaders prefer to have their family members as successors [citation:3]
  • Members who work at the grassroots are neglected [citation:3]
  • Nepotism arises [citation:3]

6.3 Money and Muscle Power

  • Election campaigns are expensive [citation:9]
  • Candidates and parties sometimes rely on unaccounted money [citation:9]
  • Parties select candidates who can collect funds [citation:6]
  • Parties may endorse criminal candidates if they can win elections [citation:6]
  • Policies may be moulded by groups supplying money and muscle power [citation:18]

6.4 Limited Meaningful Choice

  • Ideological differences between parties have narrowed [citation:6]
  • Parties sometimes copy each other's policies [citation:13]
  • Leaders shift between parties, confusing voters [citation:6]
  • Voters do not have a meaningful alternative [citation:6]
⚠️ Impact on Democracy: These challenges weaken parties and reduce their ability to perform democratic functions effectively. They can lead to voter apathy and distrust in the political system [citation:13].

7. How Can Parties Be Reformed?

Several reforms have been suggested to strengthen political parties [citation:6]:

ReformDescription
Law for Internal AffairsA law should be enacted to regulate political parties' internal affairs, making them more transparent [citation:6].
Women's RepresentationParties should be required to give around one-third of tickets to female candidates [citation:6].
State Funding of ElectionsThe government should provide funds to political parties to help with election expenses, reducing money power [citation:6].
Discouraging Criminal BackgroundsStricter eligibility criteria to prevent individuals with criminal records from holding party positions [citation:4].
Transparency in FundingAdopting transparent procedures for donations to reduce corruption [citation:4].
Empowering Election CommissionGiving the Election Commission more authority to regulate parties [citation:4].

7.1 Other Ways to Reform

If parties resist change, two other ways to bring reform are [citation:13]:

  • Citizens can press for reform through petitions, public campaigns, and peaceful agitations
  • People can join parties in large numbers and work from within to change them
💡 Key Point: It is often easier to reform a party by active participation than only by criticising it from outside [citation:13].

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Political Party: Group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power [citation:20]
  • Ruling Party: Political party that runs the government [citation:11]
  • Defection: Changing party allegiance from the party on which a person got elected [citation:11]
  • Partisan: A person strongly committed to a party [citation:20]
  • Coalition Government: Multiple parties join together to form a government [citation:4]
🎯 Key Comparisons:
  • National Party: 6% votes in 4 states + 4 Lok Sabha seats [citation:3]
  • State Party: 6% votes in State Assembly + 2 seats [citation:2]
🎯 Key Facts:
  • India has seven recognised national parties [citation:3]
  • BSP was founded by Kanshi Ram in 1984 [citation:3]
  • BJP draws inspiration from Integral Humanism [citation:13]
  • The Anti-Defection Law discourages party switching [citation:10]
  • India has over 750 registered parties [citation:11]
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Confusing national party (6% in 4 states + 4 seats) with state party (6% in state + 2 seats)
  • Forgetting that Election Commission conducts elections, not political parties
  • Mixing up the four challenges — lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money and muscle power, no meaningful choice
  • Not mentioning Kanshi Ram as BSP founder
  • Forgetting that Integral Humanism is BJP's guiding philosophy [citation:13]
