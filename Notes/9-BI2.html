Tissues High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Understanding plant and animal tissues is critical. Difference between xylem/phloem and types of muscle tissues carry heavy marks.

What is a Tissue? A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function in the body [citation:1][citation:10]. Different cells join hands to form different tissues, and each tissue has a special role.

Levels of Organisation in Our Body:

  • Cells — The basic structural and functional unit of life
  • Tissues — Groups of similar cells performing one function
  • Organs — Different tissues working together
  • Organ System — Organs working together for life processes
  • Organism — All organ systems together form a living being [citation:1]
🧠 Memory Trick — Levels of Organisation:
Cells → Tissues → Organs → Organ Systems → Organism
"CTOOO — Cells Take Over Organism's Organization"

1. Plant Tissues

Plant tissues help plants grow, stay strong, and transport food and water. They are mainly divided into Meristematic Tissue and Permanent Tissue [citation:1].

Meristematic Tissue (Growing Tissue)

Meristematic tissues are responsible for plant growth. Their cells divide continuously and remain active [citation:1].

🔬 Characteristics of Meristematic Cells:
  • Actively dividing cells
  • Large nucleus and dense cytoplasm
  • Thin cell walls (made of cellulose)
  • Vacuoles are absent — they need space for division, not storage [citation:2][citation:17]

📍 Apical Meristem

Location: Tips of roots and shoots

Function: Increases length (height of stem, depth of roots)

Growth in Length

📍 Lateral Meristem

Location: Sides of stems and roots (cambium)

Function: Increases girth (thickness)

Growth in Girth

📍 Intercalary Meristem

Location: At nodes and leaf bases

Function: Helps grasses regrow after grazing

Regrowth

Simple Permanent Tissue

These tissues are formed when meristematic cells mature and lose the ability to divide. Made of only one type of cell [citation:1][citation:2].

Feature Parenchyma Collenchyma Sclerenchyma
Cell Wall Thin (cellulose) Irregularly thickened at corners Very thick (lignin)
Living/Dead Living Living Dead at maturity
Function Storage, photosynthesis Flexibility, support Strength, rigidity
Example Leaf mesophyll Leaf stalks Coconut husk, pear fruit
Special Types Chlorenchyma (photosynthesis), Aerenchyma (buoyancy) — —
💡 Exam Insight:
  • Pear fruit crunchiness is due to sclerenchyma (lignin makes it hard) [citation:2]
  • Coconut husk removal is difficult because of tough lignified sclerenchyma cells [citation:2]
  • Tree branches bend in wind due to flexible collenchyma [citation:2]

Complex Permanent Tissue

Made of different types of cells that work together for a common function [citation:1].

💧 Xylem — Water Transport

Components: Tracheids, Vessels, Xylem parenchyma, Xylem fibres

Function: Conducts water and minerals vertically upward from roots to leaves

Mostly Dead Lignified Walls

🍯 Phloem — Food Transport

Components: Sieve tubes, Companion cells, Phloem parenchyma, Phloem fibres

Function: Conducts food (sugars) up and down from leaves to all parts

Mostly Living Thin Walls
🧠 Memory Trick — Xylem vs Phloem:
Xylem = Xpresses water UP (like an elevator going up)
Phloem = Food goes DOWN and around (like a delivery truck)

Protective Tissue — Epidermis and Cork

🛡️ Epidermis

Outermost layer of all plant organs. Single layer (multilayered in desert plants).

Functions: Protection from mechanical injury, prevention of water loss, gas exchange through stomata [citation:6][citation:16]

🪵 Cork (Phellem)

Peripheral tissue of old stems and roots. Dead cells with suberin in walls [citation:8].

Functions: Impermeable to water and gases, protects from infection and mechanical injury [citation:6]

2. Animal Tissues

Animal tissues are categorized into four types: Epithelial, Connective, Muscular, and Nervous [citation:1][citation:6].

Epithelial Tissue (Covering & Lining)

Forms the body surface and lines internal organs. Cells are closely packed with little intercellular space [citation:5].

Type Shape Location Function
Squamous Flat, scale-like Blood vessels, alveoli, mouth lining Diffusion, filtration
Cuboidal Cube-shaped Kidney tubules, salivary glands Secretion, absorption
Columnar Tall, pillar-like Intestine lining Absorption, secretion
Ciliated Columnar with cilia Respiratory tract, oviducts Movement of substances
Glandular Modified columnar Glands, intestinal lining Secretion (mucus, enzymes)
💡 Key Point: Epithelial tissue has no blood vessels (avascular). Nutrients come from underlying connective tissue [citation:5].

Connective Tissue (Connecting & Supporting)

Cells are loosely spaced and embedded in a non-living matrix. The matrix may be solid (bone), soft (areolar), or liquid (blood) [citation:5][citation:12].

🩸 Blood

Matrix: Liquid (plasma)

Function: Transport of oxygen, nutrients, hormones, wastes

Fluid Connective

🦴 Bone

Matrix: Hard (calcium & phosphorus salts)

Function: Skeletal support, protection of organs

Skeletal Connective

👂 Cartilage

Matrix: Solid but pliable (protein & sugars)

Function: Flexible support (ear, nose, trachea)

Skeletal Connective

🔗 Ligament

Function: Connects bone to bone

Property: Elastic, allows movement

Fibrous Connective

💪 Tendon

Function: Connects muscle to bone

Property: Strong, inelastic

Fibrous Connective

🧈 Adipose Tissue

Function: Stores fat, insulation, shock absorption

Location: Below skin, around organs

Loose Connective
🧠 Memory Trick — Ligament vs Tendon:
Ligament = Links Bone to Bone (L-B-B)
Tendon = Ties Muscle to Bone (T-M-B)

Muscular Tissue (Movement)

Contains contractile proteins (actin and myosin) that enable contraction and relaxation [citation:8].

Feature Striated (Skeletal) Smooth (Unstriated) Cardiac
Control Voluntary Involuntary Involuntary
Striations Present Absent Present
Cell Shape Long, cylindrical, multinucleate Spindle-shaped, uninucleate Branched, cylindrical, uninucleate
Location Limbs, body wall Intestine, blood vessels Heart
Fatigue Fatigues quickly Contracts slowly, long-lasting Never fatigues
💡 Exam Insight: Cardiac muscles are involuntary, striated, branched, and uninucleate. They contract rhythmically throughout life without fatigue [citation:2][citation:6].

Nervous Tissue (Communication)

Highly specialized for being stimulated and transmitting stimulus rapidly. Consists of neurons (nerve cells) [citation:1].

🧠 Structure of a Neuron

  • Cell body (Cyton): Contains nucleus and cytoplasm
  • Dendrites: Short, branched extensions that receive signals [citation:8]
  • Axon: Long extension that transmits signals away from cell body
  • Nerve endings: Release neurotransmitters

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Tips:
  • Xylem transports UP; Phloem transports UP & DOWN
  • Ligament connects Bone to Bone; Tendon connects Muscle to Bone
  • Meristematic cells lack vacuoles — they divide, not store
  • Sclerenchyma = Dead + Lignin (coconut husk, pear crunchiness)
  • Cardiac muscle: Involuntary + Striated + Branched + Uninucleate + Never fatigues
  • Epithelial tissue is avascular (no blood vessels)
  • Adipose tissue = Fat storage (blubber of whales, hump of camels)
