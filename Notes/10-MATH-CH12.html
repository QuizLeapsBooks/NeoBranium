Surface Areas and Volumes High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter extends your understanding of 3D shapes from Class 9 to combinations of solids. It teaches you to calculate surface areas and volumes of real-life objects like capsules, toys, buckets, and containers. Board exams consistently test these application-based problems.

1.1 Introduction

In our day-to-day life, we come across a number of solids made up of combinations of two or more of the basic solids like cuboid, cone, cylinder, and sphere [citation:15]. For example, a truck with a container fitted on its back is made of a cylinder with two hemispheres at its ends [citation:18]. Similarly, a test tube is a combination of a cylinder and a hemisphere [citation:18].

1.2 Key Concepts

TermDefinition
Surface AreaThe total area of all the surfaces of a 3D object [citation:1]
VolumeThe space occupied by a 3D object [citation:1]
CSACurved Surface Area — area of only the curved part [citation:17]
TSATotal Surface Area — area of all outer surfaces including flat faces [citation:17]
CapacityInternal space available in a hollow object [citation:13]
🎯 Important: Surface area uses square units (cm², m²) while volume uses cubic units (cm³, m³) [citation:13].

2. Formulas for Basic Solids

Before solving combination problems, you must memorize the formulas for basic solids [citation:1].

Solid CSA / LSA TSA Volume
Cube (edge = a) 4a² 6a² a³
Cuboid (l, b, h) 2h(l + b) 2(lb + bh + hl) lbh
Cylinder (r, h) 2πrh 2πr(r + h) πr²h
Cone (r, h, l) πrl πr(r + l) (1/3)πr²h
Sphere (r) 4πr² 4πr² (4/3)πr³
Hemisphere (r) 2πr² 3πr² (2/3)πr³
Important Relationship for Cone:
l² = r² + h²
where l = slant height, r = radius, h = perpendicular height [citation:9]

2.1 Solved Example — Basic Formulas

Example 1: Find the curved surface area of a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 10 cm [citation:11].

Solution:

CSA = 2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 10 = 440 cm²

Example 2: Find the volume of a sphere with radius 7 cm [citation:11].

Solution:

Volume = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3) × (22/7) × 343 = 1437.33 cm³

3. Surface Area of a Combination of Solids

3.1 Key Principle

Rule for Surface Area:
When solids are combined, the total surface area is the sum of the curved surface areas of each individual part, excluding the overlapping (internal) surfaces [citation:1][citation:7].
⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often add the total surface areas of both solids. You must subtract the area of contact (the base of the hemisphere or cone that is hidden inside) [citation:1].

3.2 Common Combinations

Combination 1: Cylinder with Two Hemispheres (Capsule Shape)

Used in: Medicine capsules, truck containers

TSA = CSA of cylinder + 2 × CSA of hemisphere
= 2πrh + 2 × (2πr²)
= 2πrh + 4πr² = 2πr(h + 2r)

Combination 2: Cone on a Cylinder

Used in: Circus tents, rocket shapes

TSA = CSA of cone + CSA of cylinder + Area of circular base
= πrl + 2πrh + πr²

Combination 3: Cone on a Hemisphere (Ice Cream Cone)

TSA = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere
= πrl + 2πr²

Combination 4: Cube with Hemisphere on Top

TSA = TSA of cube − Area of hemisphere base + CSA of hemisphere
= 6a² − πr² + 2πr² = 6a² + πr²

3.3 Solved Example — Playing Top (NCERT)

Example 3: Rasheed got a playing top (lattu) shaped like a cone surmounted by a hemisphere. The entire top is 5 cm in height and the diameter is 3.5 cm. Find the area he has to colour. (Use π = 22/7) [citation:7]

Solution:

Radius r = 3.5/2 = 1.75 cm

Height of cone = 5 − 1.75 = 3.25 cm

Slant height l = √(r² + h²) = √(1.75² + 3.25²) ≈ 3.7 cm

TSA = CSA of hemisphere + CSA of cone

= 2πr² + πrl

= 2 × (22/7) × (1.75)² + (22/7) × 1.75 × 3.7

= 19.25 + 20.35 = 39.6 cm² (approximately)

3.4 Solved Example — Decorative Block (NCERT)

Example 4: A decorative block is made of a cube with edge 5 cm and a hemisphere fixed on top with diameter 4.2 cm. Find the TSA [citation:7].

Solution:

Radius of hemisphere r = 2.1 cm

TSA of cube = 6 × 5² = 150 cm²

Area of hemisphere base (hidden) = πr² = (22/7) × 2.1² = 13.86 cm²

CSA of hemisphere = 2πr² = 2 × 13.86 = 27.72 cm²

Total TSA = 150 − 13.86 + 27.72 = 163.86 cm²

4. Volume of a Combination of Solids

Rule for Volume:
The total volume of a combination is simply the sum of the volumes of the individual solids. No subtraction is needed [citation:1].
🎯 Key Point: Unlike surface area, volume has no "overlapping" issue because the internal space is still part of the total volume.

4.1 Solved Example — Capsule Volume

Example 5: A solid is in the form of a cylinder with hemispherical ends. Total height is 20 cm and diameter is 7 cm. Find the total volume. (Use π = 22/7) [citation:2]

Solution:

Radius r = 3.5 cm

Height of cylinder = 20 − 2(3.5) = 13 cm

Volume = Volume of cylinder + 2 × Volume of hemisphere

= πr²h + 2 × (2/3)πr³

= (22/7) × (3.5)² × 13 + (4/3) × (22/7) × (3.5)³

= 500.5 + 179.67 = 680.17 cm³ (approximately)

5. Frustum of a Cone

A frustum is the portion of a cone obtained by cutting it with a plane parallel to its base [citation:6]. Buckets and drinking glasses are common examples.

5.1 Key Formulas

Let R = larger radius, r = smaller radius, h = height, l = slant height.

Slant height: l = √[h² + (R − r)²]

Volume = (1/3)πh[R² + r² + Rr]

CSA = π(R + r)l

TSA = π(R + r)l + πR² + πr²

5.2 Solved Example — Bucket

Example 6: A bucket is in the form of a frustum with capacity 12308.8 cm³. The radii of the top and bottom are 20 cm and 12 cm. Find the height. (Use π = 3.14) [citation:2]

Solution:

Volume = (1/3)πh(R² + r² + Rr)

12308.8 = (1/3) × 3.14 × h × (400 + 144 + 240)

12308.8 = (1/3) × 3.14 × h × 784

h = (12308.8 × 3) / (3.14 × 784) = 15 cm

6. Conversion of Solids (Melting and Recasting)

Key Principle: When a solid is melted and recast into another shape, the volume remains the same [citation:12][citation:14].

This principle is used to solve problems like:

  • Melting a cylinder and casting into spheres
  • Converting a cone into a wire
  • Finding the number of small toys made from a large solid

6.1 Solved Example — Melting and Recasting

Example 7: A solid metallic cylinder of diameter 12 cm and height 15 cm is melted and recast into toys each in the shape of a cone of radius 3 cm and height 9 cm. Find the number of toys [citation:2].

Solution:

Volume of cylinder = πr²h = π × 6² × 15 = 540π cm³

Volume of one toy = (1/3)πr²h = (1/3) × π × 3² × 9 = 27π cm³

Number of toys = 540π / 27π = 20 toys

6.2 Solved Example — Number of Small Spheres

Example 8: A solid sphere of radius 6 cm is melted into small spheres of radius 2 cm. Find the number of small spheres [citation:11].

Solution:

Volume of large sphere = (4/3)π(6)³ = 288π cm³

Volume of small sphere = (4/3)π(2)³ = (32/3)π cm³

Number = 288π / (32π/3) = 288 × 3 / 32 = 27 spheres

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2024) The surface area of a sphere is 616 cm². Its diameter is: (a) 7 cm (b) 14 cm (c) 21 cm (d) 28 cm [1 Mark] [citation:14]
  2. (CBSE 2023) A cone and cylinder have the same radii but the height of the cone is 3 times that of the cylinder. The ratio of their volumes is: (a) 1:1 (b) 1:3 (c) 3:1 (d) 1:9 [1 Mark] [citation:14]
  3. (CBSE 2022) During the conversion of a solid from one shape to another, the volume of the new shape: (a) increases (b) decreases (c) remains the same (d) doubles [1 Mark] [citation:14]
  4. (CBSE 2019) A solid is in the form of a cylinder with hemispherical ends. Total height is 20 cm and diameter is 7 cm. Find the total volume [citation:2]
  5. (CBSE 2019) A bucket open at the top is in the form of a frustum with capacity 12308.8 cm³. Radii are 20 cm and 12 cm. Find the height and the area of the metal sheet used [citation:2]
  6. (CBSE 2018) The diameters of the lower and upper ends of a bucket in the form of a frustum are 10 cm and 30 cm. If its height is 24 cm, find the area of the metal sheet used [citation:2]
  7. (CBSE 2017) A solid metallic cylinder of diameter 12 cm and height 15 cm is melted and recast into toys each in the shape of a cone of radius 3 cm and height 9 cm. Find the number of toys [citation:2]
  8. (CBSE 2015) A solid wooden toy is in the form of a hemisphere surmounted by a cone of same radius. The radius of hemisphere is 3.5 cm and total wood used is 166 5/6 cm³. Find the height of the toy [citation:5]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Principles:
  • Surface Area of Combination: Add CSAs of visible parts, subtract hidden contact areas [citation:1].
  • Volume of Combination: Simply add volumes of individual solids [citation:1].
  • Conversion of Solids: Volume remains constant [citation:12].
🎯 Essential Formulas:
  • Cone: l² = r² + h²
  • Frustum Volume: (1/3)πh[R² + r² + Rr]
  • Frustum Slant: √[h² + (R − r)²]
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting to subtract the overlapping surface area when combining solids [citation:1]
  • Using TSA of individual solids instead of CSA for combinations [citation:7]
  • Confusing slant height (l) with perpendicular height (h) in cone problems [citation:13]
  • Not converting diameter to radius before using formulas [citation:13]
