Outcomes of Democracy Board Exam Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter evaluates how democracy performs in real life. It assesses whether democracy delivers on its promises — accountable government, economic development, social equality, and individual dignity. Board exams frequently test the expected vs actual outcomes of democracy, economic growth comparisons, and the accommodation of social diversity.

1.1 How Do We Assess Democracy's Outcomes?

Democracy is a better form of government than its alternatives because it :

  • Promotes equality among citizens
  • Enhances the dignity of the individual
  • Improves the quality of decision-making
  • Provides a method to resolve conflicts
  • Allows room to correct mistakes
🎯 Key Point: We cannot measure democracy only by looking at its promises. We must assess its actual outcomes — what it achieves in practice.

2. Accountable, Responsive and Legitimate Government

The most basic outcome of democracy is that it produces a government that is accountable to the citizens and responsive to their needs and expectations .

2.1 Accountability

In a democracy, citizens have the right to examine the process of decision-making. This is known as transparency. A citizen who wants to know if a decision was taken through the correct procedures can find this out. This is often missing in non-democratic governments .

2.2 Responsiveness

Democracy ensures that the government is responsive to the needs and expectations of citizens. When citizens feel the government is insensitive to their demands, they can take action through demonstrations, campaigns, and protests to compel the government to act .

2.3 Legitimacy

A democratic government is a legitimate government because it is elected by the people through regular, free and fair elections. Laws are made following proper procedures after much discussion. If people feel the government has done something against the Constitution, they can challenge it in the judiciary .

FeatureDemocracyNon-Democracy
AccountabilityCitizens can question and replace rulersRulers not answerable to people
TransparencyDecision-making process open to scrutinyOften secretive and closed
LegitimacyGovernment elected by people, accepted by themGovernment may not have public acceptance
Decision-makingDeliberation and negotiation, takes time but more acceptableQuick decisions, less deliberation
💡 Key Point: Democracies may be slower in decision-making because they follow procedures and deliberation. But because they follow procedures, their decisions are more acceptable to people and more effective .

3. Economic Growth and Development

3.1 Comparing Democracies and Dictatorships

If we consider all democracies and all dictatorships for the fifty years between 1950 and 2000, dictatorships have a slightly higher rate of economic growth .

⚠️ Important Nuance: This does not mean democracy is bad for economic growth. The difference in economic development between less developed countries with dictatorships and democracies is negligible. Economic growth depends on many factors, not just the form of government .

3.2 Factors Affecting Economic Growth

Economic development of a country depends on :

  • Size of the population
  • Global situation
  • Cooperation from other countries
  • Economic policies adopted by the country
🎯 Key Point: Democracy is not a guarantee of economic development. But it is a better form of government because it provides accountability, participation, and peaceful correction of policy mistakes.

4. Reduction of Inequality and Poverty

Democracies aim for political equality — every citizen has one vote regardless of wealth, caste, or religion. However, economic inequalities often persist .

4.1 Reality of Economic Inequality

  • In many democratic countries like Brazil and South Africa, about 20% of the population takes up more than 60% of the world's income .
  • Poverty remains a major challenge in many democracies like Bangladesh, where more than half the population lives in poverty .

4.2 How Democracy Addresses Inequality

Democracy provides the space to demand policies that reduce inequality :

  • Progressive taxation
  • Social welfare programs
  • Minimum support prices for farmers
  • Public health and education
💡 Key Point: Political equality does not automatically lead to economic equality. But democracy gives poorer citizens the political rights and peaceful methods to demand welfare, redistribution, and equal opportunity .

5. Accommodation of Social Diversity

Democracy is best suited to handle social diversity because it allows different groups to live together peacefully. No society can fully and permanently resolve conflicts among different groups, but democracy provides a method to negotiate differences .

5.1 Two Conditions for Democracy to Accommodate Diversity

ConditionExplanation
Majority and Minority CooperationBoth majority and minority groups must work together. The majority must not exclude the minority permanently.
No Permanent MajorityRule by majority should not become rule by one religious, racial, or linguistic community. In a democracy, any group can form a majority in different elections and on different issues.

5.2 Examples

  • Belgium: Successfully managed diversity through federalism and equal representation for Dutch and French communities.
  • Sri Lanka: Failed to accommodate Tamil minority, leading to civil war.
🎯 Key Point: Democracy accommodates social diversity when the majority works with minorities, and when majority rule does not become permanent domination by one community.

6. Dignity and Freedom of Citizens

Democracy's greatest achievement is the respect it provides for individual dignity and freedom. In a democracy, every individual's voice matters, and every person has the right to live with dignity and make choices freely .

6.1 Dignity of Women

  • Historically, women were treated as second-class citizens in male-dominated societies
  • Democracy has pushed for equal treatment and women's rights
  • Women can now demand equal status, equal pay, and protection from discrimination

6.2 Dignity of Disadvantaged Castes

  • Democracy in India has strengthened the demand of disadvantaged and discriminated castes for equal status and opportunity
  • Caste inequalities have not disappeared, but democratic rights allow affected citizens to question discrimination and seek reform
  • The dignity and freedom that democracy offers give people a sense of empowerment and ownership in their society
💡 Key Point: Dignity and freedom are fundamental to democracy. A democratic society upholds these values by protecting individual rights, providing avenues for self-expression, and allowing people to participate in decision-making .

7. Democracy's Examination Never Gets Over

Democracy is a system that constantly evolves and improves. The process of examining democracy never ends because :

  • People consistently demand more benefits in a democracy
  • Public complaints about democracy do not always prove that democracy has failed
  • Public criticism can show that citizens are aware of their rights and expect rulers to perform better
  • Democracy changes people from passive subjects into active citizens who can examine those in power
Key Insight: Democracy is always striving for a better goal. It is a superior form of government compared to dictatorship or any other alternative because it allows for peaceful correction of mistakes and continuous improvement.

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Accountability: The duty of rulers to explain their decisions and answer to citizens
  • Responsiveness: Attention to people's needs, expectations and grievances
  • Legitimacy: Public acceptance that a government has the rightful authority to govern
  • Transparency: Openness that enables citizens to examine government decisions and procedures
  • Political Equality: Equal formal political status, especially equal voting rights
  • Economic Inequality: Unequal distribution of income, wealth and opportunity
🎯 Key Comparisons:
  • Democracy vs Dictatorship: Dictatorships have slightly higher economic growth (1950-2000), but the difference is negligible
  • Political vs Economic Equality: Democracy ensures political equality but does not guarantee economic equality
🎯 Key Points:
  • Democracy is better because it promotes equality, enhances dignity, improves decision-making, resolves conflicts, and allows correction of mistakes
  • Democracies accommodate diversity when majority and minority work together and no group permanently dominates
  • Democracy's examination never gets over — it constantly evolves and improves
  • Public criticism of democracy shows citizens are aware and engaged, not that democracy has failed
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Thinking democracy guarantees economic growth — it does not; other factors matter
  • Forgetting that dictatorship had slightly higher growth between 1950-2000, but the difference is negligible
  • Confusing political equality (equal votes) with economic equality (equal income)
  • Not mentioning the two conditions for accommodating diversity: majority-minority cooperation and no permanent majority
  • Forgetting that "democracy's examination never gets over" — it's a continuous process
