Development High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter introduces the concept of development and how it is measured. It teaches you that development is not just about income but also about health, education, equality, and freedom. Board exams frequently test definitions, comparisons, and case-based questions.

1.1 What is Development?

Development refers to the improvement in the quality of life and the overall well-being of people. It is a comprehensive process that includes economic growth along with social progress.

Development is not just about earning more money. It also includes:

  • Better education
  • Improved healthcare
  • Equal treatment and freedom
  • Security and respect
  • A clean environment
🎯 Key Point: Development means different things to different people. What is development for one person may not be development for another.

2. Different People, Different Developmental Goals

2.1 Why Goals Differ

People have different life situations, so their developmental goals also differ. What one person considers progress may be harmful or destructive for another.

Category of PersonDevelopmental Goal
Landless rural labourersMore days of work and better wages
Prosperous farmers from PunjabHigher support prices for their crops
Farmers depending only on rainAvailability of other sources of irrigation
A rural woman from a land-owning familyRegular job and high wages to increase her income
Urban unemployed youthRegular job with high wages
A girl from a rich urban familyFreedom and opportunity equal to her brother

2.2 Conflicting Goals

💡 Example of Conflicting Goals:
Industrialists want more dams to generate electricity. But building dams submerges land and displaces tribal people. What is development for industrialists is destruction for the displaced people.

Other examples:

  • A worker wants higher wages, but the employer wants lower wages.
  • A consumer wants low prices, but the seller wants high prices.

3. Income and Other Goals

3.1 Material Goals

Material goals are things that can be bought with money. Examples include food, clothes, a house, and other goods.

3.2 Non-Material Goals

Non-material goals cannot be measured only through money. Examples include:

  • Equal treatment
  • Freedom
  • Security
  • Respect for others
🎯 Key Point: Money can buy goods and services, but it cannot always provide a pollution-free environment, social respect, or equal treatment.

3.3 A Mix of Goals

For development, people look at a mix of goals. A job with high pay but no security may not be better than a lower-paying job with regular employment.

4. National Development

4.1 Definition

National development means thinking about what is fair and useful for the whole country. It is the country's ability to improve the living standards of all its residents.

4.2 Key Questions for National Development

QuestionWhy It Matters
Does the goal benefit many people?Development should not help only a small group
Does it harm another group?One group's progress may damage another's livelihood
Is it fair and just?Development should protect dignity and rights
Is there a better way?Alternatives may reduce damage
🎯 Key Point: National development is not only about increasing income. It also includes health, education, equality, public facilities, and environmental protection.

5. How to Compare Different Countries or States?

5.1 Total Income vs Per Capita Income

Total income cannot be used for comparison because different countries have different populations. A country with a large population may have high total income but low income per person.

5.2 Per Capita Income

Per capita income is also called average income. It is the total income of a country divided by its total population.

Per Capita Income = Total Income of Country / Total Population

5.3 World Bank Criterion

The World Bank uses per capita income as the main criterion to classify countries:

  • Countries with per capita income of US$ 12,056 per annum and above (2017) are called rich countries.
  • Countries with per capita income of US$ 955 or less are called low-income countries. India falls in this category.

5.4 Limitations of Per Capita Income

⚠️ Limitations:
  • It does not show income distribution
  • It does not tell whether people have equal access to education and healthcare
  • It ignores non-material goals such as freedom, respect, and security
  • It does not measure pollution or environmental damage
  • It can hide inequality between rich and poor people
Example: Country A and Country B both have the same average income of Rs 10,000. But in Country A, everyone earns around Rs 10,000. In Country B, one person earns Rs 48,000 and four people earn Rs 500 each. The average hides this huge inequality.

6. Income and Other Criteria

Besides income, other important criteria are used to measure development:

IndicatorWhat It Measures
Literacy RateProportion of literate people aged 7 years and above
Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)Number of children dying before one year per 1000 live births
Net Attendance RatioPercentage of children in a specific age group attending school
Life ExpectancyAverage expected length of life at birth
💡 Kerala vs Haryana Example:
Kerala has lower per capita income than Haryana, but Kerala has better human development ranking. Why? Because Kerala has better literacy rate, lower infant mortality rate, and better public facilities. This proves that income alone is not enough.

7. Public Facilities

7.1 Definition

Public facilities are services provided by the government for the welfare of all people. They are important because many people cannot afford these services privately.

7.2 Examples of Public Facilities

  • Schools and colleges
  • Hospitals and healthcare
  • Roads and public transport
  • Clean drinking water
  • Sanitation
  • Electricity
  • Public Distribution System (PDS)
  • Law and order

7.3 Why Public Facilities Are Important

  • They provide essential services to all sections of society
  • They help poor and middle-income families access education, healthcare, and basic needs
  • Without public facilities, only rich people can access good schools and hospitals, increasing inequality
  • Some services like preventing infectious diseases need community-level action, not just individual spending

8. Human Development Index (HDI)

8.1 What is HDI?

The Human Development Index (HDI) is used by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) to compare countries based on development. It is published in the Human Development Report.

8.2 Components of HDI

ComponentWhat It Measures
HealthLife expectancy at birth
EducationSchooling and literacy-related indicators
IncomeGross National Income per capita

8.3 World Bank vs UNDP Criteria

BasisWorld BankUNDP
Main criterionPer capita incomeHuman Development Index
FocusIncome onlyIncome, health, and education
ScopeNarrowerBroader
LimitationIgnores quality-of-life indicatorsStill cannot measure every aspect of life
🎯 Key Point: The UNDP method is broader than the World Bank method because it looks beyond income.

9. Sustainable Development

9.1 Definition

Sustainable development means development that meets the needs of the present generation without harming the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

9.2 Why Sustainability is Important

  • Natural resources are limited
  • If resources like groundwater, forests, minerals, and fossil fuels are overused, future generations will face shortages
  • Development should not only increase income today but also protect the environment for tomorrow

9.3 Examples of Unsustainable Development

  • Overuse of groundwater
  • Cutting forests without replanting
  • Excessive use of fossil fuels
  • Pollution from industries
  • Mining without environmental protection
  • Wasting natural resources
💡 Famous Quote: "The Earth has enough resources to meet the needs of all but not enough to satisfy the greed of even one person." — Mahatma Gandhi

9.4 Present Energy Sources in India

Present sources: electricity, coal, crude oil, cow dung, and solar energy.

Future possibilities: ethanol, bio-diesel, nuclear energy, and better use of wind and solar energy.

10. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (2023) "Different persons can have different developmental goals." Support the statement with an example. [1 Mark]
  2. (2022) Assume there are four families in a country. The average per capita income of these families is Rs 5000. If the income of three families is Rs 4000, Rs 7000 and Rs 3000 respectively, what is the income of the fourth family? (a) Rs 7500 (b) Rs 3000 (c) Rs 2000 (d) Rs 6000 [1 Mark]
  3. (2020) Which of the following neighbouring countries has better performance in terms of human development than India? (a) Bangladesh (b) Sri Lanka (c) Nepal (d) Pakistan [1 Mark]
  4. (2019) What is the main criterion used by the World Bank in classifying different countries? What are the limitations of this criterion? [3 Marks]
  5. (2018) "Conflicting goals can also be developmental goals." Elaborate with examples. [3 Marks]
  6. (2017) Kerala, with lower per capita income, has a better human development ranking than Haryana. Hence, per capita income is not a useful criterion at all. Do you agree? Discuss. [5 Marks]
  7. (NCERT) Why is the issue of sustainability important for development? [3 Marks]
  8. (NCERT) Why do we use averages? Are there any limitations to their use? Illustrate with your own examples. [3 Marks]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Development: Improvement in quality of life
  • Per Capita Income: Total income ÷ Total population
  • Public Facilities: Services provided by government for all
  • HDI: Measures health, education, and income
  • Sustainable Development: Meets present needs without harming future
🎯 Key Comparisons:
  • World Bank: Only per capita income
  • UNDP: Income + health + education
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Thinking development is only about income — it also includes health, education, and equality
  • Forgetting that averages hide income inequality
  • Confusing total income with per capita income
  • Not giving examples when asked about conflicting goals
