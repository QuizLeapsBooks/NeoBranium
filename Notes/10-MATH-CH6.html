Triangles High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Triangles is one of the highest-weightage geometry chapters in Class 10. It covers similarity of triangles, the Basic Proportionality Theorem, area relationships, and the Pythagoras Theorem. Board exams frequently test similarity proofs and multi-step problems combining multiple concepts.

1.1 What are Similar Figures?

Two figures having the same shape but not necessarily the same size are called similar figures.

  • Corresponding angles of similar figures are equal.
  • Corresponding sides of similar figures are proportional (in the same ratio).
🎯 Key Result: All congruent figures are similar, but all similar figures are not necessarily congruent.

1.2 Similarity vs Congruence

FeatureSimilar TrianglesCongruent Triangles
ShapeSameSame
SizeMay be differentSame
Corresponding anglesEqualEqual
Corresponding sidesProportionalEqual
Scale factorAny positive valueExactly 1

1.3 Similar Triangles — Definition

Two triangles are said to be similar if:

  1. Their corresponding angles are equal, and
  2. Their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).
If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then:
∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R
AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR

1.4 Scale Factor

The scale factor is the number by which every corresponding length is multiplied to produce the second similar figure.

Example: If ΔABC ~ ΔDEF, AB = 6 cm and DE = 9 cm, the scale factor is 9/6 = 3/2.

2. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales' Theorem)

Theorem 6.1 (BPT): If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

Statement: In ΔABC, if DE || BC, where D lies on AB and E lies on AC, then:

AD/DB = AE/EC

2.1 Proof of Basic Proportionality Theorem

Given: ΔABC in which DE || BC, D on AB and E on AC.

To Prove: AD/DB = AE/EC

Construction: Join BE and CD. Draw EM ⊥ AB and DN ⊥ AC.

Proof:

Area of ΔADE = (1/2) × AD × EM ...(i)

Area of ΔDBE = (1/2) × DB × EM ...(ii)

From (i) and (ii):

ar(ΔADE) / ar(ΔDBE) = AD / DB ...(iii)

Similarly, ar(ΔADE) / ar(ΔCDE) = AE / EC ...(iv)

Since ΔDBE and ΔCDE are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC:

ar(ΔDBE) = ar(ΔCDE) ...(v)

From (iii), (iv) and (v):

AD/DB = AE/EC

Hence proved.

2.2 Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

Theorem 6.2: If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

Statement: If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE || BC.

Use this theorem when the ratio is given and you need to prove that lines are parallel.

2.3 Solved Example

Example: In ΔABC, D lies on AB with AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm. E lies on AC and DE || BC. Find AE if EC = 9 cm.

Solution:

By BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC

4/6 = AE/9

AE = (4 × 9) / 6 = 36/6 = 6 cm

2.4 Special Cases of BPT

  • The line drawn from the mid-point of one side of a triangle parallel to another side bisects the third side.
  • The line joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.
  • The diagonals of a trapezium divide each other proportionally.
  • If the diagonals of a quadrilateral divide each other proportionally, then it is a trapezium.

3. Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

There are four criteria to prove two triangles similar without checking all angles and sides.

3.1 AAA Similarity Criterion

Statement: If in two triangles, corresponding angles are equal, then their corresponding sides are in the same ratio and hence the two triangles are similar.

If ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, and ∠C = ∠F, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

3.2 AA Similarity Criterion

Statement: If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.

If ∠A = ∠D and ∠B = ∠E, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

AA is the most tested criterion in board exams.

3.3 SSS Similarity Criterion

Statement: If in two triangles, corresponding sides are in the same ratio, then their corresponding angles are equal and hence the triangles are similar.

If AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

Critical check: All three ratios must match.

3.4 SAS Similarity Criterion

Statement: If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are in the same ratio, then the two triangles are similar.

If AB/DE = AC/DF and ∠A = ∠D, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

Critical check: The equal angle must be between the proportional sides.

3.5 Summary of Similarity Criteria

CriterionInformation RequiredCritical Check
AATwo pairs of corresponding angles equalAngles must correspond
SSSThree pairs of corresponding sides proportionalAll three ratios must match
SASTwo pairs of sides proportional + included angles equalAngle must lie between the sides
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Do not assume triangles are similar just because they look similar in a diagram. Establish equality using given facts or angle properties.
  • SSS for similarity is NOT the same as SSS for congruence. Check that ratios are equal, not lengths.
  • Do not use SSA (Side-Side-Angle) to prove similarity — it is not valid.

4. Areas of Similar Triangles

Theorem 6.6: The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then:

ar(ΔABC) / ar(ΔPQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (AC/PR)²

Important: The same square relationship holds for corresponding altitudes, medians, and angle bisectors.

4.1 Solved Example

Example: Two similar triangles have areas 25 cm² and 64 cm². If one side of the first triangle is 10 cm, find the corresponding side of the second triangle.

Solution:

Area ratio = 25/64

Side ratio = √(25/64) = 5/8

Let the required side be x.

5/8 = 10/x → x = (10 × 8) / 5 = 16 cm

🎯 Quick Check: If sides are in ratio 2:3, areas are in ratio 4:9. Conversely, if areas are in ratio 4:9, sides are in ratio 2:3 (take the square root).

5. Pythagoras Theorem

Theorem 6.8 (Pythagoras Theorem): In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

For right ΔABC, right-angled at B:

AC² = AB² + BC²

Here, AC is the hypotenuse (side opposite the right angle), and AB and BC are the other two sides.

5.1 Proof Idea (Using Similarity)

The NCERT proof uses similar triangles. Drawing an altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse creates two smaller triangles, each similar to the original and to each other. The theorem follows from the proportional sides.

5.2 Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

Theorem 6.9: If the square of one side of a triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.

If AC² = AB² + BC², then ∠B = 90°.

5.3 Solved Example

Example: Check whether a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm is right-angled.

Solution:

5² = 25

3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25

Since 5² = 3² + 4², the triangle is right-angled.

🎯 Common Pythagorean Triples:
  • (3, 4, 5)
  • (5, 12, 13)
  • (8, 15, 17)
  • (7, 24, 25)

6. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2020) In ΔABC, DE || BC. If AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and AE = 5 cm, find EC. [1 Mark]
  2. (CBSE 2015) In the given figure, DE || BC. Find EC. [2 Marks]
  3. (CBSE 2025) ΔABC ~ ΔPQR in which AB = 6 cm, BC = 4 cm, AC = 8 cm and PR = 6 cm. Find the length of (PQ + QR). [2 Marks]
  4. (CBSE 2020) ABC is an equilateral triangle of side 2a. Find the length of one of its altitudes. [2 Marks]
  5. (CBSE) In triangles ABC and DEF, ∠B = ∠E, ∠F = ∠C and AB = 3DE. Then the two triangles are: (a) congruent but not similar (b) similar but not congruent (c) neither similar nor congruent (d) congruent as well as similar. [1 Mark]
  6. (NCERT) Prove that the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding medians. [3 Marks]
  7. (NCERT) Sides of two similar triangles are in the ratio 4:9. Find the ratio of their areas. [1 Mark]
  8. (NCERT) ABC and BDE are two equilateral triangles such that D is the mid-point of BC. Find the ratio of the areas of triangles ABC and BDE. [2 Marks]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Theorems & Formulas:
  • BPT: If DE || BC, then AD/DB = AE/EC
  • Converse of BPT: If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE || BC
  • Similarity Criteria: AA, SSS, SAS
  • Areas of Similar Triangles: ar(ΔABC)/ar(ΔPQR) = (AB/PQ)²
  • Pythagoras Theorem: AC² = AB² + BC² (for right ΔABC at B)
  • Converse of Pythagoras: If AC² = AB² + BC², then ∠B = 90°
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting to square the ratio when finding areas
  • Using SSS similarity when ratios are not equal for all three sides
  • Assuming triangles are similar without establishing equality of angles or proportionality of sides
  • Not writing the similarity statement in correct vertex order
