Chapter 2: Polynomials High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? The relationship between zeroes and coefficients, and the division algorithm are frequently tested. Every year, 6-10 marks questions appear from this chapter in CBSE board exams.

1. Polynomial & Degree

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made up of variables and coefficients, involving only addition, subtraction, multiplication, and non-negative integer exponents of variables.

Standard Form

A polynomial in one variable x is written as:

p(x) = aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ... + a₁x + a₀

where aₙ ≠ 0 and a₀, a₁, ..., aₙ are real numbers (coefficients).

Degree of a Polynomial

The degree is the highest exponent of the variable in the polynomial.

Types Based on Degree

  • Linear Polynomial: Degree 1. Example: 2x + 3
  • Quadratic Polynomial: Degree 2. Example: x² + 5x − 6
  • Cubic Polynomial: Degree 3. Example: 2x³ − 3x² + 5x − 7
  • Constant Polynomial: Degree 0. Example: 5

Types Based on Number of Terms

  • Monomial: One term. Example: 3x²
  • Binomial: Two terms. Example: x² + 1
  • Trinomial: Three terms. Example: x² + x + 1
📌 Degree vs Number of Zeroes: A polynomial of degree n can have at most n zeroes. For example, a quadratic polynomial (degree 2) can have 0, 1, or 2 real zeroes.

2. Geometrical Meaning of Zeroes

A zero of a polynomial p(x) is a real number k such that p(k) = 0. Geometrically, zeroes are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) intersects the x-axis.

Graph of Linear Polynomial (ax + b)

  • The graph is a straight line.
  • It intersects the x-axis at exactly one point.
  • Therefore, a linear polynomial has exactly one zero.

Graph of Quadratic Polynomial (ax² + bx + c)

  • The graph is a parabola — open upwards if a > 0, open downwards if a < 0.
  • It can intersect the x-axis at 0, 1, or 2 points.
🔑 Three Cases for Quadratic Polynomial:
  • Two distinct zeroes: Graph cuts x-axis at two points.
  • One zero (repeated): Graph touches x-axis at one point.
  • No real zeroes: Graph does not intersect x-axis at all.

Graph of Cubic Polynomial (ax³ + bx² + cx + d)

  • The graph is an S-shaped curve.
  • It can intersect the x-axis at up to 3 points.
  • An odd-degree polynomial always has at least one real zero.

3. Relationship Between Zeroes & Coefficients

This is the most important section for board exams. The sum and product of zeroes can be directly calculated from the coefficients.

A. For Quadratic Polynomial: ax² + bx + c

If α and β are the zeroes, then:

Sum of zeroes: α + β = −b/a

Product of zeroes: αβ = c/a

B. For Cubic Polynomial: ax³ + bx² + cx + d

If α, β, and γ are the zeroes, then:

Sum: α + β + γ = −b/a

Sum of products (two at a time): αβ + βγ + γα = c/a

Product: αβγ = −d/a

Forming a Quadratic Polynomial from Zeroes

If α and β are the zeroes, the polynomial is:

p(x) = x² − (α + β)x + αβ

That is: x² − (sum of zeroes)x + (product of zeroes).

Forming a Cubic Polynomial from Zeroes

p(x) = x³ − (α+β+γ)x² + (αβ+βγ+γα)x − αβγ
📌 Solved Example: Find zeroes of p(x) = x² + 7x + 12 and verify the relationship.

Solution: p(x) = (x + 3)(x + 4) ⇒ Zeroes are −3 and −4.
Sum = −3 + (−4) = −7 = −b/a = −(7)/1 ✓
Product = (−3)(−4) = 12 = c/a = 12/1 ✓

4. Division Algorithm for Polynomials

If p(x) and g(x) are any two polynomials with g(x) ≠ 0, then we can find polynomials q(x) and r(x) such that:

p(x) = g(x) × q(x) + r(x)

where r(x) = 0 or degree of r(x) < degree of g(x)

Here:

  • p(x) = Dividend
  • g(x) = Divisor
  • q(x) = Quotient
  • r(x) = Remainder

Steps for Long Division

  1. Arrange both polynomials in descending order of their degrees.
  2. Divide the highest degree term of the dividend by the highest degree term of the divisor — this gives the first term of the quotient.
  3. Multiply the entire divisor by this term and subtract from the dividend.
  4. Bring down the next term and repeat until the degree of the remainder is less than the divisor.
🔑 Solved Example: Divide p(x) = x³ − 3x² + x + 2 by g(x) = x − 2.

Step 1: x³ ÷ x = x²
Step 2: Multiply: x²(x − 2) = x³ − 2x². Subtract: (−3x² + x) − (−2x²) = −x² + x
Step 3: −x² ÷ x = −x. Multiply: −x(x − 2) = −x² + 2x. Subtract: (x + 2) − (2x) = −x + 2
Step 4: −x ÷ x = −1. Multiply: −1(x − 2) = −x + 2. Subtract: 0.

Quotient = x² − x − 1, Remainder = 0

Remainder Theorem

If p(x) is divided by (x − a), then the remainder is p(a).

Factor Theorem

If p(a) = 0, then (x − a) is a factor of p(x). Conversely, if (x − a) is a factor, then p(a) = 0.

Most Repeated Previous Year Questions

  1. Find the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial x² + 7x + 10 and verify the relationship between zeroes and coefficients. (3 Marks) — Answer: Zeroes: −5, −2. Sum = −7 = −b/a, Product = 10 = c/a.
  2. If α and β are zeroes of p(x) = x² + x − 1, find the value of 1/α + 1/β. (CBSE 2023) — Answer: 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = (−1)/(−1) = 1.
  3. Find a quadratic polynomial whose sum and product of zeroes are 3 and −2 respectively. (2 Marks) — Answer: x² − 3x − 2.
  4. Divide p(x) = 3x² − x³ − 3x + 5 by g(x) = x − 1 − x² and verify division algorithm. (CBSE 2020) — Answer: Quotient = x − 2, Remainder = 3.
  5. Find all zeroes of 2x⁴ − 3x³ − 3x² + 6x − 2, if two of its zeroes are √2 and −√2. (3 Marks) — Answer: Zeroes: √2, −√2, 1, 1/2.
  6. If one zero of the polynomial (a² − 9)x² + 13x + 6a is the reciprocal of the other, find the value of 'a'. (3 Marks) — Answer: Product = 1 ⇒ 6a/(a²−9) = 1 ⇒ a² − 6a − 9 = 0.
  7. Find the value of k such that x² − (k+6)x + 2(2k−1) has sum of zeroes equal to half of their product. (CBSE 2019) — Answer: k = 7.
  8. If α, β are zeroes of px² + qx + r, find the value of α³β + β³α. (2 Marks) — Answer: (r/p³)(q² − 2pr).
  9. On dividing x³ − 3x² + x + 2 by a polynomial g(x), the quotient and remainder were x − 2 and −2x + 4. Find g(x). (3 Marks) — Answer: g(x) = x² − x + 1.
  10. Find the number of zeroes of the polynomial from its graph given in the figure. (1 Mark) — Answer: Count the number of times the graph cuts/touches the x-axis.

Quick Revision — At a Glance

🎯 Key Formulas to Memorize:
  • Quadratic (ax² + bx + c): α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a
  • Cubic (ax³ + bx² + cx + d): α+β+γ = −b/a, αβ+βγ+γα = c/a, αβγ = −d/a
  • Forming Quadratic: x² − (sum)x + (product)
  • Division Algorithm: p(x) = g(x)·q(x) + r(x)
  • Remainder Theorem: Remainder = p(a) when divided by (x − a)
  • Factor Theorem: p(a) = 0 ⇒ (x − a) is a factor
🎯 Key Tips for Exam:
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes.
  • Graph of a linear polynomial is a straight line; quadratic is a parabola.
  • Quadratic graph can have 0, 1, or 2 real zeroes.
  • Always verify zeroes by substituting back into p(x) = 0.
  • For division, arrange polynomials in descending order of degree first.
  • If remainder is 0, the divisor is a factor of the dividend.
Did You Know? The word "polynomial" comes from the Greek words "poly" (many) and "nominal" (terms). The study of polynomial equations dates back to ancient Babylonians (2000 BCE), and the quadratic formula was first derived by Indian mathematician Brahmagupta in 628 CE!
