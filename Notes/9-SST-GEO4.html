Climate High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains India's monsoon type of climate, the factors that control it, the four seasons, and the mechanism of the Indian monsoon. Questions on factors affecting climate, monsoon characteristics, and seasonal patterns are frequently asked in periodic assessments [citation:2][citation:5].

Introduction to Climate

Climate refers to the average weather conditions of a large area over a long period of time, typically about 30 years. Weather, in contrast, is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time, including temperature, rainfall, and humidity [citation:5].

India's Climate: Monsoon Type

India has a monsoon type of climate. The word "monsoon" comes from the Arabic word "mausim", meaning season. It refers to the seasonal reversal of wind direction [citation:1][citation:8].

🎯 Key Point: Weather changes quickly (daily), while climate changes slowly over decades. India's climate is characterized by a distinct seasonal pattern, with regional variations in temperature and rainfall [citation:1][citation:5].

1. Factors Affecting India's Climate

Several factors control the climate of India [citation:6][citation:8]:

FactorHow It Affects Climate
LatitudeThe Tropic of Cancer passes through the middle of India. Areas south of it are tropical, while areas north are subtropical. This creates characteristics of both tropical and subtropical climates [citation:6].
AltitudeThe Himalayas in the north have an average height of about 6,000 metres. They prevent cold winds from Central Asia from entering India, resulting in milder winters compared to Central Asia [citation:6].
Pressure and WindsIn winter, high pressure exists north of the Himalayas, causing cold dry winds to blow towards the sea. In summer, low pressure develops over northwestern India, reversing wind direction and bringing the southwest monsoon [citation:6].
Distance from the SeaCoastal areas have an equable climate (moderate temperature range), while interior areas have extreme climate (very hot summers, very cold winters) [citation:8].
Ocean CurrentsWarm and cold currents affect coastal climate [citation:8].
Relief (Topography)Mountains act as barriers. Windward slopes receive heavy rainfall, while leeward slopes (rain shadow) remain dry [citation:8].

2. The Indian Monsoon

The monsoon is a seasonal reversal of winds. By early June, the low-pressure condition over the northern plains intensifies, attracting the trade winds from the southern hemisphere [citation:1].

Mechanism of the Monsoon

  • South-East Trade Winds: These originate over warm subtropical areas of the southern oceans, cross the equator, and blow in a south-westerly direction, entering India as the south-west monsoon [citation:1].
  • Wind Velocity: These winds are strong, blowing at an average velocity of 30 km per hour. They cover the country in about a month, except the extreme north-west [citation:1].

Key Features of the Monsoon

  • Burst of Monsoon: The sudden onset of rainfall when normal rainfall increases suddenly and continues for several days [citation:13].
  • Break in Monsoon: Monsoon rains occur only for a few days at a time, interspersed with rainless intervals. These breaks are related to the movement of the monsoon trough [citation:1].
  • Pulsating Nature: The monsoon is not continuous; it has wet and dry spells [citation:6].
  • Uncertainty: The monsoon is irregular in timing, strength, and duration, causing floods in some regions and droughts in others [citation:12].

📝 Role of the Monsoon Trough

When the axis of the monsoon trough lies over the plains, rainfall is good in those areas. When it shifts closer to the Himalayas, there are longer dry spells in the plains, and heavy rain occurs in the Himalayan catchment areas, causing floods [citation:1].

3. The Four Seasons of India

India experiences four main seasons [citation:1]:

The Cold Weather Season (Winter) — Mid-November to February

  • Temperature decreases from south to north. Days are warm, nights are cold. Frost is common in the north [citation:1].
  • Northeast Trade Winds prevail, blowing from land to sea, making most of the country dry [citation:1].
  • Western Disturbances from the Mediterranean bring winter rain to the plains (called mahawat) and snowfall to the mountains. This is important for rabi crops [citation:1].
  • The Tamil Nadu coast receives some rainfall from these winds [citation:1].

The Hot Weather Season (Summer) — March to May

  • Temperature rises, and low pressure develops in the northern part [citation:1].
  • Loo: Strong, gusty, hot, dry winds blow during the day over north and northwestern India [citation:1].
  • Kaal Baisakhi: Localized thunderstorms in West Bengal [citation:1].
  • Mango Showers: Pre-monsoon showers in Kerala and Karnataka, helpful for mango ripening [citation:1][citation:2].

Advancing Monsoon (Rainy Season) — June to September

  • The south-west monsoon brings widespread rainfall [citation:1].
  • Western Ghats windward side receives very heavy rainfall (over 250 cm) [citation:1].
  • Mawsynram in the Khasi Hills (Meghalaya) receives the highest average rainfall in the world [citation:1].
  • Rainfall in the Ganga valley decreases from east to west [citation:1].

Retreating Monsoon (Transition Season) — October to November

  • The monsoon weakens and withdraws from northern India [citation:12].
  • October Heat: Clear skies, rising temperature, and high humidity make the weather oppressive [citation:12].
  • Tropical Cyclones form over the Bay of Bengal and hit the eastern coast (Coromandel Coast), bringing winter rainfall to Tamil Nadu [citation:12].

4. Distribution of Rainfall

India shows strong spatial variation in rainfall [citation:12]:

RegionAnnual Rainfall
Western Ghats (windward side)Over 250 cm
Northeast India (Meghalaya)Over 400 cm (Mawsynram)
Western Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat, Haryana, PunjabLess than 60 cm
Deccan Plateau (rain shadow)Low rainfall
Leh (Jammu & Kashmir)Very low rainfall
🎯 Key Point: The Tamil Nadu coast receives most of its rainfall during October and November from the retreating monsoon and cyclones, unlike most of India which receives rain from June to September [citation:6][citation:12].

5. Monsoon as a Unifying Bond

The monsoon is considered a unifying bond for India [citation:9][citation:12]:

  • The Himalayas block cold Central Asian winds, keeping India warmer than other places at the same latitude [citation:12].
  • The Peninsular Plateau, surrounded by sea on three sides, has moderate temperatures due to maritime influence [citation:12].
  • Despite regional differences, the monsoon brings a shared weather pattern and a rhythmic cycle of seasons [citation:12].
  • It shapes India's landscape, vegetation, agriculture, festivals, and daily life [citation:12].
  • Rivers fed by monsoon rains connect different regions and support farming and livelihoods [citation:12].

Quick Revision - At a Glance

  • Climate: Average weather over 30 years. Weather: Short-term atmospheric condition.
  • Monsoon: Seasonal reversal of winds; from Arabic "mausim" (season).
  • Factors: Latitude, altitude, pressure/winds, distance from sea, ocean currents, relief.
  • Four Seasons: Winter (Nov-Feb), Summer (Mar-May), Advancing Monsoon (Jun-Sep), Retreating Monsoon (Oct-Nov).
  • Loo: Hot dry winds of summer. Kaal Baisakhi: Thunderstorms in West Bengal. Mango Showers: Pre-monsoon rain in Kerala/Karnataka.

  • October Heat: Oppressive weather during retreating monsoon.
  • Monsoon as unifying bond: Shared seasonal cycle, agriculture, festivals, and river systems.
