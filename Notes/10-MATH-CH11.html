Areas Related to Circles High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter extends your understanding of circles from Class 9 to calculate areas of sectors and segments. It combines geometry with algebra and is heavily tested in board exams with real-life application problems.

1.1 What is a Circle?

A circle is a collection of all points in a plane that are at a constant distance from a fixed point [citation:5].

  • The fixed point is called the centre (usually denoted by O).
  • The constant distance is called the radius (denoted by r).

1.2 Key Terms

TermDefinition
CircumferenceThe complete boundary or outer length of a circle [citation:15]
ChordA line segment joining any two points on a circle
DiameterThe longest chord, passing through the centre (d = 2r)
ArcA part of the circumference between two points [citation:15]
Minor ArcThe shorter arc between two points
Major ArcThe longer arc between two points

2. Circumference and Area of a Circle

2.1 The Value of Pi (π)

The value of π (pi) is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter [citation:5]:

π = Circumference / Diameter

Approximate values:

  • π = 22/7 (used when the radius is a multiple of 7) [citation:5]
  • π = 3.14 (used for general calculations) [citation:6]
  • π ≈ 3.1416 (more accurate value given by Aryabhatta) [citation:5]

2.2 Circumference of a Circle

The circumference is the perimeter (boundary length) of the circle [citation:5]:

C = 2πr = πd

Where r = radius and d = diameter.

2.3 Area of a Circle

The area is the space occupied within the circle's boundary [citation:5]:

A = πr²
Example 1: Find the circumference and area of a circle with radius 7 cm. (Use π = 22/7)

Solution:

Circumference = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm

Area = (22/7) × 7 × 7 = 154 cm²

Example 2: The circumference of a circle is 44 cm. Find its area. (Use π = 22/7)

Solution:

2πr = 44 → 2 × (22/7) × r = 44 → r = 7 cm

Area = πr² = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm²

3. Sector of a Circle

3.1 Definition

A sector is the portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and the corresponding arc [citation:5][citation:8]. It looks like a slice of pizza [citation:15].

  • Minor Sector: The smaller sector formed by two radii [citation:15]
  • Major Sector: The larger remaining sector [citation:15]
  • Angle of the Sector (θ): The central angle formed by the two radii [citation:15]

3.2 Area of a Sector

A sector is a fraction of the entire circle. The area is calculated using the unitary method [citation:1][citation:8]:

Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr²

Where θ is the central angle in degrees [citation:2][citation:6].

3.3 Length of an Arc

Similarly, the arc length is a fraction of the circumference [citation:1][citation:6]:

Length of arc = (θ/360°) × 2πr

3.4 Perimeter of a Sector

The perimeter includes the arc length plus the two radii [citation:6][citation:15]:

Perimeter of sector = (θ/360°) × 2πr + 2r
⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often forget to add the two radii when calculating the perimeter of a sector. The perimeter is NOT just the arc length — it includes the two straight sides [citation:15].
Example 3: Find the area of a sector of a circle with radius 4 cm and angle 30°. (Use π = 3.14) [citation:1]

Solution:

Area = (30°/360°) × 3.14 × 4²

= (1/12) × 3.14 × 16

= 4.19 cm² (approximately)

Example 4: Find the length of an arc of a circle with radius 21 cm that subtends an angle of 60° at the centre. (Use π = 22/7) [citation:6]

Solution:

Arc length = (60°/360°) × 2 × (22/7) × 21

= (1/6) × 132

= 22 cm

4. Segment of a Circle

4.1 Definition

A segment is the portion of a circle enclosed between a chord and the corresponding arc [citation:5][citation:8].

  • Minor Segment: The smaller region cut off by the chord [citation:15]
  • Major Segment: The larger region [citation:15]

4.2 Area of a Segment

A segment is obtained by removing a triangle from a sector [citation:2][citation:6]:

Area of segment = Area of sector − Area of triangle

Where the triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord.

Key Formula:
Area of segment = [(θ/360°) × πr²] − [Area of ΔOAB]

4.3 Finding Area of Triangle OAB

To find the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord:

  • If θ = 90°: Triangle is right-angled. Area = ½ × r × r = r²/2
  • If θ = 60°: Triangle is equilateral. Area = (√3/4) × r²
  • Otherwise: Draw a perpendicular from O to the chord and use trigonometry [citation:8]
Example 5: A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding minor segment. (Use π = 3.14) [citation:11][citation:12]

Solution:

Area of sector = (90°/360°) × 3.14 × 10² = 78.5 cm²

Area of triangle OAB = ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 cm²

Area of minor segment = 78.5 − 50 = 28.5 cm²

Example 6: A chord of a circle of radius 12 cm subtends an angle of 120° at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding segment. (Use π = 3.14 and √3 = 1.73) [citation:17]

Solution:

Area of sector = (120°/360°) × 3.14 × 12² = (1/3) × 3.14 × 144 = 150.72 cm²

Area of triangle OAB = ½ × 12 × 12 × sin 120° = ½ × 144 × (√3/2) = 62.28 cm²

Area of segment = 150.72 − 62.28 = 88.44 cm²

4.4 Major Segment

To find the major segment:

Area of major segment = Area of circle − Area of minor segment

Or equivalently:

Area of major segment = πr² − [Area of minor segment]

5. Special Cases and Applications

5.1 Semicircle

A semicircle is half of a circle (formed by a diameter) [citation:5][citation:15]:

Area of semicircle = πr² / 2
Perimeter of semicircle = πr + 2r

Note: The perimeter includes the curved part and the diameter [citation:15].

5.2 Quadrant

A quadrant is one-fourth of a circle (angle = 90°) [citation:5][citation:15]:

Area of quadrant = πr² / 4
Perimeter of quadrant = (πr/2) + 2r

5.3 Circular Ring (Annulus)

The area between two concentric circles with radii R and r (R > r) [citation:15]:

Area of ring = πR² − πr² = π(R² − r²)

5.4 Clock Problems

The minute hand of a clock sweeps:

  • 360° in 60 minutes
  • 6° per minute [citation:11]

So in t minutes, the angle swept = 6t degrees.

Example 7: Find the area swept by the minute hand of a clock of length 14 cm in 5 minutes. (Use π = 22/7) [citation:11]

Solution:

Angle swept in 5 minutes = 6 × 5 = 30°

Area = (30°/360°) × (22/7) × 14²

= (1/12) × (22/7) × 196

= 154/3 cm² ≈ 51.33 cm²

5.5 Cost/Application Problems

Example 8: A circular field has a circumference of 220 m. Find the cost of ploughing it at ₹0.50 per m². (Use π = 22/7)

Solution:

2πr = 220 → 2 × (22/7) × r = 220 → r = 35 m

Area = (22/7) × 35² = 3850 m²

Cost = 3850 × 0.50 = ₹1925 [citation:1]

6. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2024) The perimeter of a sector of a circle whose central angle is 90° and radius 7 cm is: (a) 35 cm (b) 11 cm (c) 22 cm (d) 25 cm [1 Mark] [citation:16]
  2. (CBSE 2023) What is the area of a semi-circle of diameter 'd'? (a) 1/16πd² (b) 1/4πd² (c) 1/8πd² (d) 1/2πd² [1 Mark] [citation:16]
  3. (CBSE 2022) The area swept by a 7 cm long minute hand of a clock in 10 minutes is: (a) 77 cm² (b) 154 cm² (c) 38.5 cm² (d) 25.67 cm² [1 Mark] [citation:16]
  4. (CBSE 2020) A piece of wire 22 cm long is bent into the form of an arc of a circle subtending an angle of 60° at its centre. Find the radius of the circle. (Use π = 22/7) [2 Marks] [citation:10]
  5. (NCERT) Find the area of a sector of a circle with radius 6 cm if the angle of the sector is 60°. (Use π = 22/7) [2 Marks] [citation:11]
  6. (NCERT) A chord of a circle of radius 15 cm subtends an angle of 60° at the centre. Find the areas of the corresponding minor and major segments. (Use π = 3.14 and √3 = 1.73) [3 Marks] [citation:17]
  7. (NCERT) A brooch is made with silver wire in the form of a circle with diameter 35 mm. The wire is also used in making 5 diameters which divide the circle into 10 equal sectors. Find: (i) the total length of the silver wire required, (ii) the area of each sector of the brooch. [3 Marks] [citation:17]
  8. (NCERT) A horse is tied to a peg at one corner of a square-shaped grass field of side 15 m by means of a 5 m long rope. Find the area of the part of the field in which the horse can graze. Also find the increase in grazing area if the rope were 10 m long instead of 5 m. (Use π = 3.14) [3 Marks] [citation:17]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Formulas:
  • Circumference: C = 2πr = πd
  • Area of Circle: A = πr²
  • Arc Length: l = (θ/360°) × 2πr
  • Area of Sector: (θ/360°) × πr²
  • Perimeter of Sector: (θ/360°) × 2πr + 2r
  • Area of Segment: Area of sector − Area of triangle
  • Major Segment: πr² − Area of minor segment
  • Semicircle: Area = πr²/2, Perimeter = πr + 2r
  • Quadrant: Area = πr²/4
  • Circular Ring: π(R² − r²)
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting to add 2r when calculating the perimeter of a sector [citation:15]
  • Confusing the segment with the sector — a segment does NOT include the centre [citation:15]
  • Forgetting to subtract the triangle area when finding a segment [citation:15]
  • Using the wrong value of π (22/7 vs 3.14)
  • Not squaring the radius when calculating area
