Diversity in Living Organisms High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Taxonomy and the classification hierarchy (Kingdom → Species) form the foundation of biology. Questions from the Five Kingdom system, plant and animal kingdoms are frequently asked in board exams.

1. Basis of Classification

Earth is home to millions of species — from microscopic bacteria to giant whales. To study such vast biodiversity, scientists group organisms based on their similarities and differences. This process is called classification.

Why Do We Classify?

  • To make the study of organisms systematic and organized.
  • To understand evolutionary relationships between organisms.
  • To identify, name, and study organisms easily.

Hierarchy of Classification

Organisms are grouped into a series of ranks, from the broadest to the most specific:

Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species

As we move from Kingdom to Species, the number of organisms decreases but the number of similar characteristics increases.

📌 Mnemonic to Remember Hierarchy:
King Philip Came Over For Good Soup
(Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species)

Basis of Classification

  • Cellular structure: Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic
  • Mode of nutrition: Autotrophic vs Heterotrophic
  • Body organization: Unicellular vs Multicellular
  • Presence of cell wall: Plant cells have cell wall; animal cells do not
  • Presence of nucleus: Prokaryotes lack a defined nucleus

2. Five Kingdom Classification (Whittaker, 1969)

R.H. Whittaker proposed the Five Kingdom Classification, which is widely accepted today. The five kingdoms are:

  • Monera — Prokaryotic, unicellular (Bacteria)
  • Protista — Eukaryotic, unicellular (Amoeba, Paramecium)
  • Fungi — Eukaryotic, multicellular, saprophytic (Yeast, Mushroom)
  • Plantae — Eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic (Algae, Ferns, Trees)
  • Animalia — Eukaryotic, multicellular, heterotrophic (Insects, Fish, Humans)
🔑 Criteria Used by Whittaker:
  • Cell structure (prokaryotic vs eukaryotic)
  • Body organization (unicellular vs multicellular)
  • Mode of nutrition (autotrophic vs heterotrophic)
  • Phylogenetic relationships (evolutionary history)

3. Plantae (Plant Kingdom)

Plants are multicellular, eukaryotic, autotrophic organisms with a cell wall made of cellulose. The plant kingdom is divided into five major groups:

A. Thallophyta (Algae)

  • Simplest plants — body is not differentiated into root, stem, and leaves.
  • Mostly aquatic.
  • Examples: Spirogyra, Ulothrix, Cladophora, Chara.

B. Bryophyta (Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom)

  • Called "amphibians of the plant kingdom" because they live in both water and land.
  • Have root-like, stem-like, and leaf-like structures.
  • No vascular tissue (no xylem/phloem).
  • Examples: Moss (Funaria), Liverworts, Marchantia.

C. Pteridophyta

  • First plants with true vascular tissue (xylem and phloem).
  • Have well-developed roots, stems, and leaves.
  • Reproduce by spores.
  • Examples: Ferns, Marsilea, Equisetum.

D. Gymnosperms (Naked Seeds)

  • Bear naked seeds — seeds are not enclosed in a fruit.
  • Mostly woody trees and shrubs.
  • Examples: Pine, Deodar, Cycas, Ginkgo.

E. Angiosperms (Covered Seeds)

  • Bear seeds enclosed within fruits.
  • Have flowers for reproduction.
  • Divided into Monocots and Dicots.
  • Examples: Mango, Wheat, Rose, Sunflower.
📌 Exam Tip: Remember the order: Thallophyta → Bryophyta → Pteridophyta → Gymnosperms → Angiosperms (from simple to complex).

4. Animalia (Animal Kingdom)

Animals are multicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms without a cell wall. They are broadly divided into two categories:

A. Non-Chordates (Invertebrates)

Animals without a notochord. They include the following phyla:

  • Porifera: Pore-bearing animals. Example: Sponges.
  • Coelenterata (Cnidaria): Have tentacles and stinging cells. Example: Hydra, Jellyfish.
  • Platyhelminthes: Flatworms. Example: Planaria, Tapeworm.
  • Nematoda: Roundworms. Example: Ascaris, Wuchereria.
  • Annelida: Segmented worms. Example: Earthworm, Leech.
  • Arthropoda: Largest phylum of the animal kingdom. Have jointed legs. Example: Insects, Spiders, Crabs.
  • Mollusca: Soft-bodied, usually with a shell. Example: Snail, Octopus.
  • Echinodermata: Spiny-skinned, marine. Example: Starfish, Sea urchin.

B. Chordates

Animals with a notochord (a stiff rod-like structure). Chordates are further divided into:

  • Pisces (Fish): Cold-blooded, aquatic, breathe through gills. Example: Rohu, Shark.
  • Amphibia: Live both in water and on land. Example: Frog, Salamander.
  • Reptilia: Cold-blooded, scaly skin. Example: Lizard, Snake, Crocodile.
  • Aves (Birds): Warm-blooded, have feathers, lay eggs. Example: Pigeon, Eagle.
  • Mammalia: Warm-blooded, have mammary glands, give birth to young ones. Example: Human, Whale, Bat.
🎯 Key Facts:
  • Arthropoda is the largest phylum (about 2/3 of all named species).
  • Mammalia is the most advanced class of animals.
  • All chordates are not vertebrates (e.g., Herdmania is a chordate but not a vertebrate).

5. Nomenclature (Naming of Organisms)

Every organism has a common name (like "mango") and a scientific name (like Mangifera indica). Common names vary from place to place, so scientists use a universal system called Binomial Nomenclature.

Rules of Binomial Nomenclature (Carl Linnaeus)

  • Each organism has a two-part name: Genus + species.
  • The Genus name starts with a capital letter.
  • The species name starts with a small letter.
  • When printed, the name is written in italics.
  • When handwritten, both words are underlined separately.
📝 Examples of Scientific Names:
  • Human → Homo sapiens
  • Mango → Mangifera indica
  • Housefly → Musca domestica
  • Pea → Pisum sativum

Most Repeated Previous Year Questions

  1. Which group of plants is called the amphibians of the plant kingdom and why? (2 Marks) — Answer: Bryophyta. They live in both water and land, hence called amphibians of the plant kingdom.
  2. Differentiate between Gymnosperms and Angiosperms. (3 Marks) — Answer: Gymnosperms bear naked seeds (e.g., Pine), while Angiosperms bear seeds enclosed in fruits (e.g., Mango).
  3. List the conventions followed while writing the scientific names of organisms. (2 Marks) — Answer: Genus starts with capital letter, species with small letter, printed in italics, handwritten underlined separately.
  4. Name the largest phylum of the animal kingdom and give two examples. (2 Marks) — Answer: Arthropoda; examples: Insects, Spiders.
  5. What is the basis of the Five Kingdom classification proposed by Whittaker? (3 Marks) — Answer: Cell structure, body organization, mode of nutrition, and phylogenetic relationships.
  6. Why are fungi placed in a separate kingdom and not in Plantae? (2 Marks) — Answer: Fungi are heterotrophic (saprophytic) and have a cell wall made of chitin, unlike plants which are autotrophic with cellulose cell walls.
  7. Write the scientific names of the following: (a) Human (b) Mango (c) Pea. (3 Marks) — Answer: (a) Homo sapiens (b) Mangifera indica (c) Pisum sativum.
  8. Differentiate between Monera and Protista. (2 Marks) — Answer: Monera are prokaryotic (no defined nucleus), while Protista are eukaryotic (have a defined nucleus).

Quick Revision — At a Glance

🎯 Key Classification Hierarchy:
  • Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
  • Five Kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia
  • Plantae Groups: Thallophyta → Bryophyta → Pteridophyta → Gymnosperms → Angiosperms
  • Animalia (Non-Chordates): Porifera, Coelenterata, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata
  • Animalia (Chordates): Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia
🎯 Key Tips for Exam:
  • Arthropoda is the largest phylum of the animal kingdom (jointed legs).
  • Bryophyta are called "amphibians of the plant kingdom."
  • Scientific name = Genus species (Genus capital, species small, italics).
  • Gymnosperms = naked seeds; Angiosperms = covered seeds.
  • Mammalia is the most advanced class of animals (mammary glands, warm-blooded).
  • Fungi have a cell wall made of chitin, not cellulose.
Did You Know? Carl Linnaeus is called the "Father of Taxonomy" and introduced the binomial nomenclature system in the 18th century.
