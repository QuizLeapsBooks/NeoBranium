The Fundamental Unit of Life High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Cell structure and organelles are highly tested. Differences between plant and animal cells are guaranteed exam questions.

1. Discovery of Cell

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. Its discovery marked a revolution in biology [citation:1].

ScientistYearDiscovery/Contribution
Robert Hooke1665Discovered cells in cork; coined the term "cell" [citation:1][citation:5]
Anton Van Leeuwenhoek1674First observed living cells in pond water [citation:1][citation:9]
Robert Brown1831Discovered the nucleus [citation:1][citation:9]
Purkinje1839Coined the term "protoplasm" [citation:1]
Schleiden and Schwann1838-1839Proposed the Cell Theory [citation:1][citation:9]
Rudolf Virchow1855Stated "Omnis cellula e cellula" — all cells arise from pre-existing cells [citation:1][citation:9]

Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

  • Unicellular: Made of a single cell. Examples: Amoeba, Paramecium [citation:1]
  • Multicellular: Made of many cells. Examples: Humans, plants, fungi [citation:1]

2. Cell Theory

Cell Theory (Schleiden & Schwann, expanded by Virchow):
  • All living organisms are composed of one or more cells [citation:12]
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living organisms [citation:12]
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells [citation:1][citation:4]

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

FeatureProkaryotic CellsEukaryotic Cells
NucleusAbsent (nucleoid region) [citation:1]Well-defined nucleus present [citation:1]
Membrane-bound organellesAbsent [citation:1]Present [citation:1]
ChromosomesSingle [citation:1]Multiple [citation:1]
ExamplesBacteria, blue-green algae [citation:1]Plant and animal cells [citation:1]

3. Basic Structure of the Cell

A cell consists of three main features [citation:1]:

  • Plasma Membrane: The outer covering
  • Nucleus: The control centre
  • Cytoplasm: The fluid inside the cell

4. Plasma Membrane / Cell Membrane

The plasma membrane is the outermost covering of the cell that separates its contents from the external environment. It is a selectively permeable membrane [citation:1][citation:3].

Structure

Made of lipids, proteins, and carbohydrates. The fluid mosaic model describes it as a dynamic structure with proteins embedded in a lipid bilayer [citation:12].

Functions

  • Separates cell contents from external environment [citation:9]
  • Allows selective entry and exit of substances [citation:1]
  • Provides shape to the cell [citation:9]
  • Enables endocytosis in Amoeba [citation:9]

Diffusion

The spontaneous movement of molecules from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. Example: O₂ and CO₂ move across the membrane by diffusion [citation:1][citation:3].

Osmosis

The movement of water molecules from a region of high water concentration to a region of low water concentration through a selectively permeable membrane [citation:1][citation:5].

Types of Solutions

Solution TypeWater Concentration OutsideEffect on Cell
HypotonicHigher than insideCell gains water, swells [citation:1][citation:12]
IsotonicSame as insideNo net movement [citation:1][citation:12]
HypertonicLower than insideCell loses water, shrinks [citation:1][citation:14]
📌 Plasmolysis: When a plant cell is placed in a hypertonic solution, water moves out, causing the cytoplasm to shrink away from the cell wall. This phenomenon is called plasmolysis [citation:3][citation:14].

5. Nucleus

The nucleus is the most important organelle — the control centre of the cell. It was discovered by Robert Brown in 1831 [citation:1][citation:9].

Structure

  • Nuclear Membrane: Double-layered membrane with pores for material exchange [citation:4]
  • Nucleoplasm: Dense fluid inside the nucleus [citation:4]
  • Chromatin Material: Network of thread-like structures made of DNA and proteins [citation:4]
  • Nucleolus: Round structure containing RNA and proteins; helps in ribosome formation [citation:1][citation:4]

Chromosomes

Chromatin condenses into rod-shaped chromosomes during cell division. They contain DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) which stores hereditary information [citation:4].

Functions

  • Controls all metabolic activities of the cell [citation:4]
  • Contains genetic information (DNA) [citation:4]
  • Directs cell division and protein synthesis [citation:4]
  • Transmits hereditary traits from one generation to another [citation:9]

6. Cytoplasm

The fluid-like substance present between the plasma membrane and the nucleus. It contains various cell organelles and molecules [citation:1].

Functions

  • Supports cell organelles and molecules [citation:1]
  • Helps in protein formation [citation:1]
  • Allows transportation of hormones [citation:1]
  • Dissolves cellular waste [citation:1]

7. Cell Organelles

Each organelle performs specific functions essential for the cell's survival [citation:12].

Mitochondria

  • Nickname: Powerhouse of the cell [citation:4][citation:12]
  • Function: Produces ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) — the energy currency of the cell [citation:4][citation:12]
  • Has its own DNA and ribosomes [citation:15]

Plastids

  • Present only in plant cells [citation:2]
  • Chloroplasts: Contain chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis; gives green colour [citation:4]
  • Chromoplasts: Contain pigments like carotenoids; give yellow, orange, red colours [citation:4]
  • Have their own DNA [citation:15]

Lysosomes

  • Nickname: Suicide bags of the cell [citation:4]
  • Function: Contain digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and worn-out organelles [citation:12]

Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

  • Rough ER (RER): Has ribosomes on surface; synthesizes proteins [citation:12]
  • Smooth ER (SER): No ribosomes; synthesizes lipids and detoxifies poisons [citation:12]

Golgi Apparatus

  • Function: Modifies, sorts, packages, and dispatches proteins and lipids [citation:12]
  • Involved in formation of lysosomes [citation:15]

Ribosomes

  • Function: Protein synthesis [citation:12]
  • Present in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells [citation:17]

Vacuoles

  • Plant cells: Large central vacuole (occupies up to 90% of cell volume); maintains turgidity [citation:6]
  • Animal cells: Small, numerous vacuoles [citation:6]

8. Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

FeaturePlant CellAnimal Cell
Cell WallPresent (made of cellulose) [citation:2][citation:3]Absent [citation:2]
Plastids/ChloroplastsPresent [citation:2]Absent [citation:2]
VacuoleLarge, single [citation:2][citation:6]Small, many [citation:2][citation:6]
CentrosomeAbsent [citation:10]Present [citation:10]
LysosomesRare/absent [citation:10]Present [citation:10]
ShapeRigid, regular [citation:6]Flexible, irregular [citation:6]
🎯 Key Point: The cell wall in plant cells is made of cellulose. It provides structural strength and allows plant cells to withstand hypotonic solutions without bursting [citation:3][citation:16].

Most Repeated Previous Year Questions

Very Short Answer (1 Mark)

  1. Who discovered the cell? [citation:8]
  2. What is the basic structural and functional unit of life? [citation:8]
  3. Name any two cell organelles found only in plant cells. [citation:8]
  4. What is the full form of ATP? [citation:4]

Short Answer (2-3 Marks)

  1. Why is the plasma membrane called a selectively permeable membrane? (2 Marks) [citation:4] Most Imp
  2. What would happen if the plasma membrane ruptures or breaks down? (2 Marks) [citation:8]
  3. Differentiate between rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum. (3 Marks) [citation:8]
  4. Differentiate between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. (3 Marks) [citation:8]
  5. What is osmosis? Give one example. (2 Marks) [citation:8]
  6. Name the cell organelle which is termed as the powerhouse of the cell. (1 Mark) [citation:4]

Long Answer (4-5 Marks)

  1. Compare plant and animal cells with at least five points. [citation:8] Most Imp
  2. Describe the structure of the nucleus and its functions. [citation:4]
  3. Explain the structure and functions of mitochondria. [citation:8]
  4. What are plastids? Name the types and write their functions. [citation:8]
  5. Draw a labelled diagram of a plant cell. [citation:8]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • Cell discovered by Robert Hooke (1665); nucleus by Robert Brown (1831)
  • Cell Theory: Schleiden & Schwann; expanded by Virchow [citation:1]
  • Plasma Membrane: Selectively permeable; controls entry/exit
  • Nucleus: Control centre; contains DNA (hereditary material)
  • Mitochondria: Powerhouse of the cell; produces ATP
  • Plastids: Only in plant cells; chloroplasts for photosynthesis
  • Lysosomes: Suicide bags; contain digestive enzymes
  • Plant cell: Cell wall (cellulose), large vacuole, plastids
  • Animal cell: No cell wall, small vacuoles, centrioles present
  • Plant cells have a cell wall made of cellulose; animal cells do not [citation:3]
  • Nucleus controls all activities of the cell [citation:9]
  • ATP = Adenosine Triphosphate (energy currency) [citation:4]
