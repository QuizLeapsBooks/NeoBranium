Chapter 1: Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

Introduction to the fundamental concepts of matter, moles, and stoichiometry.

Key Concepts

  • Law of Conservation of Mass: Matter can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • Law of Definite Proportions: A given compound always contains exactly the same proportion of elements by weight.
  • Law of Multiple Proportions: If two elements can combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element are in the ratio of small whole numbers.
  • Mole Concept: 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number). Mass of 1 mole is the molar mass (g/mol).

Concentration Terms

  • Molarity (M): Moles of solute / Volume of solution in L.
  • Molality (m): Moles of solute / Mass of solvent in kg.
  • Mole Fraction (x): Moles of component / Total moles.
  • Limiting Reagent: The reactant that is entirely consumed and limits the amount of product formed.

Chapter 2: Structure of Atom

Development of atomic models and quantum mechanics.

Key Concepts

  • Bohr's Model: Electrons revolve in discrete orbits. Angular momentum mvr = nh/2π. Energy E_n = -13.6/n² eV (for H atom).
  • Dual Nature of Matter: de Broglie equation λ = h/mv. Particles exhibit wave-like properties.
  • Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle: Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π. Impossible to determine exactly both position and momentum simultaneously.

📌 Quantum Numbers & Rules

  • n (Principal): Size/energy level (Shell).
  • l (Azimuthal): Shape of subshell (s=0, p=1, d=2, f=3).
  • m (Magnetic): Orientation of orbital.
  • s (Spin): Electron spin (+½ or -½).
  • Aufbau Principle: Fill lowest energy orbitals first.
  • Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons can have all 4 quantum numbers same (max 2 e⁻ per orbital).
  • Hund's Rule: Maximize parallel spins before pairing.

  • Isoelectronic Species: Ions/atoms with the same number of electrons. The one with a higher nuclear charge has a smaller radius.

Chapter 4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

How atoms combine to form molecules.

Key Concepts

  • Ionic Bond: Transfer of electrons. Favored by low IE of metal, high EA of non-metal, and high lattice energy.
  • Covalent Bond: Sharing of electrons (Lewis dot structures).
  • VSEPR Theory: Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion predicts shapes based on minimizing repulsion (lp-lp > lp-bp > bp-bp).
  • Hybridization: Mixing of atomic orbitals to form new hybrid orbitals (sp, sp², sp³, etc.).
  • Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT): Atomic orbitals combine to form Bonding (lower energy) and Antibonding (higher energy) MOs. Bond Order = ½ (N_b - N_a).
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Strong dipole-dipole interaction when H is bonded to highly electronegative F, O, or N.

Chapter 5: States of Matter

Properties of gases and liquids.

Gas Laws

  • Boyle's Law: P ∝ 1/V (at constant T, n)
  • Charles's Law: V ∝ T (at constant P, n)
  • Avogadro's Law: V ∝ n (at constant P, T)
  • Ideal Gas Equation: PV = nRT
  • Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures: P_total = p₁ + p₂ + ...
  • Graham's Law of Diffusion: Rate ∝ 1/√(Molar Mass)

Real Gases

Real gases deviate from ideal behavior at High Pressure and Low Temperature. Corrected by Van der Waals equation: (P + an²/V²)(V - nb) = nRT.

Chapter 6: Thermodynamics

Energy changes in chemical reactions.

Key Concepts

  • First Law: ΔU = q + w (Internal energy change = heat + work). Work done by gas w = -P_ext ΔV.
  • Enthalpy (H): Heat content at constant pressure. ΔH = ΔU + PΔV.
  • Hess's Law: Total enthalpy change is the same regardless of the path taken.
  • Entropy (S): Measure of randomness or disorder.
  • Gibbs Free Energy (G): Criteria for spontaneity. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. For a spontaneous process, ΔG < 0.

Chapter 7: Equilibrium

Dynamic state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

Key Concepts

  • Law of Mass Action: K_c = [Products] / [Reactants]. Relation: K_p = K_c (RT)^Δn.
  • Le Chatelier's Principle: If a stress is applied to a system at equilibrium, the system shifts to counteract the stress (concentration, pressure, temperature).
  • Acids & Bases: Arrhenius (H⁺/OH⁻), Brønsted-Lowry (Proton donor/acceptor), Lewis (Electron pair acceptor/donor).
  • pH Scale: pH = -log[H⁺]. pH + pOH = 14.
  • Buffer Solutions: Resist changes in pH upon addition of small amounts of acid/base.
  • Solubility Product (K_sp): Equilibrium constant for sparingly soluble salts.

Chapter 8: Redox Reactions

Electron transfer reactions.

Key Concepts

  • Oxidation: Loss of electrons / Increase in oxidation number.
  • Reduction: Gain of electrons / Decrease in oxidation number.
  • Oxidizing Agent: Gets reduced. Reducing Agent: Gets oxidized.
  • Balancing: Use Ion-electron method or Oxidation number method.
  • Galvanic Cells: Convert chemical energy to electrical energy. Anode (Oxidation), Cathode (Reduction).

Chapter 9: Hydrogen

Properties and compounds of hydrogen.

Key Concepts

  • Isotopes: Protium (₁H¹), Deuterium (₁H²), Tritium (₁H³ - radioactive).
  • Hardness of Water: Temporary (bicarbonates of Ca/Mg - removed by boiling). Permanent (chlorides/sulfates of Ca/Mg - removed by washing soda/ion exchange).
  • Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂): Non-planar structure. Acts as an oxidizing and reducing agent. Used for bleaching.

Chapter 10: The s-Block Elements

Group 1 (Alkali) and Group 2 (Alkaline Earth) metals.

Key Concepts

  • Alkali Metals (Group 1): ns¹. Highly reactive, stored under kerosene. React violently with water to form hydroxides and H₂. Flame tests: Li (crimson), Na (yellow), K (violet).
  • Alkaline Earth Metals (Group 2): ns². Less reactive than Group 1. Important compounds: Plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O), Cement.
  • Anomalous Behavior: Li and Be behave differently from rest of their groups due to small size and high charge/radius ratio. Diagonal relationships: Li-Mg, Be-Al.

Chapter 11: The p-Block Elements

Group 13 (Boron family) and Group 14 (Carbon family).

Key Concepts

  • Group 13: Boron is a non-metal. Diborane (B₂H₆) has banana bonds (3c-2e bonds). Aluminum forms amphoteric oxide.
  • Group 14: Carbon shows catenation (chain forming) due to high C-C bond enthalpy. Allotropes: Diamond (sp³, hard, insulator), Graphite (sp², soft, conductor), Fullerenes.
  • Inert Pair Effect: Reluctance of ns² electrons to participate in bonding down the group, making lower oxidation states more stable for heavy elements (e.g., Pb²⁺ is more stable than Pb⁴⁺).

Chapter 12: Organic Chemistry — Some Basic Principles

Foundation of organic reactions and nomenclature.

Key Concepts

  • IUPAC Nomenclature: Prefix + Word Root + Primary Suffix (ane/ene/yne) + Secondary Suffix (functional group).
  • Isomerism: Structural (chain, position, functional, metamerism). Stereoisomerism (geometrical, optical).
  • Electronic Effects:
    • Inductive Effect (I): Permanent shifting of sigma electrons along a carbon chain.
    • Electromeric Effect (E): Temporary shift of pi electrons in presence of a reagent.
    • Resonance (R): Delocalization of pi electrons.
    • Hyperconjugation: Sigma-pi conjugation (no-bond resonance). Stabilizes carbocations.
  • Reaction Intermediates: Carbocations (stability 3°>2°>1°), Carbanions (1°>2°>3°), Free radicals (3°>2°>1°).

Chapter 13: Hydrocarbons

Alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic compounds.

Key Reactions

  • Alkanes: Undergo free radical substitution (Halogenation). Wurtz reaction: 2RX + 2Na → R-R + 2NaX.
  • Alkenes: Undergo electrophilic addition. Markovnikov's Rule: Negative part of addendum goes to carbon with fewer hydrogens. Anti-Markovnikov: HBr with peroxide.
  • Alkynes: Terminal alkynes are acidic. Undergo addition reactions.
  • Benzene: Aromaticity (Hückel's Rule: 4n+2 pi electrons). Undergoes Electrophilic Substitution (Nitration, Halogenation, Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation).

Chapter 14: Environmental Chemistry

Chemical phenomena in the environment.

Key Concepts

  • Smog: Classical smog (smoke+fog+SO₂, reducing). Photochemical smog (NOₓ+hydrocarbons+sunlight, oxidizing, contains PAN and ozone).
  • Acid Rain: pH < 5.6. Caused by SO₂ and NO₂.
  • Ozone Depletion: CFCs break down in stratosphere releasing Cl free radicals which destroy ozone layer.
  • Global Warming: Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs) trap Earth's infrared radiation.
  • Green Chemistry: Designing processes to reduce or eliminate the generation of hazardous substances.
