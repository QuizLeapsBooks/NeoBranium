The Rise of Nationalism in Europe High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains how nationalism emerged in 19th-century Europe and transformed the political map of the continent. It covers the French Revolution, liberalism, conservatism, and the unification of Germany and Italy. Board exams frequently test key concepts, personalities, and the process of nation-state formation.

1.1 Frédéric Sorrieu's Vision (1848)

Frédéric Sorrieu, a French artist, prepared a series of four prints in 1848 visualizing his dream of a world made up of "democratic and social Republics".

In the first print, he showed the peoples of Europe and America marching in a long train to pay homage to the statue of Liberty. The female figure of Liberty holds the torch of Enlightenment in one hand and the Charter of the Rights of Man in the other.

💡 Key Point: The French Revolution (1789) was the first clear expression of nationalism. It introduced the idea that sovereignty belongs to the people, not to the monarch.

2. The French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation

2.1 Steps Taken by French Revolutionaries

The French revolutionaries took several steps to create a sense of collective identity among the French people:

StepDescription
La Patrie and Le CitoyenNew ideas of fatherland and citizen emphasized a unified community with equal rights
Tricolour FlagThe royal standard was replaced by the new French flag
National AssemblyEstates General was elected and renamed National Assembly
Centralized AdministrationUniform laws for all citizens within its territory
Uniform Weights and MeasuresInternal customs duties were abolished
French LanguageRegional dialects were discouraged; French became the common language
🎯 Key Point: The revolutionaries declared that it was the mission of the French nation to liberate the peoples of Europe from despotism.

3. The Napoleonic Code (Civil Code of 1804)

Napoleon Bonaparte had destroyed democracy in France by establishing monarchy, but he introduced several revolutionary principles in administration to make the system more rational and efficient.

3.1 Key Features of the Civil Code of 1804

  • Abolished all privileges based on birth
  • Established equality before the law
  • Secured the right to property
  • Simplified administrative divisions
  • Abolished the feudal system and freed peasants from serfdom
  • Removed guild restrictions in towns
  • Improved transport and communication systems

3.2 Impact Outside France

In regions under French control like the Dutch Republic, Switzerland, Italy, and Germany, Napoleon implemented these reforms. Peasants, artisans, workers, and new businessmen enjoyed a new-found freedom.

🎯 Napoleon's Legacy:
  • Administrative reforms made the system more rational and efficient
  • Uniform laws, standardized weights and measures, and a common national currency facilitated trade
  • However, French taxation and censorship generated resistance

4. The Making of Nationalism in Europe

4.1 The Habsburg Empire

The Habsburg Empire was a multinational dynastic empire that included:

  • German-speaking people in Austria and Bohemia
  • Italian-speaking people in Lombardy and Venetia
  • Magyars in Hungary
  • Polish-speaking aristocrats in Galicia
  • Bohemians, Slovaks, Slovenes, Croats, and Romanians

These groups did not share a common identity. Their main connection was loyalty to the Habsburg emperor.

4.2 The Aristocracy and the New Middle Class

ClassCharacteristics
Landed AristocracyDominant social and political class; owned large rural estates and urban houses; used French in diplomacy; small part of the population
PeasantsMajority of the population
Working ClassEmerged due to industrialization; included artisans, industrialists, businessmen, and professionals

5. Liberalism and Its Ideas

5.1 What is Liberalism?

The term liberalism is derived from the Latin word 'liber', meaning free. For the new middle classes, liberalism stood for freedom for the individual and equality of all before the law.

5.2 Political Ideas of Liberalism

  • End of autocracy and clerical privileges
  • Government by consent through a parliament
  • Constitution and representative government
  • Freedom of the press and freedom of association
  • Abolition of aristocratic and clerical privileges

5.3 Economic Ideas of Liberalism

  • Freedom of markets
  • Abolition of state-imposed restrictions on movement of goods and capital
  • Inviolability of private property

5.4 The Zollverein (1834)

In 1834, a customs union called Zollverein was formed at the initiative of Prussia and joined by most German states.

  • Abolished tariff barriers
  • Reduced the number of currencies from over thirty to two
  • Created a network of railways that stimulated mobility
🎯 Zollverein: The German customs union that prepared the ground for German unification by creating economic unity without political federation.

5.5 Liberals and Women

The status of women during liberal movements was controversial. They did not have the same rights as men, even though they participated in large numbers.

  • Women formed their own political associations
  • Brought out journals and newspapers
  • Participated in political demonstrations and meetings
  • However, they were denied suffrage rights during the Frankfurt Parliament election
  • They were seated as mere observers in the visitors' gallery

6. A New Conservatism After 1815

6.1 What is Conservatism?

Conservatism is a political philosophy that stresses the importance of tradition, established institutions, and customs, and prefers gradual development to quick change.

Conservatives believed in:

  • Preserving monarchy
  • Preserving the Church
  • Social hierarchy
  • Property
  • Family and traditional institutions

6.2 The Congress of Vienna (1815)

After Napoleon's defeat, the major European powers — Britain, Russia, Prussia, and Austria — met at Vienna to restore the conservative order. The meeting was hosted by Austrian Chancellor Duke Metternich.

Main Decisions of the Congress of Vienna:

  • The Bourbon dynasty was restored in France
  • France lost the territories annexed under Napoleon
  • The Kingdom of the Netherlands was created to the north of France
  • Genoa was added to Piedmont in the south
  • Prussia received territories along France's western frontier
  • Austria gained control of northern Italy
  • Russia received part of Poland
  • The German Confederation of 39 states was left untouched
🎯 Key Point: The Congress of Vienna was held to prevent French expansion and restore monarchies. However, it could not stop the spread of nationalist and liberal ideas.

7. The Age of Revolutions (1830-1848)

7.1 July Revolution (1830) in France

The Bourbon kings were overthrown and a constitutional monarchy was established.

7.2 Greek War of Independence (1821-1832)

  • Greece had been part of the Ottoman Empire since the 15th century
  • Greek nationalists started their independence war in 1821
  • Poets and artists rallied public opinion, hailing Greece as the birthplace of European civilization
  • Greece's independence was acknowledged by the Treaty of Constantinople in 1832

7.3 Romanticism and Culture

Romanticism was a cultural movement that sought to develop nationalist sentiment:

  • Focused on emotions, intuition, and mystical feelings
  • Criticized the glorification of reason and science
  • Folk songs, folk poetry, and folk dances contributed to the true spirit of the nation (volksgeist)
  • Language played an important role in developing nationalist sentiments

7.4 The Revolution of 1848

In 1848, a revolution led by the educated middle classes emerged across Europe:

  • Events of February 1848 in France ended the monarchy and proclaimed a republic based on universal male suffrage
  • Liberal middle classes in Germany, Italy, Poland, and the Austro-Hungarian Empire demanded nation-states on parliamentary principles
  • The Frankfurt Parliament was convened on May 18, 1848
  • 831 elected representatives gathered at St. Paul Church
  • The parliament was dissolved on May 31, 1849 due to lack of support

7.5 Silesian Weavers' Uprising (1845)

Weavers in Silesia revolted against contractors who paid them less money for their work. The uprising highlighted the economic hardships faced by workers during industrialization.

8. Unification of Germany

8.1 Background

  • Nationalist feelings became popular among middle-class Germans
  • In 1848, they tried to unite different regions of the German Confederation into a nation-state governed by an elected parliament
  • The monarchy and large landowners (Junkers) of Prussia suppressed this liberal initiative

8.2 Role of Otto von Bismarck

Otto von Bismarck, the chief minister of Prussia, was the architect of German unification.

  • Used the Prussian army and bureaucracy to achieve unification
  • Fought three wars over seven years — with Denmark, Austria, and France
  • Prussian victories completed the process of unification

8.3 Proclamation of the German Empire

On 18 January 1871, King William I of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor at the Palace of Versailles.

🎯 Key Points:
  • Germany was unified under Prussian leadership
  • Bismarck used "blood and iron" policy — wars and military strength
  • Kaiser William I became the Emperor of unified Germany

9. Unification of Italy

9.1 Key Personalities

PersonalityRole
Giuseppe MazziniBorn in Genoa (1807); member of the secret society Carbonari; established Young Italy in Marseilles and Young Europe in Berne; main aim was to unify Italy into a republic
Count Camillo de CavourChief minister of Sardinia-Piedmont under King Victor Emmanuel II; led the campaign to bring Italy together; not a democratic or revolutionary; formed a political coalition that defeated Austrian armies in 1859
Giuseppe GaribaldiMet Mazzini in 1833; joined Young Italy; participated in a republican uprising in Piedmont (1834); fled to South America; in 1860 led the famous Expedition of the Thousand to South Italy; volunteers were called Red Shirts; in 1867 led an army to Rome to fight the Papal States
Victor Emmanuel IIKing of unified Italy (proclaimed in 1861)

9.2 Process of Unification

  • Mazzini's Young Italy movement failed to achieve unification
  • Cavour's diplomacy and alliance with France helped defeat Austria in 1859
  • Garibaldi's Red Shirts captured South Italy in 1860
  • In 1870, during the war with Prussia, France withdrew its troops from Rome, and the Papal States were finally joined to Italy
🎯 Key Points:
  • Italy was unified through the contributions of Mazzini, Cavour, Garibaldi, and Victor Emmanuel II
  • Victor Emmanuel II became the new king of unified Italy in 1861

10. Britain and the Balkans

10.1 Britain

  • The English Parliament seized power from the monarchy
  • The Act of Union (1707) between England and Scotland resulted in the formation of the United Kingdom of Great Britain
  • Ireland was incorporated in 1801 after a failed revolution
  • The British nation was facilitated through the mass propagation of English culture

10.2 The Balkans

The Balkans included modern-day Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Romania, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Albania, Serbia, Macedonia, Montenegro, and Slovenia.

  • This area was made explosive by Romantic nationalism
  • Major European powers wanted to have control over this area
  • Rival nationalist claims and competition among European powers contributed to the tensions that led to the First World War

  • Giuseppe Garibaldi: Led Red Shirts, Expedition of the Thousand

  • Victor Emmanuel II: King of unified Italy (1861)

  • Conservatism: Preserving tradition, monarchy, Church
  • Zollverein: German customs union (1834)
  • Volksgeist: True spirit of the nation
  • Junkers: Large landowners of Prussia
  • Red Shirts: Garibaldi's volunteers

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Confusing Mazzini (Young Italy, failed) with Cavour (diplomacy, succeeded) and Garibaldi (Red Shirts, South Italy)
  • Forgetting that Zollverein was an economic union, not a political one
  • Mixing up the year of German unification (1871) with Italian unification (1861)
  • Not mentioning Junkers when discussing German unification
  • Forgetting that women were denied suffrage in the Frankfurt Parliament
  • Confusing Liberalism (freedom, equality) with Conservatism (tradition, monarchy)
