Circles High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Circles is a fundamental geometry chapter that deals with tangents and their properties. It teaches you to prove important theorems and solve problems involving tangents from external points, which are frequently asked in board exams.

1.1 What is a Circle?

A circle is a collection of all points in a plane which are at a constant distance from a fixed point.

  • The fixed point is called the centre of the circle.
  • The constant distance from the centre is called the radius of the circle.

1.2 Circle and Line in a Plane

When a line and a circle are drawn in a plane, there can be three possibilities:

CaseCommon PointsName
1No common pointNon-intersecting line
2One common pointTangent
3Two common pointsSecant

2. Key Terms Related to Circles

2.1 Radius

The distance from the centre to any point on the circle is called the radius.

2.2 Chord

A line segment joining any two points on a circle is called a chord.

2.3 Diameter

A chord passing through the centre of the circle is called the diameter. It is the longest chord of the circle.

Note: Diameter = 2 × Radius.

2.4 Secant

A straight line that cuts the circle at two points is called a secant.

2.5 Tangent

A line that meets the circle at exactly one point is called a tangent.

  • The common point of the tangent and the circle is called the point of contact.
  • A tangent is a special case of a secant when the two endpoints of the corresponding chord coincide.
🎯 Key Point: A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. This point is called the point of contact.

3. Tangent to a Circle

3.1 Important Properties

  • There is only one tangent at a point of the circle.
  • The tangent is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
  • The line containing the radius through the point of contact is sometimes called the normal to the circle at the point.

3.2 Real-Life Example

When a bicycle wheel moves along a straight line on the ground, the wheel represents a circle and the ground line is a tangent to the circle. The radius through the point of contact appears to be at right angles to the tangent.

4. Important Theorems

Theorem 10.1: The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

4.1 Proof of Theorem 10.1

Given: A circle with centre O and a tangent XY at point P.

To Prove: OP ⊥ XY

Proof:

  • Take a point Q on XY other than P and join OQ.
  • Point Q must lie outside the circle (if it were inside, XY would become a secant).
  • Therefore, OQ > OP (since OP is the radius).
  • This is true for every point on line XY except point P.
  • So, OP is the shortest distance from O to line XY.
  • Hence, OP is perpendicular to XY.
Theorem 10.2: The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

4.2 Proof of Theorem 10.2

Given: A circle with centre O, an external point P, and two tangents PQ and PR from P.

To Prove: PQ = PR

Proof:

  • Join OP, OQ, and OR.
  • ∠OQP = ∠ORP = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent at point of contact).
  • In right triangles OQP and ORP:
  • OQ = OR (radii of the same circle)
  • OP = OP (common side)
  • Therefore, ΔOQP ≅ ΔORP (RHS congruency rule).
  • Hence, PQ = PR (CPCT).

4.3 Important Result from Theorem 10.2

If two tangents are drawn to a circle from an external point, then:

  • They subtend equal angles at the centre: ∠POQ = ∠POR.
  • They are equally inclined to the segment joining the centre to the external point: ∠OPQ = ∠OPR.

4.4 Solved Example (NCERT)

Example: Two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. Prove that ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ.

Solution:

Let ∠PTQ = θ.

By Theorem 10.2, TP = TQ, so ΔTPQ is isosceles.

Therefore, ∠TPQ = ∠TQP = (1/2)(180° − θ) = 90° − θ/2

Also, ∠OPT = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent at point of contact).

So, ∠OPQ = ∠OPT − ∠TPQ = 90° − (90° − θ/2) = θ/2

Therefore, ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ. Hence proved.

5. Number of Tangents from a Point on a Circle

The number of tangents that can be drawn to a circle from a given point depends on the position of the point:

Position of PointNumber of TangentsExplanation
Inside the circle0Any line through an interior point is a secant (intersects at two points).
On the circle1There is exactly one tangent at any point on the circle.
Outside the circle2Exactly two tangents can be drawn from an external point.

5.1 Solved Example: Finding Radius

Example: The length of a tangent from a point 15 cm away from the centre of a circle is 12 cm. Find the radius of the circle.

Solution:

Let O be the centre, P be the external point (OP = 15 cm), and PT be the tangent (PT = 12 cm).

By Theorem 10.1, OT ⊥ PT, so ΔOTP is right-angled at T.

Using Pythagoras theorem:

OP² = OT² + PT²

15² = r² + 12²

225 = r² + 144

r² = 81 → r = 9 cm

Radius of the circle = 9 cm

5.2 Solved Example: Finding Tangent Length

Example: Find the length of the tangent from a point 25 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 7 cm.

Solution:

OP = 25 cm, r = 7 cm

PT = √(OP² − r²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 cm

Tangent length = 24 cm

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting that the radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
  • Confusing the point of contact with the external point.
  • Not using the RHS congruency rule correctly when proving tangent lengths are equal.
  • Assuming tangents can be drawn from an interior point.

6. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2022) Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal. [3 Marks]
  2. (CBSE 2022) In the given figure, PQ is a chord of length 8 cm of a circle of radius 5 cm and centre O. The tangents at P and Q intersect at point T. Find the length of TP. [3 Marks]
  3. (CBSE 2022) In the figure, two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. Prove that ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ. [3 Marks]
  4. (CBSE 2022) Prove that a parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus. [3 Marks]
  5. (CBSE 2020) In the given figure, O is the centre of the circle and TP is the tangent to the circle from an external point T. If ∠PBT = 30°, prove that BA : AT = 2 : 1. [3 Marks]
  6. (CBSE 2014) Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact. [2 Marks]
  7. (NCERT) A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at a point Q so that OQ = 12 cm. Length PQ is: (A) 12 cm (B) 13 cm (C) 8.5 cm (D) √119 cm. [1 Mark]
  8. (NCERT) If tangents PA and PB from a point P to a circle with centre O are inclined to each other at an angle of 80°, then ∠POA is equal to: (A) 50° (B) 60° (C) 70° (D) 80°. [1 Mark]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

  • Theorem 10.2: Lengths of tangents from an external point are equal.
🎯 Number of Tangents:
  • Inside point: 0 tangents
  • On the circle: 1 tangent
  • Outside point: 2 tangents

  • Tangents from an external point subtend equal angles at the centre.
  • They are equally inclined to the segment joining the centre to the external point.
  • Tangent length = √(OP² − r²) where OP is distance from centre to external point.
