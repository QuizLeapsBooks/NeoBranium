Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts High Scoring Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering properties, pH scale, neutralization, important salts (baking soda, washing soda, plaster of Paris, bleaching powder), and the chlor-alkali process. Includes most repeated board exam questions.

Why this chapter is important? Every year 3-5 questions appear — definition of pH, uses of important salts, neutralization in daily life, and chlor-alkali process.

1. Properties of Acids and Bases

🧪 Acids

  • Sour taste
  • Turn blue litmus → Red
  • Release H+ ions in water
  • Examples: HCl, H2SO4, CH3COOH

🧴 Bases

  • Bitter taste, soapy touch
  • Turn red litmus → Blue
  • Release OH- ions in water
  • Examples: NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)2
📌 Important Concept: Acids and bases conduct electricity because they produce ions in aqueous solution. Dry HCl gas does NOT change litmus paper — water is essential for ionization.

3. pH Scale – Measure of Acidity/Basicity

pH scale ranges from 0 to 14

  • pH < 7: Acidic solution
  • pH = 7: Neutral (Pure water)
  • pH > 7: Basic solution
🧠 Important pH Values to Remember:
Stomach acid: ~1.5-3.5  |  Lemon juice: ~2  |  Tomato: ~4  |  Pure water: 7  |  Blood: ~7.4  |  Milk of magnesia: ~10  |  NaOH solution: ~13-14

Importance of pH in daily life:
📌 Plants grow best at pH 6.5-7.5
📌 Tooth decay starts when mouth pH falls below 5.5
📌 Stomach produces HCl (pH ~1-3) for digestion; antacids neutralize excess acid.

4. Neutralization Reaction

When an acid reacts with a base, salt and water are formed. This is called neutralization.

General Equation: Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat (exothermic)
Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

Real-life Applications:

  • Antacids (Mg(OH)2) neutralize excess stomach acid.
  • Bee sting (acidic) treated with baking soda (base).
  • Soil treatment: If soil is too acidic, farmers add slaked lime (Ca(OH)2).

5. Important Salts – Formulas, Preparation & Uses Board Special

🧂 Common Salt (Sodium Chloride) – NaCl

Sea water, rock salt. Raw material for many chemicals.

🥄 Baking Soda (Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate) – NaHCO3

Preparation: NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 → NaHCO3 + NH4Cl

Uses: Antacid, baking powder (produces CO2 on heating), fire extinguisher.

🔥 Heating effect: 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2

🧼 Washing Soda (Sodium Carbonate Decahydrate) – Na2CO3.10H2O

Preparation: Na2CO3 + 10H2O → Na2CO3.10H2O

Uses: Cleaning agent, water softening, glass and soap manufacturing.

⚪ Plaster of Paris (POP) – CaSO4.½H2O (Calcium Sulphate Hemihydrate)

Preparation: CaSO4.2H2O (Gypsum) heated at 373K → CaSO4.½H2O + 1½H2O

Uses: Plaster for fractured bones, sculptures, decorative materials.

⚠️ Important: Plaster of Paris should be stored in moisture-proof containers because it absorbs water and hardens into Gypsum.

🧴 Bleaching Powder – CaOCl2 (Calcium Oxychloride)

Preparation: Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 → CaOCl2 + H2O

Uses: Bleaching cotton/linen, disinfecting drinking water, oxidizing agent.

6. Chlor-Alkali Process

Electrolysis of aqueous sodium chloride (brine) produces three important products:

Reaction: 2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)
✅ Chlorine gas (Cl2) – used in bleaching powder, PVC
✅ Hydrogen gas (H2) – fuel, margarine
✅ Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) – soaps, paper, detergents

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. Why does dry HCl gas not change the color of dry litmus paper? (Answer: H⁺ ions are produced only when HCl dissolves in water. Dry litmus has no water for ionization.)
  2. What is the common name of CaSO4.½H2O? Why should it be stored in moisture-proof containers? (Answer: Plaster of Paris; because it absorbs moisture and hardens.)
  3. Fresh milk has pH 6. How does pH change as it turns into curd? Why? (Answer: pH decreases (becomes more acidic) due to formation of lactic acid by bacteria.)
  4. Write the chemical name and formula of baking soda. Give two important uses. (Answer: Sodium hydrogen carbonate NaHCO3; uses: antacid, baking powder.)
  5. What is the chlor-alkali process? Write the balanced equation and name the products. (Answer: Electrolysis of brine → NaOH, Cl2, H2)
