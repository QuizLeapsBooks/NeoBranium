Physical Features of India High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? India displays great physical variation—mountains, plains, deserts, plateaus, and islands. This chapter is the foundation for understanding India's climate, drainage, vegetation, and agriculture. Questions on the Himalayan ranges, Northern Plains divisions, and Peninsular Plateau are frequently asked in exams [citation:3][citation:6].

Introduction

India has all the major physical features of the earth: mountains, plains, deserts, plateaus, and islands [citation:3]. Geologically, the Peninsular Plateau is one of the ancient landmasses on the earth's surface, while the Himalayas and the Northern Plains are the most recent landforms [citation:3].

Major Physiographic Divisions

The physical features of India are grouped under the following physiographic divisions [citation:3][citation:6]:

  • The Himalayan Mountains
  • The Northern Plains
  • The Peninsular Plateau
  • The Indian Desert
  • The Coastal Plains
  • The Islands

1. The Himalayan Mountains

The Himalayas are young fold mountains stretching over the northern borders of India. They run in a west-east direction from the Indus to the Brahmaputra, covering a distance of about 2,400 km [citation:3][citation:7].

Three Parallel Ranges (North to South)

RangeKey Features
Himadri (Great/Inner Himalayas)Northernmost and most continuous range. Average height 6,000 metres. Composed of granite. Contains loftiest peaks [citation:3][citation:7].
Himachal (Lesser Himalayas)Most rugged mountain system. Pir Panjal is the longest and most important range. Famous for hill stations [citation:3][citation:10].
Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas)Outermost range, composed of unconsolidated sediments. Width varies from 10-50 km [citation:3].

Duns

The longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalayas and the Shiwaliks are known as Duns. Examples: Dehradun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun [citation:3][citation:13].

Regional Divisions (West to East)

  • Punjab Himalaya: Between Indus and Satluj
  • Kumaon Himalaya: Between Satluj and Kali
  • Nepal Himalaya: Between Kali and Teesta
  • Assam Himalaya: Between Teesta and Dihang [citation:3][citation:7]

Purvachal (Eastern Hills)

Beyond the Dihang gorge, the Himalayas bend sharply to the south along the eastern boundary of India. This region is known as Purvachal. It comprises the Patkai Hills, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, and Mizo Hills [citation:3][citation:5].

🎯 Key Point: The Himalayas are young fold mountains and form an unstable zone geologically. The Peninsular Plateau, in contrast, is one of the most stable land blocks [citation:12].

2. The Northern Plains

The Northern Plains have been formed by the interplay of three major river systems: the Indus, the Ganga, and the Brahmaputra along with their tributaries. The deposition of alluvium over millions of years formed this fertile plain [citation:3][citation:5].

Key Facts

  • Area: About 7 lakh sq. km
  • Length: About 2,400 km
  • Breadth: About 240 to 320 km
  • It is a densely populated physiographic division [citation:5][citation:8]

Three Sections

  • Punjab Plains: Western part, formed by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj). Dominated by doabs [citation:3][citation:5].
  • Ganga Plains: Extends between Ghaggar and Teesta rivers. Spread over Haryana, Delhi, U.P., Bihar, and parts of Jharkhand and West Bengal [citation:3].
  • Brahmaputra Plains: Located in Assam [citation:3].

Four Relief Divisions (North to South)

DivisionDescription
BhabarA narrow belt of 8-16 km width parallel to the Shiwaliks. Rivers deposit pebbles here, and all streams disappear [citation:3][citation:5].
TeraiA wet, swampy, and marshy region south of Bhabar where streams re-emerge. Once thickly forested with wildlife [citation:3][citation:5].
BhangarThe largest part, formed of older alluvium. Lies above floodplains, terrace-like. Contains calcareous deposits called Kankar [citation:3][citation:5].
KhadarNewer, younger deposits of floodplains. Renewed almost every year, very fertile, ideal for intensive agriculture [citation:3][citation:5].
🎯 Key Point: The Northern Plains are agriculturally the most productive part of India due to rich alluvial soil, adequate water supply, and favourable climate [citation:6][citation:8].

3. The Peninsular Plateau

The Peninsular Plateau is a tableland composed of old crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks. It was formed due to the breaking and drifting of the Gondwana land, making it one of the oldest landmasses [citation:2][citation:3][citation:5].

Two Broad Divisions

Central Highlands

  • Lies north of the Narmada River, covering a major area of the Malwa Plateau [citation:2][citation:5].
  • Bounded by the Aravalli Range on the northwest and the Vindhya Range on the south [citation:2].
  • Eastward extensions: Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand [citation:2][citation:5].
  • The Chotanagpur Plateau is the further eastern extension, drained by the Damodar River [citation:2][citation:8].

Deccan Plateau

  • A triangular landmass lying south of the Narmada River [citation:5].
  • Higher in the west and slopes gently eastwards [citation:2].
  • The Western Ghats form its western edge (continuous, higher) and the Eastern Ghats form its eastern edge (discontinuous, lower) [citation:2].
  • The western part is covered by Deccan Traps (basaltic lava flows) which weather to form rich black soil [citation:2].
  • Northeastern extensions: Meghalaya, Karbi-Anglong Plateau, and North Cachar Hills [citation:5].

Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats

FeatureWestern GhatsEastern Ghats
ContinuityContinuousDiscontinuous

4. The Indian Desert

The Indian Desert lies towards the western margins of the Aravalli Hills [citation:7][citation:15].

  • It is an undulating sandy plain covered with sand dunes [citation:7].
  • Receives very low rainfall: below 150 mm per year [citation:8][citation:15].
  • Crescent-shaped dunes are called Barchans [citation:4][citation:15].
  • The Luni is the only large river in this region [citation:8][citation:15].
  • Climate is arid with low vegetation cover [citation:7].

5. The Coastal Plains

The Peninsular Plateau is flanked by narrow coastal strips along the Arabian Sea on the west and the Bay of Bengal on the east [citation:7].

Western Coastal Plains

  • Konkan: Northern part (Mumbai to Goa)
  • Kannad Plain: Central stretch
  • Malabar Coast: Southern stretch [citation:7][citation:15]

Eastern Coastal Plains

  • Northern Circar: Northern part
  • Coromandel Coast: Southern part [citation:7][citation:15]
  • Wider and low-lying, with large river deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) [citation:2].

6. The Islands

India has two main island groups [citation:2][citation:7]:

Lakshadweep Islands

  • Located in the Arabian Sea, close to the Malabar Coast of Kerala [citation:7][citation:12].
  • Composed of coral islands [citation:1][citation:12].
  • Earlier known as Laccadive, Minicoy, and Amindive; renamed in 1973 [citation:1][citation:12].
  • Total area: about 32 sq. km [citation:2][citation:12].
  • Kavaratti is the administrative headquarters [citation:2][citation:12].
  • Pitti Island (uninhabited) has a bird sanctuary [citation:2][citation:12].

Andaman and Nicobar Islands

  • Located in the Bay of Bengal, extending from north to south [citation:7][citation:12].
  • Bigger in size and more numerous [citation:12].
  • The Andamans are in the north and the Nicobars in the south [citation:7].
  • Rich in tropical flora and fauna [citation:2].

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • Six physiographic divisions: Himalayas, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands.
  • Himalayas: Three ranges—Himadri (6,000m), Himachal, Shiwaliks. Duns lie between Himachal and Shiwaliks.
  • Northern Plains: Formed by Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra. Four relief divisions—Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, Khadar.
  • Peninsular Plateau: Oldest landmass, part of Gondwana. Two divisions—Central Highlands and Deccan Plateau.
  • Western Ghats: Continuous, higher (Anai Mudi 2,695m). Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous, lower (Mahendragiri 1,501m).
  • Indian Desert: West of Aravallis. Luni is the only river. Barchans are crescent dunes.
  • Lakshadweep: Coral islands, Arabian Sea, Kavaratti HQ. Andaman & Nicobar: Bay of Bengal.
