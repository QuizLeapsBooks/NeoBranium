Sectors of the Indian Economy High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains how the Indian economy is divided into three sectors — primary, secondary, and tertiary. It teaches you about GDP, employment patterns, organised vs unorganised sectors, and the role of the public and private sectors. Board exams frequently test sector classification, GDP contribution, and employment issues.

1.1 What are Economic Sectors?

Economic activities are classified into different sectors based on the nature of work involved. These sectors are interdependent and together form the economy of a country.

1.2 The Three Sectors

SectorAlso CalledNature of Work
PrimaryAgriculture and related sectorNatural products are produced by exploiting natural resources
SecondaryIndustrial sectorNatural products are changed into other forms through manufacturing
TertiaryService sectorServices that support primary and secondary sectors
🎯 Easy Way to Remember:
  • Primary: "From Nature" — farming, fishing, mining
  • Secondary: "From Factory" — manufacturing, construction
  • Tertiary: "From Service" — banking, transport, teaching

2. Primary Sector

2.1 Definition

The primary sector includes activities where natural products are produced by exploiting natural resources. It is also called the agriculture and related sector.

2.2 Examples of Primary Sector Activities

  • Agriculture (farming of crops)
  • Animal husbandry (rearing of animals)
  • Fishing
  • Forestry
  • Mining and quarrying
  • Dairy farming

2.3 Why is it Called Primary?

It is called the primary sector because it forms the base for all other economic activities. Without raw materials from this sector, the secondary and tertiary sectors cannot function.

💡 Example: When we produce cotton, it is an agricultural activity. When we turn cotton into cloth, it becomes a manufacturing activity. Cotton comes from the primary sector.

3. Secondary Sector

3.1 Definition

The secondary sector includes activities where natural products are changed into other forms through manufacturing. It is also called the industrial sector.

3.2 Examples of Secondary Sector Activities

  • Manufacturing of cloth from cotton
  • Making sugar from sugarcane
  • Manufacturing of steel from iron ore
  • Production of cars, machines, and tools
  • Construction of buildings and roads
  • Baking bread from wheat

3.3 Why is it Called Secondary?

It is called the secondary sector because it depends on the primary sector for raw materials. It adds value to natural products and creates finished goods.

💡 Example: Sugarcane is grown in the primary sector. When it is processed in a factory to make sugar, it becomes part of the secondary sector.

4. Tertiary Sector

4.1 Definition

The tertiary sector includes activities that help in the development of the primary and secondary sectors. It is also called the service sector.

4.2 Examples of Tertiary Sector Activities

  • Transport (trucks, trains, ships, aeroplanes)
  • Storage and warehousing
  • Communication (post, telephone, internet)
  • Banking and financial services
  • Insurance
  • Trade (buying and selling)
  • Education and teaching
  • Healthcare (doctors, nurses)
  • Government services (police, administration)
  • Tourism and hospitality

4.3 Why is it Called Tertiary?

It is called the tertiary sector because it supports both primary and secondary sectors. These activities do not produce goods themselves but provide services.

💡 Example: A farmer needs a loan to buy seeds — the bank (tertiary) helps. A factory needs trucks to transport goods — the transport service (tertiary) helps.

5. Interdependence of Sectors

All three sectors are interdependent. They cannot function independently.

SectorDepends OnExample
PrimarySecondary and TertiaryFarmer needs fertilisers (secondary) and transport (tertiary) to sell crops
SecondaryPrimary and TertiaryFactory needs raw materials (primary) and banking (tertiary)
TertiaryPrimary and SecondaryTransport sector needs goods to move (primary and secondary)
Example of Interdependence — Cotton to Cloth:
  1. Farmer grows cotton (Primary)
  2. Cotton is transported to factory (Tertiary)
  3. Factory converts cotton into cloth (Secondary)
  4. Cloth is transported to shops (Tertiary)
  5. Shopkeeper sells cloth to consumers (Tertiary)

6. GDP and Sectoral Contribution

6.1 What is GDP?

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year.

GDP = Value of all final goods and services produced in a country in one year

6.2 Historical Change in Sectoral Contribution

PeriodPrimary SectorSecondary SectorTertiary Sector
Initial Years (1950s)HighestSecondLowest
Present DayLowestSecondHighest

6.3 Reasons for Growth of Tertiary Sector

  • Increase in demand for services like education, healthcare, banking, and communication
  • Development of transport and communication
  • Growth of IT and software services
  • Rise in income levels leading to demand for more services
  • Role of government in providing public services
🎯 Key Point: The tertiary sector has become the largest contributor to India's GDP. But employment is still highest in the primary sector — this is called disguised unemployment.

7. Employment and Underemployment

7.1 Employment Pattern

In India, the primary sector (especially agriculture) employs the largest number of people, but its contribution to GDP is the lowest. This is a major problem in the Indian economy.

7.2 Disguised Unemployment

Disguised unemployment (also called underemployment) occurs when more people are working on a job than required. Even if some workers are removed, the total output does not decrease.

💡 Example: A farm needs only 4 workers, but 7 family members work on it. If 3 people stop working, the output remains the same. These 3 are disguisedly unemployed.

7.3 Causes of Underemployment

  • Population growth
  • Limited job opportunities in secondary and tertiary sectors
  • Dependence on agriculture
  • Lack of skill development
  • Slow industrial growth

7.4 How to Reduce Underemployment?

  • Provide loans for small industries
  • Develop infrastructure like dams, roads, and markets
  • Promote education and skill development
  • Encourage industrial growth in rural areas
  • Provide alternative employment opportunities

8. Organised vs Unorganised Sector

FeatureOrganised SectorUnorganised Sector
RegistrationRegistered by the governmentNot registered
RulesFollows government rules and regulationsOutside government control
Job SecurityRegular employment and securityNo job security
Working HoursFixed hours, overtime paidIrregular hours, no overtime
BenefitsPaid leave, provident fund, gratuityNo paid leave or benefits
ExamplesGovernment jobs, registered factoriesSmall farmers, street vendors, domestic workers

8.1 Why Do Workers in Unorganised Sector Face Problems?

  • Low wages and exploitation
  • No job security — can be fired anytime
  • Long working hours with no overtime
  • No health or maternity benefits
  • No pension or retirement benefits
  • Poor working conditions

8.2 How to Protect Unorganised Sector Workers?

  • Government should provide social security
  • Minimum wages should be enforced
  • Workers should be registered
  • Health and safety standards should be implemented
  • Credit facilities should be provided

9. Public vs Private Sector

FeaturePublic SectorPrivate Sector
OwnershipOwned by governmentOwned by individuals or companies
MotivePublic welfare, not profitProfit maximisation
ExamplesRailways, post offices, government schoolsTata, Reliance, private hospitals
ServicesEssential services like water, electricity, transportGoods and services for profit

9.1 Role of Public Sector

  • Provides essential services at affordable rates
  • Develops infrastructure like roads, dams, and bridges
  • Ensures equal distribution of resources
  • Provides employment to many people
  • Supports sectors where private companies do not invest due to low profit

9.2 Role of Private Sector

  • Creates jobs and employment
  • Produces goods and services efficiently
  • Contributes to GDP growth
  • Encourages innovation and competition
  • Provides better quality services in some cases
🎯 Key Point: Both sectors are important. The public sector ensures welfare and equality, while the private sector drives growth and efficiency.

  6. (2017) "Tertiary sector is different from other sectors." Justify the statement with suitable examples. [5 Marks]
  7. (NCERT) Why is the tertiary sector becoming so important in India? Give reasons. [3 Marks]
  8. (NCERT) How can more employment be created in rural areas? [3 Marks]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Primary Sector: Natural products — farming, mining, fishing
  • Secondary Sector: Manufacturing — cloth from cotton, sugar from sugarcane
  • Tertiary Sector: Services — banking, transport, education
  • GDP: Value of all final goods and services produced in a year
  • Disguised Unemployment: More workers than needed, no change in output if removed
  • Organised Sector: Registered, follows rules, provides benefits
  • Unorganised Sector: Not registered, no job security or benefits
🎯 Key Comparisons:
  • GDP Contribution: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
  • Employment Share: Primary > Tertiary > Secondary
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Confusing GDP contribution with employment contribution — they are very different
  • Thinking tertiary sector produces goods — it only provides services
  • Forgetting that primary sector still employs the largest number of people
  • Not giving examples when asked about interdependence of sectors
