Coordinate Geometry High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? Coordinate Geometry bridges algebra and geometry. It teaches you to locate points on a plane, find distances, divide line segments, and calculate areas using coordinates. Board exams frequently test the distance formula, section formula, and area of triangle with multi-step problems.

1.1 What is Coordinate Geometry?

Coordinate Geometry (also called Analytical Geometry) is the branch of mathematics that uses a coordinate system to study geometric figures and their relationships algebraically [citation:3]. It allows us to represent geometric shapes and their properties using equations and coordinates.

1.2 The Cartesian Coordinate System

The Cartesian plane is formed by two perpendicular number lines that intersect at a point called the origin (denoted by O) [citation:1][citation:5].

  • x-axis: The horizontal number line.
  • y-axis: The vertical number line.
  • Origin: The point of intersection, with coordinates (0, 0).

1.3 Coordinates of a Point

Any point on the plane is represented as an ordered pair P(x, y), where [citation:1][citation:9]:

  • Abscissa (x-coordinate): The perpendicular distance of the point from the y-axis.
  • Ordinate (y-coordinate): The perpendicular distance of the point from the x-axis.
Point P(x, y)
Abscissa = x (distance from y-axis)
Ordinate = y (distance from x-axis)

1.4 The Four Quadrants

The axes divide the plane into four regions called quadrants [citation:5]:

QuadrantSign of xSign of yExample
I++(3, 4)
II−+(−3, 4)
III−−(−3, −4)
IV+−(3, −4)
🎯 Memory Trick: Starting from Quadrant I and moving anti-clockwise: (+,+), (−,+), (−,−), (+,−).

1.5 Points on the Axes

  • Points on x-axis: Of the form (x, 0) — y-coordinate is always zero [citation:5][citation:11].
  • Points on y-axis: Of the form (0, y) — x-coordinate is always zero [citation:5][citation:11].
  • Origin: (0, 0).
⚠️ Common Mistake: A point on the x-axis does not have an x-coordinate of zero. Its y-coordinate is zero because its vertical distance from the x-axis is zero [citation:5].

1.6 Distance from the Axes

For a point P(x, y) [citation:5]:

  • Distance from the x-axis = |y| (absolute value of ordinate).
  • Distance from the y-axis = |x| (absolute value of abscissa).

Example: For P(−6, 4), distance from x-axis = |4| = 4 units, distance from y-axis = |−6| = 6 units.

1.7 Reflection of a Point

  • Reflection across the x-axis: (x, y) → (x, −y) [citation:5]
  • Reflection across the y-axis: (x, y) → (−x, y) [citation:5]
  • Reflection across both axes: (x, y) → (−x, −y) [citation:5]

2. Distance Formula

The distance formula calculates the straight-line distance between two points on the Cartesian plane [citation:1][citation:3].

Distance between P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

2.1 Derivation (Using Pythagoras Theorem)

Draw horizontal and vertical lines through the two points to form a right-angled triangle. The horizontal difference and vertical difference are the two perpendicular sides, and the distance is the hypotenuse [citation:3][citation:9].

Derivation: In ΔPTQ (right-angled at T):
PQ² = PT² + QT²
= (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²
PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

2.2 Distance from the Origin

The distance of a point P(x, y) from the origin O(0, 0) is [citation:1][citation:10]:

OP = √(x² + y²)

2.3 Solved Examples

Example 1: Find the distance between P(2, 6) and Q(8, 3). [citation:1]

Solution:

PQ = √[(8 − 2)² + (3 − 6)²] = √[6² + (−3)²] = √(36 + 9) = √45 = 3√5 units

Example 2: Find the distance between A(1, 2) and B(4, 6). [citation:3]

Solution:

AB = √[(4 − 1)² + (6 − 2)²] = √[3² + 4²] = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 units

Example 3: Find the distance between A(−2, 3) and B(4, −5). [citation:5]

Solution:

AB = √[(4 − (−2))² + (−5 − 3)²] = √[6² + (−8)²] = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 units

2.4 Distance Between Points on the Same Axis

  • If both points are on the x-axis: distance = |x₂ − x₁|.
  • If both points are on the y-axis: distance = |y₂ − y₁| [citation:17].
🎯 Key Point: The subtraction order does not matter because differences are squared: (x₂ − x₁)² = (x₁ − x₂)² [citation:5].

3. Section Formula

The section formula finds the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment in a given ratio [citation:1][citation:3].

3.1 Internal Division

If point P(x, y) divides the line segment joining A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio m : n, then [citation:7][citation:10]:

x = (m·x₂ + n·x₁) / (m + n)
y = (m·y₂ + n·y₁) / (m + n)
Example: Find the coordinates of the point dividing the line segment joining A(1, 2) and B(4, 6) in the ratio 2 : 3. [citation:3]

Solution:

x = (2×4 + 3×1) / (2 + 3) = (8 + 3) / 5 = 11/5

y = (2×6 + 3×2) / (2 + 3) = (12 + 6) / 5 = 18/5

Required point: (11/5, 18/5)

3.2 Finding Ratio When Point is Given

To find the ratio k : 1 in which a point P divides AB [citation:9]:

  1. Assume the ratio is k : 1.
  2. Substitute into the section formula for x-coordinate (or y-coordinate).
  3. Solve for k.
Example: Find the ratio in which the point (2, −5) divides the line segment joining A(−3, 5) and B(4, −9). [citation:9]

Solution:

Let ratio be k : 1. Using x-coordinate:

2 = (4k − 3) / (k + 1)

2k + 2 = 4k − 3 → 2k = 5 → k = 5/2

Ratio = 5 : 2

🎯 Board Exam Tip: When a point lies on the x-axis, its y-coordinate is 0. When it lies on the y-axis, its x-coordinate is 0 [citation:1].

4. Midpoint Formula

The midpoint is the point that divides a line segment exactly in half (ratio 1 : 1) [citation:1][citation:3].

Midpoint M(x, y) = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
Example: Find the midpoint of the segment joining A(2, 4) and B(6, 8). [citation:9]

Solution:

x = (2 + 6)/2 = 4

y = (4 + 8)/2 = 6

Midpoint = (4, 6)

4.1 Centroid of a Triangle

The centroid (O) of a triangle with vertices A(x₁, y₁), B(x₂, y₂), and C(x₃, y₃) is [citation:10][citation:11]:

Centroid = ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3)

5. Area of a Triangle

The area of a triangle with vertices A(x₁, y₁), B(x₂, y₂), and C(x₃, y₃) is given by [citation:7][citation:11]:

Area = (1/2) |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|

Important: The absolute value ensures the area is always positive. The expression inside the modulus can be negative depending on the order of vertices [citation:11].

5.1 Solved Example

Example: Find the area of the triangle with vertices P(−5, 7), Q(−4, −5), and R(4, 5). [citation:4]

Solution:

Area = (1/2) |(−5)(−5 − 5) + (−4)(5 − 7) + 4(7 − (−5))|

= (1/2) |(−5)(−10) + (−4)(−2) + 4(12)|

= (1/2) |50 + 8 + 48|

= (1/2) × 106 = 53 square units

5.2 Area of a Quadrilateral

To find the area of a quadrilateral, divide it into two triangles by drawing a diagonal, then add the areas of the two triangles [citation:7].

6. Collinearity of Points

Three points are collinear if they lie on the same straight line.

Condition: Points A(x₁, y₁), B(x₂, y₂), and C(x₃, y₃) are collinear if and only if the area of the triangle formed by them is zero [citation:7][citation:11].
x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂) = 0

6.1 Alternative Method (Distance Formula)

Three points A, B, C are collinear if the sum of two distances equals the third distance [citation:6]:

  • AB + BC = AC, or
  • AB + AC = BC, or
  • AC + BC = AB

6.2 Solved Example

Example: Check whether points A(3, 1), B(6, 4), and C(8, 6) are collinear. [citation:6]

Solution:

AB = √[(6−3)² + (4−1)²] = √(9+9) = √18 = 3√2

BC = √[(8−6)² + (6−4)²] = √(4+4) = √8 = 2√2

AC = √[(8−3)² + (6−1)²] = √(25+25) = √50 = 5√2

Since AB + BC = 3√2 + 2√2 = 5√2 = AC, the points are collinear.

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2024) Find the distance between points (a, b) and (−a, −b). [1 Mark]
  2. (CBSE 2023) Find the ratio in which the y-axis divides the line segment joining the points (5, −6) and (−1, −4). [2 Marks]
  3. (CBSE 2020) Find the coordinates of a point A, where AB is a diameter of a circle with centre (−2, 2) and B is the point with coordinates (3, 4). [2 Marks] [citation:8]
  4. (CBSE 2019) Find the ratio in which the segment joining the points (1, −3) and (4, 5) is divided by the x-axis. Also find the coordinates of this point on the x-axis. [3 Marks] [citation:8]
  5. (CBSE 2018) The line segment joining the points A(2, 1) and B(5, −8) is trisected at the points P and Q such that P is nearer to A. If P also lies on the line given by 2x − y + k = 0, find the value of k. [3 Marks] [citation:4]
  6. (CBSE 2018) Find the area of triangle PQR formed by the points P(−5, 7), Q(−4, −5), and R(4, 5). [2 Marks] [citation:4]
  7. (CBSE 2014) If the point A(0, 2) is equidistant from the points B(3, p) and C(p, 5), find p. Also find the length of AB. [3 Marks] [citation:6]
  8. (NCERT) Find the point on the y-axis which is equidistant from A(2, 4) and B(4, 8). [2 Marks] [citation:1]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Formulas:
  • Distance Formula: PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
  • Distance from Origin: OP = √(x² + y²)
  • Section Formula (Internal): x = (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), y = (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)
  • Midpoint Formula: M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
  • Centroid: ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3)
  • Area of Triangle: (1/2)|x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|
  • Collinearity Condition: Area of triangle = 0
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Forgetting the absolute value in the area formula.
  • Mixing up the section formula order (m·x₂ + n·x₁, not m·x₁ + n·x₂).
  • Assuming a point on the x-axis has x-coordinate 0 (it has y-coordinate 0).
  • Not using the distance formula correctly for points in different quadrants.
