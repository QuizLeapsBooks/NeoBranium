Chapter 1: Nature and Significance of Management

Meaning of Management

Definition
Management is the process of getting things done with the aim of achieving goals effectively and efficiently.
  • Effectiveness: Completing the task on time (doing the right task).
  • Efficiency: Completing the task with minimum cost/resources (doing the task right).

Characteristics of Management

Goal-oriented process, all-pervasive, multidimensional (work, people, operations), continuous process, group activity, dynamic function, intangible force.

Levels of Management

  • Top Level: Board of Directors, CEO. Determine objectives and policies.
  • Middle Level: Departmental Managers. Interpret policies and coordinate between top and lower levels.
  • Lower/Supervisory Level: Supervisors, Foremen. Oversee actual workforce, ensure quality and discipline.

Coordination — The Essence of Management

Coordination is the force that binds all other functions of management. It ensures unity of action towards common goals.

Chapter 2: Principles of Management

Meaning

Principles of management are broad and general guidelines for decision making and managerial behavior.

Fayol's 14 Principles of Management (Administrative Management)

  1. Division of Work: Specialization increases efficiency.
  2. Authority and Responsibility: Should be balanced.
  3. Discipline: Obedience to organizational rules.
  4. Unity of Command: One subordinate should receive orders from one boss only.
  5. Unity of Direction: One head and one plan for a group having similar objectives.
  6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest.
  7. Remuneration of Employees: Fair and equitable.
  8. Centralisation and Decentralisation.
  9. Scalar Chain: Formal line of authority. (Gang Plank: shortcut in emergencies).
  10. Order: A place for everything and everything in its place.
  11. Equity: Fair treatment to all.
  12. Stability of Personnel: No frequent transfers.
  13. Initiative: Encourage employees to develop plans.
  14. Esprit De Corps: Team spirit.

Taylor's Scientific Management

  • Science, not rule of thumb.
  • Harmony, not discord.
  • Cooperation, not individualism.
  • Development of each person to his/her greatest efficiency.
Techniques by Taylor
Functional Foremanship, Standardisation & Simplification, Method Study, Motion Study, Time Study, Fatigue Study, Differential Piece Wage System.

Chapter 3: Business Environment

Meaning

Definition
Business environment refers to the sum total of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside the control of a business enterprise but may affect its performance.

Dimensions of Business Environment

  • Economic Environment: Interest rates, inflation, stock market indices, value of rupee.
  • Social Environment: Customs, traditions, values, social trends, expectations of society.
  • Technological Environment: Scientific improvements, new methods of production.
  • Political Environment: Political stability, peace, attitude of the elected government.
  • Legal Environment: Legislations passed by the government, court judgments.

Demonetisation (2016)

Withdrawal of legal tender status of ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes to curb corruption, black money, and counterfeit currency, and to promote digital transactions.

Chapter 4: Planning

Meaning

Planning is deciding in advance what to do and how to do it. It bridges the gap from where we are to where we want to go.

Importance

Provides direction, reduces risks of uncertainty, reduces overlapping/wasteful activities, promotes innovative ideas, facilitates decision making, establishes standards for controlling.

Limitations

Leads to rigidity, may not work in a dynamic environment, reduces creativity, involves huge costs, time-consuming, does not guarantee success.

Types of Plans

  • Standing Plans: Used repeatedly. (Policy, Procedure, Method, Rule).
  • Single-Use Plans: For non-recurring situations. (Programme, Budget).
  • Objectives and Strategy are neither standing nor single-use plans.

Chapter 5: Organising

Meaning

Organising is the process of identifying and grouping work, defining and delegating responsibility and authority, and establishing relationships.

Organisation Structure

  • Functional Structure: Grouping of jobs of similar nature under functions (e.g., Production, Marketing, HR). Best for single-product companies.
  • Divisional Structure: Grouping jobs related to a particular product line or territory. Best for multi-product companies.

Delegation

Transfer of authority from superior to subordinate. Elements: Authority (power to command), Responsibility (obligation to perform), Accountability (answerability for final outcome - cannot be delegated).

Decentralisation

Systematic delegation of authority through all levels of management. Delegation is compulsory, decentralisation is an optional policy decision.

Chapter 6: Staffing

Meaning

Staffing means finding the right people for the right job with the right qualifications.

Elements of Staffing

  1. Recruitment: The process of stimulating people to apply for jobs. Can be Internal (Transfers, Promotions) or External (Direct recruitment, Advertisement, Employment Exchange, Campus recruitment).
  2. Selection: Choosing the best candidate from a pool of applicants. (Negative process because more rejected than selected).
  3. Training and Development: Equipping employees with skills (training) and overall growth (development).
    • On-the-job: Apprenticeship, Internship.
    • Off-the-job: Vestibule training.

Chapter 7: Directing

Meaning

Directing refers to the process of instructing, guiding, counseling, motivating and leading people in the organization to achieve its objectives.

Elements of Directing

  • Supervision: Overseeing subordinates at work.
  • Motivation: Stimulating people to action. (Maslow's Need Hierarchy: Physiological > Safety > Social > Esteem > Self-Actualization).
  • Leadership: Influencing behavior of people. Styles: Autocratic (directive), Democratic (participative), Laissez-Faire (free-rein).
  • Communication: Exchange of ideas. Can be Formal or Informal (Grapevine). Barriers to communication: Semantic, Psychological, Organizational, Personal.

Chapter 8: Controlling

Meaning

Controlling means ensuring that activities in an organization are performed as per the plans. Planning without controlling is meaningless, and controlling without planning is blind.

Steps in Controlling Process

  1. Setting performance standards.
  2. Measurement of actual performance.
  3. Comparing actual performance with standards.
  4. Analyzing deviations:
    • Critical Point Control (CPC): Focus on Key Result Areas (KRAs).
    • Management by Exception (MBE): If you try to control everything, you may end up controlling nothing. Focus only on significant deviations.
  5. Taking corrective action.

Chapter 9: Financial Management

Meaning

Financial management is concerned with optimal procurement as well as usage of finance.

Financial Decisions

  • Investment Decision: How the firm's funds are invested in different assets (Capital Budgeting for long-term, Working Capital for short-term).
  • Financing Decision: Quantum of finance to be raised from various long-term sources (Debt vs. Equity mix, i.e., Capital Structure). Debt is cheaper (tax deductible interest) but riskier.
  • Dividend Decision: How much of the profit to distribute as dividends and how much to retain for future growth (Retained Earnings).

Financial Planning

Preparation of a financial blueprint of an organization's future operations. Objectives: Ensure availability of funds whenever required; see that firm does not raise resources unnecessarily.

Chapter 10: Financial Markets

Meaning

A market for the creation and exchange of financial assets. It allocates savings from households (savers) to business firms (investors).

Money Market

Market for short-term funds (maturity up to 1 year). Instruments: Treasury Bill (T-Bill), Commercial Paper (CP), Call Money, Certificate of Deposit (CD), Commercial Bill.

Capital Market

Market for medium and long-term funds.

  • Primary Market: New Issue Market. Securities are sold for the first time. Methods: Public issue, Offer for sale, Private placement, Rights issue, e-IPOs.
  • Secondary Market: Stock Exchange. Market for existing/second-hand securities.

SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)

The regulatory body for the securities market in India. Functions: Protective (prevent fraud), Regulatory (rules/guidelines), Developmental (training, research).

Chapter 11: Marketing

Meaning

Marketing is a social process by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating, offering, and freely exchanging products and services of value.

Marketing Mix (The 4 Ps)

  • Product Mix: Branding, Packaging (Primary, Secondary, Transportation), Labeling.
  • Price Mix: Factors affecting price (Cost, Demand, Competition, Govt regulations).
  • Place (Physical Distribution) Mix: Channels of distribution (Zero-level, One-level, Two-level), physical movement (Transportation, Warehousing, Inventory).
  • Promotion Mix: Advertising (paid, impersonal), Personal Selling (face-to-face), Sales Promotion (short-term incentives like discounts/coupons), Public Relations (managing public image).

Chapter 12: Consumer Protection

Meaning

Protecting the consumers against unfair trade practices, adulteration, misleading advertisements, etc.

Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Provides a mechanism for speedy resolution of consumer disputes.

Rights of a Consumer

  1. Right to Safety (against hazardous goods).
  2. Right to be Informed (about quality, quantity, price, expiry).
  3. Right to Choose (access to variety of goods at competitive prices).
  4. Right to be Heard (to file a complaint).
  5. Right to seek Redressal (compensation against unfair practices).
  6. Right to Consumer Education (to acquire knowledge to be an informed consumer).

Redressal Agencies

  • District Commission: Claims up to ₹1 Crore.
  • State Commission: Claims from ₹1 Crore to ₹10 Crore.
  • National Commission: Claims above ₹10 Crore.
