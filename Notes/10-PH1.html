Light – Reflection and Refraction High Weightage Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering laws of reflection, spherical mirrors, mirror formula, sign convention, refraction, refractive index, lenses, lens formula, power of lens, and most repeated board exam questions with numericals.

Why this chapter is important? Every year 4-6 questions appear including numerical problems on mirror/lens formula, ray diagrams, definition of refractive index, and power of lens.

1. Reflection of Light

When light falls on a surface and bounces back, it is called reflection.

📌 Laws of Reflection:
1️⃣ The angle of incidence (i) is equal to the angle of reflection (r).
2️⃣ The incident ray, reflected ray, and the normal all lie in the same plane.

Types of Reflection: Regular reflection (smooth surface) and Diffused reflection (rough surface).

2. Spherical Mirrors Board Favourite

A spherical mirror is a part of a hollow sphere. Two types:

🔍 Concave Mirror

  • Reflecting surface curved inward
  • Converging mirror
  • Used in torches, headlights, shaving mirrors, dentist mirrors
  • Can form real/virtual, magnified/diminished images

🪞 Convex Mirror

  • Reflecting surface curved outward
  • Diverging mirror
  • Used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles (gives wider field of view)
  • Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished image
⭐ Rear-view mirrors in vehicles are convex because they give a wider field of view and always form an erect image.

3. Important Terms for Spherical Mirrors

  • Pole (P): Center of the mirror surface
  • Center of Curvature (C): Center of the hollow sphere
  • Radius of Curvature (R): Distance from pole to center of curvature
  • Principal Focus (F): Point where parallel rays converge (concave) or appear to diverge (convex)
  • Focal Length (f): Distance from pole to focus. f = R/2

📊 Image Formation by Concave Mirror (Exam Important)

Position of ObjectPosition of ImageNature & Size

📊 Image Formation by Convex Mirror

For a convex mirror, regardless of object position, image is always formed behind the mirror, virtual, erect, and diminished.

4. Mirror Formula and Magnification

🔢 Mirror Formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Where: u = object distance, v = image distance, f = focal length
📏 Magnification (m): m = hi/ho = -v/u
• m > 1 → Image enlarged
• m < 1 → Image diminished
• m positive → Virtual & erect image
• m negative → Real & inverted image
🎯 Solved Example: An object is placed at 20 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find image distance and magnification.
Solution: u = -20 cm, f = -10 cm
1/v + 1/(-20) = 1/(-10) → 1/v = -1/10 + 1/20 = -1/20 → v = -20 cm
m = -v/u = -(-20)/(-20) = -1 → Image is real, inverted, same size.

5. Sign Convention (Cartesian Sign Convention) Must Memorize

⚠️ Important Rules for Sign Convention:
✅ All distances are measured from the pole (P) of the mirror.
✅ Distances measured in the direction of incident light → Positive
✅ Distances measured opposite to incident light → Negative
✅ Object distance (u) is ALWAYS negative.
✅ Focal length (f) → Concave mirror = negative, Convex mirror = positive.

6. Refraction of Light

The bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another is called refraction.

📌 Laws of Refraction:
1️⃣ The incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
2️⃣ Snell's Law: sin i / sin r = constant = n21 (refractive index)

🔢 Refractive Index (n)

Refractive index = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in medium = c/v

  • n < 1 → light travels faster in medium than in vacuum (impossible, so n ≥ 1 always)
  • Higher n → optically denser medium (light slows down more)
⭐ Refractive index of water = 1.33, glass = 1.5, diamond = 2.42

🔍 Real and Apparent Depth

Due to refraction, an object in water appears raised. Apparent depth = Real depth / n

7. Lenses – Convex and Concave

🔵 Convex Lens (Converging)

  • Thicker at center, thinner at edges
  • Converges light rays
  • Used in magnifying glass, camera, human eye lens
  • f = positive

🔴 Concave Lens (Diverging)

  • Thinner at center, thicker at edges
  • Diverges light rays
  • Used in myopia correction (spectacles)
  • f = negative

📐 Lens Formula and Power

Lens Formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
Power of Lens (P): P = 1/f (where f is in meters)
Unit of Power: Dioptre (D)
Power of convex lens = positive, Concave lens = negative

📊 Image Formation by Convex Lens

Object PositionImage PositionNature & Size
At infinityAt focus (F)Real, inverted, highly diminished
Beyond 2FBetween F and 2FReal, inverted, diminished
At 2FAt 2FReal, inverted, same size
Between F and 2FBeyond 2FReal, inverted, enlarged

Between F and OSame side as objectVirtual, erect, enlarged

📊 Image Formation by Concave Lens

Always forms virtual, erect, and diminished image between optical center and focus.

8. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. Define the principal focus of a concave mirror. (Answer: It is the point on principal axis where parallel rays converge after reflection.)
  2. A ray of light traveling in air enters obliquely into water. Does it bend towards or away from the normal? Why? (Answer: Towards the normal because water is optically denser than air.)
  3. What is the power of a lens of focal length -2.0 m? What type of lens is it? (Answer: P = 1/f = 1/(-2) = -0.5 D; Concave lens.)
  4. An object is placed at 15 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the nature and position of the image. (Answer: 1/v - 1/(-15) = 1/10 → v = 30 cm; Real, inverted, enlarged.)
  5. Why do we prefer a convex mirror as a rear-view mirror in vehicles? (Answer: It gives a wider field of view and always produces an erect, diminished image.)
  6. State Snell's law of refraction. Write the formula for refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1. (Answer: sin i / sin r = constant = n21)

🎯 Quick Tips for Numericals:
• u is always negative for both mirrors and lenses (object in front).
• For convex lens, f is positive; concave lens, f is negative.
• For concave mirror, f negative; convex mirror, f positive.
• v positive → image on opposite side (real); v negative → image on same side (virtual).
