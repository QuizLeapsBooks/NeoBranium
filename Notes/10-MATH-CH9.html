Some Applications of Trigonometry High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter applies trigonometric ratios to real-world situations involving heights and distances. It teaches you to measure the height of a building, the width of a river, or the distance between two ships — all without physically measuring them. Board exams consistently test these application-based problems.

1.1 Introduction

In Chapter 8, you learned about trigonometric ratios. This chapter explores how those ratios are used in everyday life. Astronomers use trigonometry to calculate distances between planets, Earth, and stars. Architects and engineers use it to determine heights and distances indirectly [citation:12].

1.2 Line of Sight

The line of sight is the straight line drawn from the eye of an observer to the point on the object being viewed [citation:8][citation:18].

1.3 Horizontal Line

The horizontal line is the straight line through the eye of the observer, parallel to the ground level [citation:12].

1.4 Angle of Elevation

The angle of elevation is the angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object being viewed is above the horizontal level. This is the case when you raise your head to look at the object [citation:5][citation:18].

Angle of Elevation: Observer looks UP from horizontal

1.5 Angle of Depression

The angle of depression is the angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object being viewed is below the horizontal level. This is the case when you lower your head to look at the object [citation:6][citation:18].

Angle of Depression: Observer looks DOWN from horizontal
🎯 Important Relationship: The angle of elevation of an object as seen by an observer is equal to the angle of depression of the observer as seen from the object [citation:2].

2. Trigonometric Ratios Used in Height & Distance Problems

For a right-angled triangle with angle θ, the ratios commonly used are [citation:17]:

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Most commonly used in this chapter: tan θ, because it relates the height (opposite side) to the horizontal distance (adjacent side) [citation:17].

RatioFormulaWhen to Use
sin θOpposite / HypotenuseWhen hypotenuse and one side are involved
cos θAdjacent / HypotenuseWhen hypotenuse and one side are involved
tan θOpposite / AdjacentWhen height and horizontal distance are involved (most common)
🎯 Quick Decision Guide:
  • If height and distance are known/required → use tan θ
  • If hypotenuse and one side are known/required → use sin θ or cos θ

3. Problems Involving Angle of Elevation

In these problems, the observer looks upward at an object. The angle is measured from the horizontal [citation:5].

3.1 Standard Setup

Consider a tower of height h at a horizontal distance d from the observer. If the angle of elevation of the top is θ, then:

tan θ = h / d

h = d × tan θ
d = h / tan θ

3.2 Solved Example — Tower Height

Example 1: A tower stands vertically on the ground. From a point on the ground, which is 15 m away from the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is found to be 60°. Find the height of the tower [citation:18].

Solution:

Let AB be the tower, CB = 15 m, and ∠ACB = 60°.

In right ΔABC:

tan 60° = AB / BC

√3 = AB / 15

AB = 15√3 m

Height of the tower = 15√3 m

3.3 Solved Example — Shadow of a Tower

Example 2: The shadow of a tower standing on level ground is found to be 40 m longer when the Sun's altitude is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower [citation:1].

Solution:

Let AB = h be the tower, BC = x be the shadow when Sun's altitude is 60°, and BD = (x + 40) m when altitude is 30°.

In ΔABC: tan 60° = h/x → h = x√3 ...(i)

In ΔABD: tan 30° = h/(x + 40) → 1/√3 = h/(x + 40) ...(ii)

From (i) and (ii): 1/√3 = x√3/(x + 40)

x + 40 = 3x → 2x = 40 → x = 20

h = 20√3 m

Height of the tower = 20√3 m

4. Problems Involving Angle of Depression

In these problems, the observer looks downward at an object. The angle is measured from the horizontal through the observer's eye [citation:6].

4.1 Key Technique

When dealing with angle of depression, draw a horizontal line through the observer's eye. The angle of depression is equal to the angle of elevation from the object to the observer (alternate interior angles) [citation:2].

4.2 Solved Example — Ships from Lighthouse

Example 3: As observed from the top of a 75 m high lighthouse from the sea-level, the angles of depression of two ships are 30° and 45°. If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships [citation:20].

Solution:

Let AB = 75 m be the lighthouse. Let C and D be the positions of the two ships.

In ΔABD: tan 45° = AB/BD → 1 = 75/BD → BD = 75 m

In ΔABC: tan 30° = AB/BC → 1/√3 = 75/BC → BC = 75√3 m

Distance between ships = CD = BC − BD = 75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) m

Distance = 75(√3 − 1) m ≈ 54.9 m

4.3 Solved Example — Building and Tower

Example 4: The angles of depression of the top and bottom of an 8 m tall building from the top of a multi-storeyed building are 30° and 45°, respectively. Find the height of the multi-storeyed building and the distance between the two buildings [citation:1].

Solution:

Let PC be the multi-storeyed building and AB be the 8 m building. Let AC = x be the distance between them.

In ΔPBD (where BD = AC = x and PD = PC − 8):

tan 30° = PD/BD → 1/√3 = (PC − 8)/x ...(i)

In ΔPAC:

tan 45° = PC/AC → 1 = PC/x → PC = x ...(ii)

From (i): x = √3(PC − 8) = √3(x − 8)

x = √3x − 8√3 → x(√3 − 1) = 8√3 → x = 8√3/(√3 − 1) = 4(3 + √3) m

PC = x = 4(3 + √3) m

Height = 4(3 + √3) m, Distance = 4(3 + √3) m

5. Problem-Solving Strategy

Follow these steps to solve any height and distance problem [citation:4][citation:17]:

Step 1: Draw a Clear Diagram
Always draw a neat, labelled diagram. Mark the observer, the object, the horizontal line, and the angle (elevation or depression).
Step 2: Identify the Right Triangle(s)
Determine which right-angled triangle(s) contain the known and unknown quantities.
Step 3: Choose the Correct Ratio
Select the trigonometric ratio based on what is given and what is required (tan is most common).
Step 4: Set Up Equations
Write the trigonometric equation(s) using the chosen ratio.
Step 5: Solve and Verify
Solve step-by-step. Check if the answer is reasonable. Use √3 = 1.732 if needed.
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Not drawing a diagram — this is the biggest cause of errors.
  • Confusing angle of elevation with angle of depression.
  • Forgetting to add the observer's height when the observer is not at ground level.
  • Using the wrong trigonometric ratio (e.g., using sin when tan is needed).
  • Not converting units properly (metres vs kilometres).

6. Important NCERT Solved Examples

6.1 Circus Rope Problem

Example 5: A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope, which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is 30° [citation:5].

Solution:

Let AB be the pole and AC = 20 m be the rope. ∠ACB = 30°.

In right ΔABC: sin 30° = AB/AC

1/2 = AB/20 → AB = 10 m

Height of the pole = 10 m

6.2 Transmission Tower Problem

Example 6: From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a 20 m high building are 45° and 60° respectively. Find the height of the tower [citation:15].

Solution:

Let BC = 20 m be the building and AB = h be the tower. Let D be the point on the ground.

In ΔBCD: tan 45° = BC/CD → 1 = 20/CD → CD = 20 m

In ΔACD: tan 60° = AC/CD → √3 = (h + 20)/20

h + 20 = 20√3 → h = 20√3 − 20 = 20(√3 − 1) m

Height of the tower = 20(√3 − 1) m

6.3 Statue on Pedestal Problem

Example 7: A statue, 1.6 m tall, stands on the top of a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is 60° and from the same point the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is 45°. Find the height of the pedestal [citation:15][citation:20].

Solution:

Let BC = h be the pedestal, AB = 1.6 m be the statue. Let D be the point on the ground with CD = x.

In ΔBCD: tan 45° = BC/CD → 1 = h/x → x = h ...(i)

In ΔACD: tan 60° = AC/CD → √3 = (1.6 + h)/x ...(ii)

From (i) and (ii): √3 = (1.6 + h)/h

√3h = 1.6 + h → h(√3 − 1) = 1.6 → h = 1.6/(√3 − 1) = 0.8(√3 + 1) m

Height of the pedestal = 0.8(√3 + 1) m

6.4 Two Poles Problem

Example 8: Two poles of equal heights are standing opposite each other on either side of the road, which is 80 m wide. From a point between them on the road, the angles of elevation of the top of the poles are 60° and 30°, respectively. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from the poles [citation:15].

Solution:

Let AB = CD = h be the poles. Let O be the point with BO = x and DO = 80 − x.

In ΔABO: tan 60° = h/x → √3 = h/x → h = x√3 ...(i)

In ΔCDO: tan 30° = h/(80 − x) → 1/√3 = h/(80 − x) ...(ii)

From (i) and (ii): 1/√3 = x√3/(80 − x) → 80 − x = 3x → 4x = 80 → x = 20

h = 20√3 m, BO = 20 m, DO = 60 m

Height = 20√3 m, Distances = 20 m and 60 m

7. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (CBSE 2017) The shadow of a tower at a time is three times as long as its shadow when the angle of elevation of the sun is 60°. Find the angle of elevation of the sun at the time of the longer shadow [citation:3]. [3 Marks]
  2. (CBSE 2016) A man standing on the deck of a ship, which is 10 m above the water level, observes the angle of elevation of the top of a hill as 60° and the angle of depression of the base of the hill as 30°. Calculate the distance of the hill from the ship and the height of the hill [citation:7]. [3 Marks]
  3. (CBSE 2015) The angle of elevation of the top Q of a vertical tower PQ from a point X on the ground is 60°. At a point Y, 40 m vertically above X, the angle of elevation is 45°. Find the height of the tower PQ and the distance XQ [citation:7]. [3 Marks]
  4. (CBSE 2020) A 1.5 m tall boy is standing at some distance from a 30 m tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top of the building increases from 30° to 60° as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked towards the building [citation:9][citation:15]. [3 Marks]
  5. (NCERT) A kite is flying at a height of 60 m above the ground. The string attached to the kite is temporarily tied to a point on the ground. The inclination of the string with the ground is 60°. Find the length of the string, assuming that there is no slack in the string [citation:9][citation:15]. [2 Marks]
  6. (NCERT) A tree breaks due to a storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground making an angle of 30° with it. The distance between the foot of the tree to the point where the top touches the ground is 8 m. Find the height of the tree [citation:4]. [3 Marks]
  7. (NCERT) From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Determine the height of the tower [citation:9]. [3 Marks]
  8. (NCERT) The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of the tower is 30° and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building [citation:15]. [3 Marks]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Concepts:
  • Line of Sight: Line from observer's eye to the object.
  • Angle of Elevation: Looking up from horizontal.
  • Angle of Depression: Looking down from horizontal.
  • Most Used Ratio: tan θ = Height / Distance.
🎯 Essential Formulas:
  • Height: h = d × tan θ
  • Distance: d = h / tan θ
  • Hypotenuse: h = d × sin θ (if using sin) or h = d / cos θ
⚠️ Final Tips for Board Exams:
  • Always draw a diagram — it carries marks in the marking scheme.
  • Write the trigonometric ratio equation clearly before solving.
  • Use √3 = 1.732 when the question asks for a numerical answer.
  • Check if the observer's height needs to be added/subtracted.
  • For angle of depression problems, convert to angle of elevation using alternate angles.
