Life Processes High Weightage Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering nutrition (autotrophic/heterotrophic), photosynthesis, respiration (aerobic/anaerobic), transportation (human heart and double circulation, plant transport), and excretion (nephron structure). Includes most repeated board exam questions.

Why this chapter is important? Every year 5-7 questions appear — diagram of human heart, nephron structure, difference between types of respiration, photosynthesis equation, and double circulation.

1. Nutrition – Modes and Types

Nutrition: The process of obtaining and utilizing food for energy, growth, and maintenance.

🌱 Autotrophic Nutrition

Organisms that make their own food using inorganic sources. Example: Green plants (photosynthesis), some bacteria (chemosynthesis).

🦁 Heterotrophic Nutrition

Organisms that depend on other organisms for food.

  • Holozoic: Ingestion of complex food (humans, animals)
  • Saprophytic: Feed on dead/decomposing matter (fungi, bacteria)
  • Parasitic: Feed on living host (tapeworm, cuscuta, ticks)
⭐ Cuscuta (Amarbel) is a parasitic plant. Fungi like mushrooms are saprophytes.

2. Photosynthesis – Food Making Process

🌿 Photosynthesis Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
(Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen)

Site of photosynthesis: Chloroplasts (contain chlorophyll)

Stages of photosynthesis:

  • Light Reaction: Light energy → chemical energy (ATP + NADPH); oxygen released
  • Dark Reaction (Calvin Cycle): CO₂ fixed to form glucose using ATP
📌 Raw materials for photosynthesis: CO₂ (from air through stomata), H₂O (from soil through roots), Sunlight, Chlorophyll

3. Respiration – Energy Release Most Repeated

Respiration: Breakdown of glucose to release energy (ATP). Occurs in mitochondria (powerhouse of cell).

🔬 Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

FeatureAerobic RespirationAnaerobic Respiration
Oxygen requirementRequiredNot required
End productsCO₂ + H₂OLactic acid (animals) OR Ethanol + CO₂ (yeast)
Energy releasedHigh (36-38 ATP)Low (2 ATP)
Occurs inMitochondriaCytoplasm
ExamplesHumans, plants, animalsYeast, muscle cells during exercise

⚡ ATP – Energy Currency of Cell

ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) stores and releases energy. When ATP breaks down to ADP + Pi, energy is released for cellular activities.

📌 Why do we get muscle cramps? During heavy exercise, oxygen deficiency leads to anaerobic respiration in muscles → lactic acid buildup → cramps.

4. Transportation in Humans

❤️ Human Circulatory System

Components: Heart, Blood vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries), Blood

Structure of Human Heart:

  • 4 chambers: Right Atrium, Right Ventricle, Left Atrium, Left Ventricle
  • Septum: Wall separating left and right sides (prevents oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixing)
  • Valves: Prevent backflow of blood (tricuspid on right, bicuspid/mitral on left)
  • Pericardium: Double-walled protective sac around heart
⭐ Left ventricle has thickest muscular wall because it pumps blood to the entire body.

Blood Vessels:

  • Arteries: Carry blood away from heart → thick walls, carry oxygenated blood (except pulmonary artery)
  • Veins: Carry blood toward heart → thin walls, valves present, carry deoxygenated blood (except pulmonary vein)
  • Capillaries: Thin-walled vessels for exchange of materials

5. Double Circulation in Humans Board Favourite

Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle.

🔄 Pathway of Double Circulation:

  1. Pulmonary Circulation: Right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs (oxygenation) → pulmonary vein → left atrium
  2. Systemic Circulation: Left ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cava → right atrium
📌 Advantages of Double Circulation:
• Complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
• Higher pressure for efficient oxygen delivery to body tissues
• Allows warm-blooded animals to maintain body temperature

Blood Pressure:

  • Systolic pressure: Pressure during ventricular contraction (normal ~120 mmHg)
  • Diastolic pressure: Pressure during ventricular relaxation (normal ~80 mmHg)
  • Measured using Sphygmomanometer

6. Transportation in Plants

🌳 Xylem – Water Transport

Carries water and minerals from roots to leaves. Movement is unidirectional (upward).

  • Tracheids and Vessels: Dead cells, hollow tubes
  • Transpiration pull: Loss of water vapor from leaves creates suction that pulls water upward

🍃 Phloem – Food Transport (Translocation)

Carries organic food (sucrose, amino acids) from leaves (source) to other parts (sink). Movement is bidirectional.

  • Sieve tubes + Companion cells: Living cells
  • Requires energy (ATP) for transport
⭐ Difference: Xylem transports water upward; Phloem transports food in both directions.

7. Excretion Board Favourite

🚽 Excretion in Humans

Removal of metabolic waste (urea, uric acid, CO₂, excess water, salts).

Kidney – Structure and Function

Each kidney contains about 1 million nephrons (functional units).

🔬 Nephron Structure:

  • Bowman's Capsule: Cup-shaped structure surrounding glomerulus
  • Glomerulus: Cluster of capillaries where filtration occurs
  • Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT): Reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, water
  • Loop of Henle: Reabsorption of water and salts
  • Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT): Further reabsorption and secretion
  • Collecting Duct: Collects urine from multiple nephrons

Steps of Urine Formation:

  1. Glomerular Filtration: Blood filtered in glomerulus → filtrate (water, glucose, urea, salts)
  2. Tubular Reabsorption: Useful substances reabsorbed into blood
  3. Secretion: Additional wastes added to filtrate
  4. Urine: Collects in collecting duct → ureter → bladder → urethra
📌 Important: Urea is the main nitrogenous waste in humans. Hemodialysis is artificial kidney treatment for kidney failure.

🌿 Excretion in Plants

  • Oxygen and CO₂ diffused through stomata
  • Wastes stored in vacuoles, old leaves, bark
  • Some wastes removed as gums, resins, latex

8. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. How is the small intestine designed to absorb digested food?
    Answer: Villi (finger-like projections) increase surface area for absorption; rich blood supply for transport.
  2. What is the role of acid in our stomach?
    Answer: Hydrochloric acid kills bacteria, activates pepsin enzyme, creates acidic medium for protein digestion.
  3. Describe double circulation in human beings. Why is it necessary?
    Answer: Blood passes through heart twice — pulmonary and systemic circulation. Ensures efficient oxygen delivery and separation of oxygenated/deoxygenated blood.
  4. What is the difference between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? Give examples.
    Answer: Aerobic requires O₂, produces CO₂+H₂O, releases more energy. Anaerobic doesn't require O₂, produces lactic acid/ethanol, releases less energy.
  5. Draw a labeled diagram of nephron and explain its function.
    Answer: Nephron filters blood, reabsorbs useful substances, excretes urine.
  6. What is the function of xylem and phloem in plants?
    Answer: Xylem transports water and minerals upward; Phloem transports food (sucrose) in both directions.
  7. Why do we get muscle cramps after heavy exercise?
    Answer: Oxygen deficiency causes anaerobic respiration in muscles → lactic acid buildup → cramps.

🎯 Quick Exam Tips:
• Plants breathe through stomata (leaves) and lenticels (stems)
• ATP is the energy currency of cell — stored energy in chemical bonds
• Human heart is myogenic (contraction originates in heart muscle itself)
• Stomata open in presence of light (for photosynthesis)
• Dialysis removes waste when kidneys fail
