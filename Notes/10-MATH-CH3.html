Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter is the foundation of algebra. It teaches you to solve real-life problems involving two unknown quantities using multiple methods — graphical, substitution, elimination, and cross-multiplication. It carries significant weightage in board exams.

1.1 What is a Linear Equation in Two Variables?

A linear equation in two variables is an equation of the form:

ax + by + c = 0

where a, b, c are real numbers, and a ≠ 0, b ≠ 0. Here, x and y are the two variables.

Example: 2x + 3y − 5 = 0

1.2 What is a Pair of Linear Equations?

A pair of linear equations in two variables consists of two equations that involve the same two variables. The general form is:

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

1.3 Solution of a Pair of Linear Equations

A solution is a pair of values (x, y) that satisfies both equations simultaneously. Geometrically, this corresponds to the point(s) where the two lines intersect.

Example: For equations x + y = 5 and x − y = 1, the solution is (3, 2) because both equations are satisfied.

🎯 Key Point: Every solution of a linear equation is a point on the line representing it. A pair of linear equations can have:
  • Unique solution (lines intersect at one point) [citation:15]
  • Infinitely many solutions (lines coincide) [citation:15]
  • No solution (lines are parallel) [citation:15]

2. Graphical Method of Solution

To solve a pair of linear equations graphically:

  1. Find at least two solutions for each equation (make a table of values).
  2. Plot the points on a graph paper and draw the lines.
  3. Find the point(s) of intersection — that is the solution.

2.1 Example: Solve Graphically

Equations: x + 3y = 6 and 2x − 3y = 12

Step 1: Make tables of values.

x + 3y = 6
x063
y201
2x − 3y = 12
x036
y−4−20

Step 2: Plot the points and draw the lines. The lines intersect at (6, 0).

Solution: x = 6, y = 0 [citation:3][citation:15]

⚠️ Common Mistake: Always verify that the point of intersection satisfies both equations. A common error is plotting points incorrectly or misreading the intersection point.

3. Nature of Solutions — Algebraic Conditions

For the pair of equations:

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

Case 1: Intersecting Lines (Unique Solution)

Condition: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂

The lines intersect at exactly one point. The pair is consistent.

Example: 5x − 4y + 8 = 0 and 7x + 6y − 9 = 0 [citation:1]

Case 2: Coincident Lines (Infinitely Many Solutions)

Condition: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

The lines coincide (one line lies on top of the other). The pair is consistent (dependent).

Example: 2x + 3y − 9 = 0 and 4x + 6y − 18 = 0 [citation:15]

Case 3: Parallel Lines (No Solution)

Condition: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂

The lines are parallel and never meet. The pair is inconsistent.

Example: x + 2y − 4 = 0 and 2x + 4y − 12 = 0 [citation:15]

3.1 Summary Table

3.2 Practice Example

Check whether the pair has a unique solution, infinite solutions, or no solution:

2x − 3y − 8 = 0 and 4x − 6y − 9 = 0 [citation:9]

Solution: a₁ = 2, b₁ = −3, c₁ = −8; a₂ = 4, b₂ = −6, c₂ = −9

a₁/a₂ = 2/4 = 1/2; b₁/b₂ = −3/−6 = 1/2; c₁/c₂ = −8/−9 = 8/9

Since a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂, the lines are parallel and have no solution. The pair is inconsistent. [citation:9]

4. Substitution Method

The substitution method involves expressing one variable in terms of the other and substituting it into the second equation.

4.1 Steps

  1. From one equation, express one variable (say y) in terms of the other (x).
  2. Substitute this expression into the other equation.
  3. Solve the resulting equation for the remaining variable.
  4. Substitute back to find the value of the other variable.

4.2 Example

Solve: x + y = 14 and x − y = 4 [citation:4]

Step 1: From equation 1, x = 14 − y

Step 2: Substitute in equation 2: (14 − y) − y = 4

Step 3: 14 − 2y = 4 → 2y = 10 → y = 5

Step 4: x = 14 − 5 = 9

Solution: x = 9, y = 5

4.3 Another Example

Solve: s − t = 3 and (s/3) + (t/2) = 6 [citation:4]

Step 1: From first equation, s = 3 + t

Step 2: (3 + t)/3 + t/2 = 6 → (6 + 2t + 3t)/6 = 6 → 6 + 5t = 36 → 5t = 30 → t = 6

Step 3: s = 3 + 6 = 9

Solution: s = 9, t = 6

5. Elimination Method

The elimination method involves eliminating one variable by adding or subtracting the equations.

5.1 Steps

  1. Multiply one or both equations by suitable numbers so that the coefficients of one variable become equal.
  2. Add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable.
  3. Solve for the remaining variable.
  4. Substitute back to find the other variable.

5.2 Example

Solve: x + 2y = 2 and x − 3y = 7 [citation:2]

Step 1: Subtract equation 2 from equation 1:

(x + 2y) − (x − 3y) = 2 − 7

x + 2y − x + 3y = −5

5y = −5 → y = −1

Step 2: x + 2(−1) = 2 → x − 2 = 2 → x = 4

Solution: x = 4, y = −1

5.3 Another Example

Solve: 3x − y = 3 and 9x − 3y = 9 [citation:4]

Multiply first equation by 3: 9x − 3y = 9

Now both equations are the same. The pair has infinitely many solutions.

6. Cross-Multiplication Method

This is a formula-based method that directly gives the solution.

6.1 Formula

For equations a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0:

x / (b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) = y / (c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) = 1 / (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

Therefore:

x = (b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) / (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)
y = (c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) / (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

6.2 When to Use

The cross-multiplication method is especially useful when the coefficients are large or not easily eliminable.

🎯 Memory Trick: Write the coefficients in the order: b₁c₂ − b₂c₁, c₁a₂ − c₂a₁, a₁b₂ − a₂b₁. Remember the pattern by thinking "b, c, a" in a cyclic order.

7. Word Problems

Word problems are a very important part of this chapter. The key is to translate the given conditions into two linear equations.

7.1 Types of Word Problems

  • Age Problems: Aftab's age and his daughter's age [citation:10]
  • Digit Problems: Sum of digits and the two-digit number [citation:2]
  • Money Problems: Cost of items, income, savings [citation:2]
  • Speed/Distance Problems: Boat upstream/downstream, train/car travel [citation:13]
  • Fraction Problems: Numerator and denominator of a fraction [citation:8]

7.2 Solved Example: Two-Digit Number

Problem: The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 8, and the difference between the number and the number formed by reversing the digits is 18. Find the number. [citation:2]

Solution:

Let the tens digit be y and the units digit be x.

Original number = 10y + x

Reversed number = 10x + y

Condition 1: x + y = 8 ...(i)

Condition 2: (10y + x) − (10x + y) = 18 → 9y − 9x = 18 → y − x = 2 ...(ii)

From (i): y = 8 − x. Substitute in (ii): (8 − x) − x = 2 → 8 − 2x = 2 → 2x = 6 → x = 3

y = 8 − 3 = 5

Original number = 10(5) + 3 = 53

7.3 Solved Example: Age Problem

Problem: Aftab's age is 7 times his daughter's age. After 3 years, he will be 3 times her age. Find their present ages. [citation:10]

Solution:

Let Aftab's present age = x years, daughter's = y years.

Condition 1: x − 7 = 7(y − 7) → x − 7y = −42 ...(i)

Condition 2: x + 3 = 3(y + 3) → x − 3y = 6 ...(ii)

Subtract (ii) from (i): −4y = −48 → y = 12

x = 6 + 3(12) = 42

Aftab = 42 years, Daughter = 12 years

8. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (CBSE)

  1. (2026) If a pair of linear equations in two variables is represented by two coincident lines, then the pair of equations has: (a) unique solution (b) two solutions (c) no solution (d) infinite solutions [1 Mark] [citation:6]
  2. (2024) Solve graphically: x + 3y = 6 and 2x − 3y = 12. Also find the area of the triangle formed by these lines with the y-axis. [3 Marks] [citation:8]
  3. (2023) For what value of k will the pair 3x + y = 1 and (2k−1)x + (k−1)y = 2k+1 have no solution? [2 Marks] [citation:8]
  4. (2022) The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 12. The number obtained by interchanging the digits exceeds the original number by 18. Find the number. [3 Marks] [citation:8]
  5. (2020) Solve: x + 2y = 2 and x − 3y = 7 using the elimination method. [2 Marks] [citation:2]
  6. (NCERT) 10 students of Class X took part in a Mathematics quiz. If the number of girls is 4 more than the number of boys, find the number of boys and girls who took part in the quiz. [3 Marks] [citation:1][citation:7]
  7. (NCERT) 5 pencils and 7 pens together cost ₹50, whereas 7 pencils and 5 pens together cost ₹46. Find the cost of one pencil and one pen. [3 Marks] [citation:1][citation:7]
  8. (NCERT) Solve: 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x − 4y = −24. Hence find the value of m for which y = mx + 3. [3 Marks]

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Formulas & Conditions:
  • Unique Solution: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂
  • Infinitely Many Solutions: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂
  • No Solution: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂
  • Cross-Multiplication: x = (b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) / (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁), y = (c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) / (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)
  • Graphical: Intersecting → 1 solution, Coincident → ∞ solutions, Parallel → No solution
