Resources and Development High Weightage Chapter

Why this chapter is important? This chapter introduces the concept of resources and how they are classified, developed, and conserved. It covers land use patterns, soil types, erosion, and conservation methods. Board exams frequently test resource classification, soil types, land degradation causes, and conservation measures.

1.1 What is a Resource?

A resource is anything in our environment that can satisfy our needs. It must be technologically accessible, economically viable, and culturally acceptable.

Human beings are crucial components of resources — they transform materials available in nature into usable resources using their knowledge, skill, and technology.

💡 Example: A river is just water until humans build a dam to generate hydroelectricity — then it becomes a valuable energy resource.
🎯 Key Point: Mere presence of materials in nature does not make them resources. They become resources only when human beings interact with them.

2. Classification of Resources

Resources are classified in several ways:

2.1 On the Basis of Origin

TypeDefinitionExamples
Biotic ResourcesObtained from the biosphere and have lifeHumans, plants, animals, fish, livestock
Abiotic ResourcesComposed of non-living thingsWater, minerals, metals, wind, solar energy

2.2 On the Basis of Exhaustibility

TypeDefinitionExamples
Renewable ResourcesCan be replenished or reproduced by natural processesSolar energy, wind energy, water, forests
Non-Renewable ResourcesForm over millions of years and exist in finite quantitiesCoal, petroleum, natural gas, minerals

2.3 On the Basis of Ownership

TypeDefinitionExamples
Individual ResourcesOwned privately by individualsPersonal land, houses, wells
Community Owned ResourcesAvailable to all members of a communityGrazing grounds, public parks, village ponds
National ResourcesBelong to a nation within its political bordersRoads, canals, railways, forests, minerals
International ResourcesManaged by international organizationsOceanic resources beyond 200 nautical miles

2.4 On the Basis of Status of Development

TypeDefinitionExamples
Potential ResourcesExist in a region but have not been utilizedWind and solar energy in Rajasthan
Developed ResourcesSurveyed and their quantity and quality determined for useCoal, petroleum, hydroelectric power
StockMaterials with potential to satisfy needs but no technology to access themHydrogen as energy source
ReservesSubset of stock that can be used with current technology but use has not startedWater in dams, forest resources for future
🎯 Memory Trick:
  • Potential: "Present but not used" — Rajasthan solar energy
  • Developed: "Surveyed and in use" — Coal, petroleum
  • Stock: "No technology" — Hydrogen fuel
  • Reserves: "Technology available, not started" — Water in dams

3. Development of Resources

Resources have been used by human beings indiscriminately, leading to major problems:

  • Depletion of resources to satisfy the greed of a few individuals
  • Accumulation of resources in a few hands, dividing society into rich and poor
  • Global ecological crises — global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution, and land degradation
💡 Sustainable Economic Development: Development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise the needs of future generations.

4. Resource Planning

In India, some regions are self-sufficient in resources while others face acute shortages. This calls for balanced resource planning at national, state, regional, and local levels.

4.1 Steps in Resource Planning in India

StepDescription
1. Identification and InventorySurveying, mapping, and qualitative/quantitative estimation of resources across regions
2. Planning StructureEvolving appropriate technology, skill, and institutional setup for implementing resource development plans
3. Matching with National PlansCoordinating resource development plans with overall national development plans
Key Point: Resources can contribute to development only when accompanied by appropriate technological development and institutional changes. India has made efforts towards resource planning since the First Five Year Plan after Independence.

4.2 Agenda 21

In June 1992, more than 100 heads of state met in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil for the first International Earth Summit. The leaders signed the Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity and adopted Agenda 21 for achieving sustainable development in the 21st century.

5. Land Resources

Land is a natural resource of utmost importance. It supports natural vegetation, wildlife, human life, economic activities, transport, and communication systems.

5.1 Land Utilisation

Land resources are used for the following purposes:

  • Forests
  • Land not available for cultivation: Barren and wasteland; land put to non-agricultural uses (buildings, roads)
  • Fallow lands: Current fallow and fallow other than current
  • Other uncultivated lands (excluding fallow land)
  • Net sown area: The most important category for agriculture

5.2 Land Use Pattern in India

The use of land is determined by:

Factor TypeExamples
Physical FactorsTopography, climate, soil types
Human FactorsPopulation density, technological capability, culture and traditions

6. Land Degradation and Conservation Measures

6.1 Causes of Land Degradation

Human ActivityImpact
DeforestationLoss of vegetation, soil erosion
OvergrazingRemoval of vegetation cover, soil compaction
Mining and QuarryingDeep scars, overburdening of land
Over-irrigationWaterlogging, salinity, and alkalinity (Punjab, Haryana)
Industrial EffluentsLand and water pollution

6.2 Conservation Measures

  • Afforestation and proper management of grazing
  • Planting of shelter belts of plants
  • Stabilisation of sand dunes by growing thorny bushes
  • Proper management of wastelands
  • Control of mining activities
  • Proper discharge and disposal of industrial effluents after treatment
🎯 State-Specific Land Degradation:
  • Punjab & Haryana: Over-irrigation (waterlogging, salinity)
  • Rajasthan: Overgrazing (main cause)
  • Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha: Deforestation due to mining
  • Gujarat & Rajasthan: Overgrazing

7. Soil as a Resource

Soil is the most important renewable natural resource. It is the medium of plant growth and supports different types of living organisms.

7.1 Formation of Soil

  • Takes millions of years to form soil up to a few centimeters in depth
  • Forces of nature: changes in temperature, running water, wind, glaciers, decomposers
  • Important factors: Parent rock, climate, vegetation, other life forms, and time
  • Soil consists of organic (humus) and inorganic materials
💡 Why is soil important? Soil is crucial for agriculture, providing nutrients for plant growth, supporting biodiversity, regulating water flow, and storing carbon. It's essential for food production and ecosystem health.

8. Classification of Soils

On the basis of factors responsible for soil formation, colour, thickness, texture, age, and chemical and physical properties, soils of India are classified as follows:

8.1 Alluvial Soils

  • Found in the entire northern plains and eastern coastal plains
  • Deposited by the Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra river systems
  • Very fertile — ideal for sugarcane, paddy, wheat
  • Old alluvial is called Bangar (more kankar); new alluvial is Khadar (finer, more fertile)

8.2 Black Soil (Regur)

  • Also called Regur soil or black cotton soil
  • Found in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh
  • Moisture-retaining, ideal for cotton cultivation
  • Develops deep cracks during hot weather, which helps in aeration
  • Rich in iron, lime, calcium, magnesium, and alumina

8.3 Red and Yellow Soils

  • Found in areas with moderate rainfall — Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Karnataka
  • Rich in iron, hence reddish color
  • Yellow color when in hydrated form

8.4 Laterite Soils

  • Found in high rainfall areas — Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu
  • Formed due to leaching — silica is washed away, leaving iron and aluminum oxides
  • Suitable for crops like tea and coffee
  • Low humus content, needs fertilizers

8.5 Arid/Desert Soils

  • Found in dry areas — Rajasthan, Gujarat
  • Low humus content, sandy texture
  • Requires irrigation for agriculture

8.6 Forest Soils

  • Found in mountainous regions
  • Supports dense vegetation
  • Varies in texture depending on altitude
🎯 Soil Identification Quick Guide:
  • Alluvial: Northern plains, fertile, Khadar/Bangar
  • Black: Maharashtra, cotton, deep cracks
  • Laterite: High rainfall, tea/coffee, leaching
  • Arid: Rajasthan, sandy, low humus

9. Soil Erosion and Conservation

9.1 What is Soil Erosion?

Soil erosion is the process by which soil is moved from one place to another, often due to natural forces like wind, water, or ice, as well as human activities such as agriculture, deforestation, or construction.

9.2 Types of Soil Erosion

TypeDescription
Water ErosionGully erosion (deep channels) and sheet erosion (thin layer removal)
Wind ErosionCommon in arid and semi-arid regions
Glacial ErosionMovement of glaciers
Tillage ErosionDue to agricultural practices
Erosion by GravityLandslides and mass movement

9.3 Soil Conservation Techniques

TechniqueDescriptionRegion
Contour PloughingPloughing along contour lines to slow water flowWestern and Central Himalayas
Terrace CultivationStep-like farming on slopesHimalayas, Uttarakhand
Strip CroppingGrowing crops in strips to reduce wind erosionPlains
Shelter BeltsPlanting trees to reduce wind erosionDesert regions
Crop RotationChanging crops to maintain soil nutrientsAll regions
💡 Chipko Movement: An environmental movement started in the 1970s in India where people (mostly women) hugged trees to prevent them from being cut down. It successfully raised awareness about deforestation and led to forest conservation policies.

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Terms:
  • Resource: Anything in environment satisfying needs; technologically accessible, economically viable, culturally acceptable
  • Renewable Resources: Can be replenished naturally (solar, wind, water)
  • Non-Renewable Resources: Finite, take millions of years to form (coal, petroleum)
  • Sustainable Development: Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' needs
  • Agenda 21: Adopted at Rio Earth Summit 1992 for sustainable development
  • Land Degradation: Caused by deforestation, overgrazing, mining, over-irrigation
  • Soil Erosion: Movement of soil by wind, water, or human activities
🎯 Soil Types Quick Reference:
  • Alluvial: Northern plains, fertile, Khadar/Bangar
  • Black (Regur): Maharashtra, cotton, deep cracks
  • Red & Yellow: Moderate rainfall, iron-rich
  • Laterite: High rainfall, tea/coffee, leaching
  • Arid: Rajasthan, sandy, low humus
  • Forest: Mountainous regions, dense vegetation
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Confusing "Stock" with "Reserves" — Stock lacks technology; Reserves have technology but use hasn't started
  • Forgetting that Punjab's land degradation is due to over-irrigation, not overgrazing
  • Mixing up Khadar (new, fertile) and Bangar (old, kankar) soils
  • Not giving state-specific examples when asked about land degradation
  • Confusing laterite soil (tea/coffee) with black soil (cotton)
