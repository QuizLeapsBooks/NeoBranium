Managing Your Personal Finances New Chapter (2026-27)

Why this chapter is important? This is a newly added chapter in the 2026-27 NCERT textbook. It teaches practical life skills—how to budget, save, invest, protect against risks, and understand income tax. These concepts are not just for exams but for real life. The chapter introduces the five pillars of personal finance and the power of compounding .

Why Personal Finance Matters

Managing money wisely is not just about earning more. It is about making careful financial decisions at every stage—whether it involves spending, saving, investing, protecting against risks, borrowing responsibly, or paying taxes. Financial decisions made today can shape a person's future financial stability and ability to manage opportunities and challenges .

🎯 The Five Pillars of Personal Finance:
  • Income – Earnings from work or other sources
  • Budgeting – Planning how to spend
  • Saving – Setting aside money for future use
  • Investing – Growing wealth over time
  • Protection & Risk Management – Safeguarding against uncertainties [citation:20]

1. Budgeting: Needs vs Wants

A budget is a plan for how to spend money. The first step in budgeting is distinguishing between needs and wants .

NeedsWants
Essential for living, health, education, or livelihoodDesirable but not always necessary
Examples: School fees, food, medicine, productive farm inputsExamples: New mobile phone (if old one works), festival celebrations (scale can be adjusted) [citation:16][citation:30]

📝 Example: Budgeting ₹1,00,000

If a family receives ₹1,00,000, they should first identify essential needs, then allocate money for savings, emergencies, education, household requirements, and productive activities. One possible allocation: Savings 30%, Education 15%, Household expenses 20%, Health/Emergency 10%, Farm/Business investment 12%, Home improvement 6%, Festivals/Recreation 5%, Other 2% [citation:30].

💡 Tip: The "Save First, Spend Later" approach means setting aside savings before planning expenses. If income is ₹1,00,000 and you save ₹20,000 first, only ₹80,000 remains for spending [citation:16].

2. Savings

Saving means keeping aside a part of income for future use. It is the money accumulated over time that is not spent .

Why Save?

  • To meet future needs
  • To provide financial support during emergencies
  • To support comfortable living
  • To provide money for future investments [citation:26]
Expenses = Income − Savings

(Save first, then spend what remains)

3. Investing and the Power of Compounding

Investing means putting savings into assets with the aim of protecting their value and generating returns. Simply keeping money at home does not grow it—in fact, inflation reduces its purchasing power over time .

Key Terms

TermMeaning
LiquidityHow quickly and easily an asset can be converted into cash without affecting its value [citation:23]
InflationThe increase in prices of goods and services over time, which reduces purchasing power [citation:23]
Purchasing PowerThe real-world value of money—how many goods and services a unit of currency can buy [citation:23]
CompoundingThe process where earnings (interest or dividends) are reinvested to generate their own additional earnings over time [citation:23]

📝 The Power of Early Investment

Suppose Bhavisha and Dhruv each save ₹1,000 every year at 7% annual interest. Bhavisha starts at age 25; Dhruv starts at age 35. By age 65, Dhruv accumulates only about 40% as much as Bhavisha—even though both saved the same amount each year. This is because compounding allows earlier investments to earn returns for a longer period [citation:23].

⚠️ Important: The earlier you start investing, the more time your money has to grow through compounding. Even small amounts invested early can grow significantly over time.

5. Protection and Risk Management

Life involves uncertainties—illness, accidents, loss of income, property damage. These can cause financial losses. Insurance is a legal contract where a person pays a regular amount called a premium to an insurance company. In return, the company provides financial compensation for specific losses covered under the policy .

Why Insurance Matters

  • Provides financial security during emergencies
  • Reduces the burden of unexpected losses
  • Prevents eligible unexpected losses from becoming severe financial burdens [citation:26]
💡 Note: A policy exclusion identifies a loss, event, or expense that the insurance contract does not cover. Always read the exclusions before buying a policy [citation:16].

6. Income Tax Basics

Paying taxes honestly and on time is an important responsibility of every citizen. India follows a slab system for calculating income tax under the new tax regime .

Key Concepts

  • Progressive Taxation: Higher rates apply to higher income ranges—those with greater taxable capacity contribute more [citation:16].
  • Tax Slab: An income range assigned a particular tax rate; not all income is taxed at the same rate [citation:16].
⚠️ Important: The new tax regime (introduced in 2020, default from April 2023) offers lower tax rates but requires giving up most exemptions and deductions [citation:20]. Students are expected to understand the slab system and how to calculate tax liability.
