Drainage High Weightage (Periodic Test)

Why this chapter is important? This chapter explains India's river systems, drainage patterns, and the economic significance of rivers and lakes. Questions on the Himalayan vs Peninsular rivers, the Ganga and Indus systems, and important lakes are frequently asked in exams [citation:1][citation:2].

Introduction to Drainage

The term drainage describes the river system of an area. Small streams flowing from different directions come together to form the main river, which ultimately drains into a large water body such as a lake, sea, or ocean [citation:1][citation:4].

Drainage Basin and Water Divide

  • Drainage Basin: The area drained by a single river system is called a drainage basin. The Ganga basin is the largest river basin in India [citation:2][citation:5].
  • Water Divide: Any elevated area, such as a mountain or upland, which separates two drainage basins. Example: Ambala is located on the water divide between the Indus and Ganga river systems [citation:2][citation:5].

1. Drainage Patterns

The streams within a drainage basin form certain patterns depending on the slope of land, underlying rock structure, and climatic conditions [citation:1][citation:4].

PatternDescription
DendriticDevelops when the river channel follows the slope of the terrain. Tributaries resemble the branches of a tree [citation:1][citation:4].
TrellisDevelops when tributaries join the main river at right angles, where hard and soft rocks exist parallel to each other [citation:1][citation:3].
RectangularDevelops on strongly jointed rocky terrain resistant to erosion [citation:1][citation:4].
RadialDevelops when streams flow in different directions from a central peak or dome-like structure [citation:1][citation:4].

2. The Himalayan Rivers

The major Himalayan rivers are the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra. Most are perennial—they have water throughout the year, receiving water from both rain and melted snow [citation:1][citation:4]. These rivers perform intensive erosional activity in their upper courses and carry huge loads of silt and sand [citation:4].

The Indus River System

  • Rises in Tibet near Lake Mansarovar [citation:1][citation:4].
  • Enters India in the Ladakh district of Jammu and Kashmir [citation:1].
  • Total length: about 2,900 km—one of the longest rivers in the world [citation:1][citation:4].
  • Tributaries in Kashmir: Zaskar, Nubra, Shyok, Hunza [citation:1].
  • Joined by Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, and Jhelum in Pakistan [citation:1].
  • Drains into the Arabian Sea [citation:1].
  • A little over one-third of the Indus basin lies in India (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab) [citation:1].

The Ganga River System

  • Headwaters called Bhagirathi, fed by the Gangotri Glacier, joined by Alaknanda at Devprayag in Uttarakhand [citation:1][citation:4].
  • Emerges from the mountains onto the plains at Haridwar [citation:1].
  • Length: over 2,500 km [citation:4][citation:8].
  • Major tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi (from Nepal), Chambal, Betwa, Son (from peninsular uplands) [citation:1].
  • At Farakka in West Bengal, it divides; the distributary Bhagirathi-Hooghly flows into the Bay of Bengal [citation:1].
  • Forms the world's largest delta—the Sundarban Delta—with the Brahmaputra [citation:4][citation:9].
  • The Ganga basin is the largest river basin in India [citation:2][citation:5].

The Brahmaputra River System

  • Rises in Tibet east of Mansarovar Lake, close to the sources of the Indus and Satluj [citation:1][citation:4].
  • Slightly longer than the Indus; most of its course lies outside India [citation:4].
  • In Tibet, it carries less water and silt due to the cold and dry climate [citation:2][citation:5].
  • In India, it passes through a region of high rainfall, carrying large volume of water and silt [citation:2].
  • Forms the world's largest riverine island—Majuli Island in Assam [citation:7][citation:9].
  • Has a braided channel in Assam and shifts its channel frequently [citation:7].
🎯 Key Point: Himalayan rivers are perennial (flow all year), while Peninsular rivers are mostly seasonal (dependent on rainfall) [citation:2][citation:4].

3. The Peninsular Rivers

The main water divide in Peninsular India is formed by the Western Ghats. Most major Peninsular rivers flow eastwards and drain into the Bay of Bengal, making deltas. The Narmada and Tapi are the only long rivers that flow west and make estuaries [citation:7].

East-Flowing Peninsular Rivers

RiverKey Features
MahanadiRises in Chhattisgarh highlands; flows through Odisha; length ~860 km [citation:7][citation:14].
GodavariLargest Peninsular river; rises from Western Ghats in Nasik, Maharashtra; length ~1,500 km; known as Dakshin Ganga; largest drainage basin among peninsular rivers [citation:7][citation:9].
KrishnaRises near Mahabaleshwar; length ~1,400 km; tributaries include Tungabhadra, Koyana, Ghatprabha, Musi, Bhima [citation:7].
KaveriRises in Brahmagiri range; length ~760 km; tributaries include Amravati, Bhavani, Hemavati, Kabini; basin covers Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu [citation:7][citation:14].

West-Flowing Peninsular Rivers

  • Narmada: Rises in Amarkantak hills (Madhya Pradesh); flows west in a rift valley; creates Marble rocks near Jabalpur and Dhuadhar falls [citation:7][citation:9].
  • Tapi: Rises in Satpura ranges (Betul district, MP); flows in a rift valley parallel to Narmada; shorter than Narmada [citation:7].
  • Both Narmada and Tapi flow through troughs/rift valleys and form estuaries (not deltas) [citation:2][citation:5].

📝 Why Peninsular Rivers Have Shorter Courses

Peninsular rivers have shorter and shallower courses because they are seasonal and the coastal plains between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea are very narrow [citation:7][citation:8].

4. Lakes of India

India has many lakes differing in size and origin. Most are permanent, while some contain water only during the rainy season [citation:4][citation:12].

Types of Lakes

  • Tectonic Lakes: Formed by Earth's crust movement. Example: Wular Lake (Jammu & Kashmir)—the largest freshwater lake in India [citation:9][citation:14].
  • Oxbow Lakes: Formed when a meandering river cuts off a bend [citation:14].
  • Lagoons: Formed by spits and bars in coastal areas. Examples: Chilika, Pulicat, Kolleru [citation:14].
  • Saltwater Lakes: In inland drainage areas. Example: Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan)—used to make salt [citation:5][citation:14].
  • Artificial Lakes: Formed by damming rivers for hydroelectric power. Example: Guru Gobind Sagar (Bhakra Nangal Dam) [citation:12][citation:14].

5. Role of Rivers in the Economy

Rivers have been fundamental to human civilization. Riverbanks have attracted settlers since ancient times, and these settlements have grown into big cities [citation:12].

  • Irrigation: Essential for agriculture, especially in India where farming is the major livelihood [citation:2][citation:5].
  • Hydropower Generation: Rivers provide water for hydroelectricity [citation:5][citation:12].
  • Navigation: Inland waterways for transport [citation:5].
  • Industrial Use: Water as raw material, coolant, and for power generation [citation:5].
  • Fertile Soil: Rivers carry silt that makes floodplains fertile [citation:5].

Economic Benefits of Lakes

  • Regulate river flow and prevent flooding during heavy rainfall [citation:5][citation:14].
  • Maintain even water flow during dry seasons [citation:5].
  • Moderate the climate of surrounding areas [citation:5].
  • Support aquatic ecosystems and enhance natural beauty/tourism [citation:5][citation:14].

6. River Pollution

The demand for river water is increasing for domestic, municipal, industrial, and agricultural needs, which affects water quality [citation:12].

  • More water is being drained out, reducing river volume [citation:12].
  • Heavy loads of untreated sewage and industrial effluents are dumped into rivers [citation:12].
  • This affects not only water quality but also the self-cleansing capacity of rivers [citation:12].
  • Degraded water quality creates health problems for humans, fish, animals, and birds [citation:15].

Quick Revision - At a Glance

🎯 Key Takeaways:
  • Drainage: River system of an area. Drainage basin: Area drained by a single river system.
  • Water divide: Elevated area separating two drainage basins (e.g., Ambala between Indus and Ganga).
  • Drainage patterns: Dendritic, Trellis, Rectangular, Radial.
  • Himalayan rivers: Perennial (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra). Peninsular rivers: Seasonal (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, Tapi).
  • Ganga: Bhagirathi + Alaknanda at Devprayag; largest basin in India; Sundarban Delta.
  • Godavari: Largest Peninsular river (Dakshin Ganga). Narmada & Tapi: Flow west through rift valleys, form estuaries.
  • Lakes: Wular (largest freshwater), Sambhar (saltwater), Chilika (lagoon), Guru Gobind Sagar (artificial).
  • Rivers in economy: Irrigation, hydropower, navigation, fertile soil.
