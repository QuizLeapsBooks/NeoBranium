Control and Coordination High Weightage Chapter

Complete detailed notes covering nervous system, neuron structure, reflex action, human brain structure and functions, plant hormones (auxin, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, ethylene), animal hormones (insulin, thyroxine, adrenaline, growth hormone), and coordination mechanisms. Includes most repeated board exam questions.

Why this chapter is important? Every year 4-6 questions appear — reflex arc diagram, brain parts and functions, plant tropisms, hormone functions, and differences between nervous and hormonal coordination.

1. Human Nervous System

The nervous system controls and coordinates body functions by transmitting electrical signals (nerve impulses).

🔹 Central Nervous System (CNS)

  • Brain: Control center for all body activities
  • Spinal Cord: Carries signals between brain and body; controls reflex actions

🔹 Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

  • Sensory (Afferent) Nerves: Carry signals from body to CNS
  • Motor (Efferent) Nerves: Carry signals from CNS to muscles/glands
⭐ PNS is divided into Somatic (voluntary actions) and Autonomic (involuntary actions like heartbeat, breathing).

2. Neuron – The Structural and Functional Unit Board Favourite

🔬 Structure of Neuron:

  • Dendrites: Receive signals from other neurons
  • Cell Body (Soma): Contains nucleus, integrates signals
  • Axon: Long fiber that carries impulses away from cell body
  • Myelin Sheath: Insulating layer (speeds up impulse transmission)
  • Synaptic Terminals (Axon endings): Release neurotransmitters to pass signal to next neuron

⚡ Synapse – Communication Point

The gap between two neurons where chemical signals (neurotransmitters) transmit information.

📌 How signal travels:
Dendrite → Cell Body → Axon → Synapse → Next neuron
Electrical impulse → Chemical (neurotransmitter) → Electrical impulse

3. Reflex Action and Reflex Arc

Reflex Action: Sudden, involuntary response to a stimulus that does not involve conscious thinking (e.g., pulling hand from hot object, knee jerk).

🔄 Reflex Arc Pathway:
Receptor → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord (interneuron) → Motor Neuron → Effector (muscle/gland)

Examples of Reflex Actions:

  • Knee jerk reflex (patellar reflex)
  • Withdrawal of hand from hot object
  • Blinking when something approaches eye
  • Salivation when food is in mouth
⭐ Reflex actions are controlled by the spinal cord, not the brain. The brain is informed later.

4. Human Brain Most Repeated

The brain is protected by the skull (cranium) and covered by three membranes called meninges. It has three main parts:

🧠 Forebrain (Cerebrum)

  • Functions: Thinking, reasoning, memory, intelligence, emotions, voluntary actions
  • Parts: Left and right hemispheres; outer part = grey matter (cerebral cortex)

🧠 Midbrain

  • Connects forebrain to hindbrain
  • Controls visual and auditory reflexes

🧠 Hindbrain

  • Cerebellum: Coordinates voluntary movements, balance, posture
  • Medulla Oblongata: Controls involuntary actions (heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, swallowing, coughing, vomiting)
  • Pons: Relays signals between cerebrum and cerebellum
📌 Easy Mnemonic:
Cerebrum → Conscious thinking
Cerebellum → Coordination and balance
Medulla → Maintains vital functions (Heart, Breathing)

5. Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

Chemical substances that regulate growth and development in plants. They act in very small quantities.

Hormone Functions Example
Auxin • Promotes cell elongation
• Phototropism (bends towards light)
• Apical dominance (suppresses lateral buds)		 Synthesized in shoot tips
Gibberellins • Stem elongation
• Seed germination
• Fruit growth		 Promotes bolting in cabbages
Cytokinins • Promotes cell division
• Delays leaf aging (senescence)
• Works with auxin		 Found in root tips and fruits
Abscisic Acid (ABA) • Stress hormone (drought, cold)
• Inhibits growth
• Closes stomata
• Promotes seed dormancy		 Wilting leaves
Ethylene • Fruit ripening
• Promotes senescence
• Abscission (leaf fall)		 Ripening of bananas, tomatoes

🌱 Plant Movements (Tropisms):

  • Phototropism: Growth towards light (auxin causes bending)
  • Geotropism (Gravitropism): Roots grow downwards (positive), shoots grow upwards (negative)
  • Hydrotropism: Growth towards water
  • Thigmotropism: Growth in response to touch (climbing plants, tendrils)
  • Chemotropism: Growth in response to chemicals (pollen tube towards ovule)
⭐ Mimosa pudica (Touch-me-not) shows rapid movement due to loss of water from touch-sensitive cells.

6. Animal Hormones – Endocrine System

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands directly into the bloodstream. They act on specific target organs.

🏥 Major Endocrine Glands and Hormones:

🧠 Pituitary Gland (Master Gland):
• Growth Hormone (GH) – regulates body growth
• TSH – stimulates thyroid
• ADH – regulates water balance in kidney
🦋 Thyroid Gland:
• Thyroxine – regulates metabolic rate, growth, development
• Iodine deficiency → Goiter (enlarged thyroid)
🍬 Pancreas:
• Insulin – lowers blood glucose (deficiency → Diabetes mellitus)
• Glucagon – raises blood glucose
⚡ Adrenal Gland (Adrenaline – Emergency Hormone):
• Increases heart rate, blood pressure, breathing rate
• Prepares body for "Fight or Flight" response
• Increases blood glucose by converting glycogen
🎽 Testes (Male): Testosterone – male secondary sexual characteristics, sperm production
👩 Ovaries (Female): Estrogen – female secondary sexual characteristics; Progesterone – maintains pregnancy
📌 Feedback Mechanism: Hormones regulate their own secretion through feedback loops (e.g., high thyroxine reduces TSH release).

7. Nervous vs Hormonal Coordination

Feature Nervous System Hormonal System
Signal type Electrical impulse + chemical (neurotransmitter) Chemical (hormones)
Speed Very fast (milliseconds) Slow (seconds to minutes/hours)
Duration of effect Short-lived Long-lasting
Transmission Via neurons (specific pathway) Via bloodstream (widespread)
Target Specific organs/muscles Target organs with specific receptors
Example Pulling hand from fire Adrenaline release during danger

8. Most Repeated Previous Year Questions (2020-2025)

Top Board Questions

  1. What is a synapse? What happens at the synapse between two neurons?
    Answer: Synapse is the gap between neurons. Electrical impulse triggers release of neurotransmitters which cross the gap and generate new impulse.
  2. Compare and contrast nervous and hormonal mechanisms for control and coordination.
    Answer: Nervous – fast, short-lived, electrical; Hormonal – slow, long-lasting, chemical via blood.
  3. How does the "Touch-me-not" plant (Mimosa pudica) respond to touch?
    Answer: Loss of water from touch-sensitive cells causes leaflets to fold due to sudden change in turgor pressure.
  4. What is the role of auxin in phototropism?
    Answer: Auxin accumulates on the shaded side, causing cells to elongate more, bending the plant towards light.
  5. Name the hormone secreted by thyroid. What happens due to its deficiency?
    Answer: Thyroxine. Deficiency causes goiter (enlarged thyroid) and hypothyroidism.
  6. Draw a labeled diagram of a neuron and explain its function.
    Answer: Neuron transmits nerve impulses; dendrites receive, axon transmits.
  7. Why is the pituitary gland called the master gland?
    Answer: It controls the secretion of other endocrine glands like thyroid, adrenals, gonads.
  8. What is the function of adrenaline?
    Answer: Prepares body for emergency (fight or flight) — increases heart rate, blood pressure, glucose.

🎯 Quick Exam Tips:
• Reflex actions do NOT involve the brain (spinal cord pathway)
• Plant growth movements are called tropisms (positive = towards, negative = away)
• Iodine is essential for thyroxine production
• Feedback mechanism regulates hormone secretion (e.g., insulin-glucose balance)
