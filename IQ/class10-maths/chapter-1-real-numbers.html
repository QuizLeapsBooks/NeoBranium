Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

If two positive integers a and b are written as a = x3y2 and b = xy3, where x, y are prime numbers, then HCF(a, b) is:

  • (A) xy
  • (B) xy2
  • (C) x3y3
  • (D) x2y2
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

HCF is the product of the smallest power of each common prime factor involved in the numbers:

a = x3 × y2
b = x1 × y3
HCF(a, b) = xmin(3,1) × ymin(2,3) = x1y2 = xy2
Board Exam Tip: HCF takes the MINIMUM power of common primes. LCM takes the MAXIMUM power of all primes (LCM = x^3 y^3).
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

Given that HCF(306, 657) = 9, what is the LCM(306, 657)?

  • (A) 22338
  • (B) 2118
  • (C) 2238
  • (D) 22330
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (A)

Using the fundamental relationship: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

LCM(306, 657) = (306 × 657) / HCF(306, 657)
= (306 × 657) / 9 = 34 × 657 = 22,338
Board Exam Tip: Formula: Product of two numbers = HCF x LCM. Always simplify by dividing 306/9 = 34 first.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Short Answer

Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 and 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 5 are composite numbers.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
For the first expression:
7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (7 × 11 + 1) = 13 × (77 + 1) = 13 × 78 = 13 × 13 × 6

Since this number has more than two factors (it has factors 1, 6, 13, 78, and itself), by definition it is a composite number.

For the second expression:
7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 5 = 5 × (7 × 6 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 1) = 5 × (1008 + 1) = 5 × 1009

Since 1009 is a prime number, the given expression can be written as the product of prime factors 5 and 1009. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, every composite number can be expressed uniquely as a product of primes. Hence, it is a composite number.

Board Exam Tip: Take common terms out (13 in part 1, 5 in part 2) to show the number is a product of two factors greater than 1.
Q4 2 Marks CBSE 2023, 2019 Short Answer

Check whether 6n can end with the digit 0 for any natural number n.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

For any natural number n, if the number 6n ends with the digit 0, it must be divisible by 10, which means it must have prime factors 2 and 5 (since 10 = 2 × 5).

Prime factorisation of 6 = 2 × 3
Therefore, 6n = (2 × 3)n = 2n × 3n

The only prime factors of 6n are 2 and 3. By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, the uniqueness of prime factorisation guarantees that there are no other prime factors in 6n.

Since 5 is not a prime factor of 6n, 6n cannot end with the digit 0 for any natural number n.

Board Exam Tip: State clearly: 'A number ending with 0 must contain 2 and 5 in its prime factorization. Here 5 is absent'.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2023, 2020 Proof of Irrationality

Prove that √5 is an irrational number.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Method of Contradiction:

Let us assume to the contrary that √5 is a rational number.
Then √5 can be written as a/b, where a and b are co-prime integers and b ≠ 0.

√5 = a / b → 5 = a2 / b2 → a2 = 5b2 … (Equation 1)

This shows that 5 divides a2. By theorem, if a prime number p divides a2, then p divides a. Therefore, 5 divides a.

Substitution:

Since 5 divides a, we can write a = 5c for some integer c.
Substituting in Equation 1:

(5c)2 = 5b2 → 25c2 = 5b2 → b2 = 5c2

This means 5 divides b2, so 5 divides b.

Conclusion:

From above, 5 is a common factor of both a and b. But this contradicts our assumption that a and b are co-prime (having no common factor other than 1).
This contradiction has arisen because of our incorrect assumption that √5 is rational.
Hence, √5 is irrational.

Board Exam Tip: This proof is 100% guaranteed in CBSE Board Exams (either √2, √3, or √5). Memorize the exact 3 steps.
Q6 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2022 Proof of Irrationality

Given that √3 is irrational, prove that 5 + 2√3 is an irrational number.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Proof:

Let us assume to the contrary that 5 + 2√3 is rational.
Then we can find co-prime integers a and b (b ≠ 0) such that:

5 + 2√3 = a / b
2√3 = (a / b) - 5 = (a - 5b) / b
√3 = (a - 5b) / (2b)

Since a and b are integers, (a - 5b) / (2b) is a rational number.
This implies that √3 is a rational number.

But this contradicts the given fact that √3 is irrational.
This contradiction arises because of our incorrect assumption that 5 + 2√3 is rational.
Therefore, 5 + 2√3 is irrational.

Board Exam Tip: Isolate the root term (√3) on the LHS and group all rational integers on the RHS.
Q7 4 Marks CBSE 2023 Sample Paper Case Study / Application

A sweet seller has 420 Kaju Barfis and 130 Badam Barfis. She wants to stack them in such a way that each stack has the same number of barfis, and they take up the least area of the tray.

  1. What is the number of barfis that can be placed in each stack for this purpose?
  2. Find the total number of stacks formed in the tray.
  3. What mathematical concept is used to find the capacity of each stack?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Capacity of each stack:

To take up the least area, the number of barfis in each stack must be the maximum possible equal quantity, which is given by HCF(420, 130).

420 = 22 × 3 × 5 × 7
130 = 2 × 5 × 13
HCF(420, 130) = 2 × 5 = 10

Therefore, 10 barfis can be placed in each stack.

2. Total number of stacks:
Stacks of Kaju Barfis = 420 / 10 = 42
Stacks of Badam Barfis = 130 / 10 = 13
Total stacks = 42 + 13 = 55 stacks
3. Mathematical Concept:

The concept of Highest Common Factor (HCF) and the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

Board Exam Tip: Whenever question says 'least area' or 'maximum capacity', it's an HCF problem. 'Minimum time' or 'coincide together' is LCM.
