Q1 1 Mark CBSE Annual Exam MCQ

One commercial unit of electrical energy (1 kWh) is equal to:

  • (A) 3.6 × 105 J
  • (B) 3.6 × 106 J
  • (C) 36 × 106 J
  • (D) 360 J
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 106 Joules (J).

Exam Tip: 1 kWh = 3.6 x 10^6 J.
Q2 3 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Kinetic Energy Derivation

Define Kinetic Energy. Derive the mathematical expression for the kinetic energy of an object of mass m moving with velocity v.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Definition:

The energy possessed by an object on account of its motion is called Kinetic Energy.

Derivation:

Work done to accelerate mass m from rest (u = 0) to velocity v over distance s by force F:

W = F × s … (1)
F = ma … (2)
v2 - u2 = 2as → s = (v2 - 0) / 2a = v2 / 2a … (3)

Substitute (2) and (3) in (1):

W = (ma) × (v2 / 2a) = ½ mv2

Since work done equals kinetic energy acquired:

Ek = ½ mv2
Exam Tip: Ek = 1/2 mv^2. Work done = Change in kinetic energy (Work-Energy theorem).
Q3 3 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Conservation of Energy

State the Law of Conservation of Energy. Show that the total mechanical energy of a freely falling body remains constant at all points during its fall.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Statement:

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

Proof for Free Fall of Mass m from Height h:
  • At top point A (height h, u = 0):
    PE = mgh, KE = 0 → Total Energy EA = mgh
  • At intermediate point B (after falling distance x, height h - x):
    v2 = 2gx → KE = ½ m(2gx) = mgx
    PE = mg(h - x) = mgh - mgx
    Total Energy EB = mgx + (mgh - mgx) = mgh
  • At ground point C (just before hitting ground, height = 0):
    v2 = 2gh → KE = ½ m(2gh) = mgh
    PE = 0 → Total Energy EC = mgh

Total mechanical energy is mgh at all points. Energy is conserved.

Exam Tip: Total energy at A, B, and C is always equal to mgh.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Power Numerical

An electric heater is rated 1500 W. How much energy does it use in 10 hours? Express your answer in Joules and commercial units (kWh).

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Calculation:
  • Power P = 1500 W = 1.5 kW, Time t = 10 h
  • Energy in kWh = P × t = 1.5 kW × 10 h = 15 kWh (units)
  • Energy in Joules = 15 × (3.6 × 106 J) = 5.4 × 107 Joules
Exam Tip: Power in kW x time in hours = kWh.
