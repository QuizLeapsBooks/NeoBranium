Q1 1 Mark CBSE Annual Exam MCQ

The diagonals of a rhombus are $16\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$. The length of each side of the rhombus is:

  • (A) $9\text{ cm}$
  • (B) $10\text{ cm}$
  • (C) $8\text{ cm}$
  • (D) $20\text{ cm}$
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles ($90^\circ$).
Half lengths of diagonals: $d_1/2 = 8\text{ cm}$, $d_2/2 = 6\text{ cm}$.
By Pythagoras Theorem in the right triangle formed by half-diagonals:
$$\text{Side} = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{ cm}$$

Exam Tip: Side of rhombus = √[(d1/2)² + (d2/2)²].
Q2 2 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Angle Ratio

The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio $3 : 5 : 9 : 13$. Find all the angles of the quadrilateral.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Solution:

Let the angles be $3x, 5x, 9x,$ and $13x$.
The sum of all interior angles of a quadrilateral is $360^\circ$:

$$3x + 5x + 9x + 13x = 360^\circ$$
$$30x = 360^\circ \implies x = 12^\circ$$

The angles are:

  • $3x = 3 \times 12^\circ = 36^\circ$
  • $5x = 5 \times 12^\circ = 60^\circ$
  • $9x = 9 \times 12^\circ = 108^\circ$
  • $13x = 13 \times 12^\circ = 156^\circ$
Exam Tip: Verify: 36° + 60° + 108° + 156° = 360°.
Q3 3 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Parallelogram Theorem

Prove that a diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Given:

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram and $AC$ is a diagonal.

To Prove:

$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$.

Proof:

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDA$:

  • $AB \parallel DC$ and $AC$ is transversal $\implies \angle BAC = \angle DCA$ (Alternate interior angles)
  • $BC \parallel AD$ and $AC$ is transversal $\implies \angle BCA = \angle DAC$ (Alternate interior angles)
  • $AC = CA$ (Common side)

By ASA Congruence Rule:
$$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$$
Hence proved.

Exam Tip: As a corollary: AB = CD and BC = AD (opposite sides are equal).
Q4 5 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Mid-Point Theorem

State and prove the Mid-Point Theorem.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Statement:

The line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is equal to half of it.

Given:

In $\triangle ABC$, $E$ and $F$ are mid-points of $AB$ and $AC$ respectively.

To Prove:

$EF \parallel BC$ and $EF = \frac{1}{2}BC$.

Construction:

Draw a line through $C$ parallel to $AB$, intersecting $EF$ produced at $D$.

Proof:

In $\triangle AEF$ and $\triangle CDF$:

  • $\angle AFE = \angle CFD$ (Vertically opposite angles)
  • $AF = CF$ ($F$ is mid-point of $AC$)
  • $\angle EAF = \angle DCF$ (Alternate interior angles as $AB \parallel CD$)

By ASA Congruence: $\triangle AEF \cong \triangle CDF$.
$$\implies EF = DF \quad \text{and} \quad AE = CD \quad [\text{CPCTC}]$$

Since $E$ is mid-point of $AB$, $AE = BE$. Thus, $BE = CD$.
Also, $BE \parallel CD$ (by construction).
One pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel $\implies BCDE$ is a parallelogram.
$$\therefore ED \parallel BC \implies EF \parallel BC$$
And $ED = BC \implies 2EF = BC \implies EF = \frac{1}{2}BC$.
Hence proved.

Exam Tip: Mid-Point Theorem is one of the most frequently asked 5-mark proofs in CBSE Class 9.
Q5 5 Marks CBSE Annual Exam Mid-Point Theorem Application

$ABCD$ is a rhombus and $P, Q, R, S$ are the mid-points of the sides $AB, BC, CD, DA$ respectively. Prove that the quadrilateral $PQRS$ is a rectangle.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Construction:

Join diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ of rhombus $ABCD$. Diagonals of a rhombus intersect at right angles ($90^\circ$).

Step 1: Prove $PQRS$ is a parallelogram

In $\triangle ABC$, $P$ and $Q$ are mid-points of $AB$ and $BC$.
By Mid-point Theorem: $PQ \parallel AC$ and $PQ = \frac{1}{2}AC$ — (1)

In $\triangle ADC$, $S$ and $R$ are mid-points of $AD$ and $CD$.
By Mid-point Theorem: $SR \parallel AC$ and $SR = \frac{1}{2}AC$ — (2)

From (1) and (2): $PQ \parallel SR$ and $PQ = SR$.
Therefore, $PQRS$ is a parallelogram.

Step 2: Prove one angle is $90^\circ$

Let $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at $O$, $PQ$ intersect $BD$ at $M$, and $QR$ intersect $AC$ at $N$.
In quadrilateral $OMPN$: $PM \parallel ON$ and $PN \parallel OM$.
So $OMPN$ is a parallelogram.
Since diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular, $\angle MON = 90^\circ$.
Opposite angles of parallelogram are equal $\implies \angle MPN = 90^\circ$, i.e., $\angle SPQ = 90^\circ$.

A parallelogram with one angle $90^\circ$ is a rectangle.
Hence, $PQRS$ is a rectangle. Hence proved.

Exam Tip: Midpoints of rhombus form a rectangle; midpoints of rectangle form a rhombus.
