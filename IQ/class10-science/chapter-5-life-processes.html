Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2021 MCQ

During cellular respiration, the breakdown of pyruvate into carbon dioxide, water, and energy takes place in:

  • (A) Cytoplasm
  • (B) Chloroplast
  • (C) Mitochondria
  • (D) Nucleus
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

The initial breakdown of 6-carbon glucose into 3-carbon pyruvate takes place in the cytoplasm (glycolysis). The subsequent aerobic breakdown of pyruvate using oxygen to produce CO2, water, and 36–38 ATP molecules occurs inside the mitochondria.

Board Exam Tip: Glucose -> Pyruvate happens in Cytoplasm. Pyruvate -> CO2 + H2O + ATP happens in Mitochondria.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

Which of the following statements about bile juice is correct?

  • (A) It contains hydrochloric acid that activates salivary amylase.
  • (B) It contains no enzymes, but emulsifies fats and provides an alkaline medium for pancreatic enzymes.
  • (C) It digests proteins into amino acids in the stomach.
  • (D) It is secreted by the pancreas and stored in the spleen.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Bile juice is secreted by the liver and stored in the gall bladder. It does not contain any digestive enzymes, yet is indispensable because: (i) It makes the acidic food coming from the stomach alkaline for pancreatic enzymes (trypsin, lipase) to act. (ii) Bile salts break down large fat globules into smaller droplets (emulsification), drastically increasing the surface area for lipase action.

Board Exam Tip: Emulsification of fats = breaking large fat globules into smaller globules by bile salts.
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2018 Autotrophic Nutrition

(a) Write the balanced chemical equation for photosynthesis.
(b) List the three major events that occur during the process of photosynthesis.
(c) How do desert plants perform photosynthesis when their stomata are closed during the day?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
(a) Balanced Chemical Equation:
6CO2 + 12H2O →Chlorophyll / Sunlight C6H12O6 [Glucose] + 6O2 ↑ + 6H2O
(b) Three Major Events:
  1. Absorption: Absorption of light energy by green pigment chlorophyll.
  2. Conversion & Photolysis: Conversion of light energy into chemical energy, and splitting (photolysis) of water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen.
  3. Reduction: Reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates (glucose).
(c) Desert Plants Adaptation:

Desert plants take up CO2 at night to prevent water loss (transpiration) and prepare an intermediate acid (malate). During the daytime, when stomata are closed, they absorb sunlight energy to complete the synthesis of carbohydrates using this intermediate.

Board Exam Tip: Write the 3 steps in exact sequential order as given in NCERT to secure full marks.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2019 Respiration Pathways

Explain the three different pathways of breakdown of glucose in living organisms with a neat flow diagram and mention the products formed in each case.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

In all living cells, 6-carbon Glucose is first broken down in the Cytoplasm into 3-carbon Pyruvate + Energy.

  • Pathway 1 (Absence of Oxygen – Yeast / Fermentation):
    Pyruvate → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide + Energy (2 ATP)
  • Pathway 2 (Lack of Oxygen – Human Muscle Cells during rigorous exercise):
    Pyruvate → Lactic acid + Energy
    Note: The accumulation of lactic acid causes muscle cramps.
  • Pathway 3 (Presence of Oxygen – Aerobic Respiration in Mitochondria):
    Pyruvate → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy (36–38 ATP)
Board Exam Tip: Draw the flow chart from NCERT (Glucose -> Pyruvate -> 3 arrows). Muscle cramps = Lactic acid accumulation.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2022 Circulation Mechanism

Explain the mechanism of Double Circulation in human beings. Why is it necessary to separate oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in mammals and birds?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Mechanism of Double Circulation:

Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cardiac cycle. It comprises two circuits:

  1. Pulmonary Circulation: Deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle is pumped to the lungs via the pulmonary artery → oxygenated in alveoli → returns to the left atrium via pulmonary veins.
  2. Systemic Circulation: Oxygenated blood from the left ventricle is pumped throughout the body via the aorta → delivers oxygen → returns as deoxygenated blood to the right atrium via the vena cava.
Why Separation is Necessary:

Mammals and birds are warm-blooded (endothermic) animals that constantly require high amounts of energy to maintain a constant body temperature. Separation prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, ensuring highly efficient oxygen delivery to body tissues for rapid cellular respiration.

Board Exam Tip: Double circulation = Pulmonary circuit + Systemic circuit. Emphasize: warm-blooded animals maintain constant internal temperature.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2021 Digestive Enzymes & Functions

State the source and specific function of each of the following in human digestion:

  1. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) and Pepsin in the stomach
  2. Mucus secreted by the gastric glands
  3. Trypsin and Lipase secreted by the pancreas
  4. Villi in the small intestine
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. HCl & Pepsin:
  • HCl: Creates an acidic medium (pH 1.5–2.0) necessary for the activation of inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin; also kills harmful microbes ingested with food.
  • Pepsin: A protein-digesting enzyme that breaks proteins down into peptones and proteoses in an acidic medium.
2. Mucus:

Protects the inner epithelial lining of the stomach from the corrosive action of hydrochloric acid under normal conditions.

3. Trypsin & Lipase:
  • Trypsin: Secreted in pancreatic juice; digests remaining proteins into peptides and amino acids in an alkaline medium.
  • Lipase: Breaks down emulsified fats into fatty acids and glycerol.
4. Villi:

Finger-like projections in the inner wall of the ileum (small intestine). They tremendously increase the surface area for absorption of digested nutrients and are richly supplied with blood capillaries to transport food to cells.

Board Exam Tip: Pepsin works in acidic pH; Trypsin works in alkaline pH. Villi maximize surface area.
Q7 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Excretory System / Nephron

Describe the structure of a nephron and explain the three steps involved in the formation of urine in human kidneys.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Nephron Structure:

The nephron is the basic structural and functional filtration unit of the kidney. It consists of a cup-shaped Bowman's capsule enclosing a tuft of capillaries called the glomerulus, leading into a long coiled tubule (PCT, Henle's loop, DCT) and finally into a collecting duct.

Three Steps in Urine Formation:
  1. Glomerular Ultrafiltration: Under high blood pressure in the glomerulus, water, glucose, amino acids, urea, and salts filter out across Bowman's capsule to form initial filtrate (~180 L/day). Blood cells and large proteins remain behind.
  2. Selective Tubular Reabsorption: As the filtrate flows through the tubule, essential substances like glucose, amino acids, salts, and a major amount of water are selectively reabsorbed back into the peritubular capillaries according to the body's hydration level.
  3. Tubular Secretion: Excess K+, H+ ions, creatinine, and drugs are secreted from capillary blood into the tubule to form final urine (~1.5 L/day), which passes through ureters into the urinary bladder.
Board Exam Tip: Initial filtrate is 180 L, but excreted urine is only 1.5 L due to selective reabsorption of water in the nephron tubule.
Q8 5 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Transport in Plants

Compare the transport of materials in Xylem with that in Phloem on the basis of:

  1. Substances transported
  2. Direction of transport
  3. Vessels/Cells involved
  4. Mechanism of transport and energy (ATP) requirement
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Comparison Table:
FeatureXylem TransportPhloem Transport (Translocation)
SubstancesWater and dissolved mineral ionsSoluble products of photosynthesis (sucrose, amino acids, plant hormones)
DirectionUnidirectional (always upward from roots to leaves)Bidirectional / Multidirectional (source leaves to storage organs or growing buds)
Conducting CellsTracheids and vessels (dead cells with lignified walls)Sieve tubes with perforated sieve plates and living companion cells
Mechanism & EnergyDriven by physical forces: Transpirational pull (suction force) and root pressure. No direct ATP expenditure required.Active transport: Sucrose is loaded using ATP energy, creating osmotic pressure that draws water and pushes food from high to low pressure regions.
Board Exam Tip: Xylem transport is passive (driven by transpiration suction pull), whereas Phloem translocation requires metabolic energy (ATP).
