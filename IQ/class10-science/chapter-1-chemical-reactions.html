Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2020 MCQ

When solid lead nitrate is heated in a dry boiling tube, the observations recorded are:

  • (A) A brown residue is left and colorless gas is evolved
  • (B) A yellow residue is left and brown fumes are evolved
  • (C) A white residue is left and pungent choking gas is evolved
  • (D) A green residue is left and yellow gas is evolved
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

When lead nitrate [Pb(NO3)2] is heated, it undergoes thermal decomposition to form yellow lead monoxide (PbO) as residue, reddish-brown fumes of nitrogen dioxide (NO2), and oxygen gas (O2).

2Pb(NO3)2(s) → 2PbO(s) [Yellow] + 4NO2(g) [Brown fumes] + O2(g)
Board Exam Tip: Remember: PbO residue is yellow when cold, and reddish-brown fumes always indicate NO2 gas.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2019 MCQ

Which of the following represents a redox reaction where Fe2O3 acts as an oxidising agent?

Fe2O3 + 2Al → Al2O3 + 2Fe
  • (A) Al is reduced and Fe2O3 is oxidised
  • (B) Fe2O3 is reduced to Fe, and Al is oxidised to Al2O3
  • (C) Both Fe and Al are oxidised
  • (D) It is only a displacement reaction, not a redox reaction
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

In this reaction, Fe2O3 loses oxygen to form Fe (reduction), while Al gains oxygen to form Al2O3 (oxidation). Therefore, Fe2O3 is the oxidising agent (substance reduced) and Al is the reducing agent (substance oxidised).

Board Exam Tip: The substance that gives oxygen or gets reduced is always the oxidising agent.
Q3 1 Mark CBSE 2024 Exemplar Assertion-Reason

Assertion (A): Respiration is considered an exothermic reaction.
Reason (R): During respiration, glucose combines with oxygen in the cells of our body and releases energy.

  • (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • (B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  • (C) A is true but R is false.
  • (D) A is false but R is true.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (A)

During digestion, carbohydrates are broken down into glucose. This glucose combines with oxygen in our body cells to provide energy along with CO2 and water. Since heat/energy is released, it is an exothermic process.

C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(aq) → 6CO2(aq) + 6H2O(l) + Energy
Board Exam Tip: Always mention the balanced biochemical equation when explaining why respiration is exothermic.
Q4 2 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Short Answer

What is a precipitation reaction? Explain by giving a balanced chemical equation with state symbols.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Definition: Any reaction that produces an insoluble solid (called a precipitate) which separates out from the solution is called a precipitation reaction.

Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4(s) ↓ (White ppt) + 2NaCl(aq)

When sodium sulphate solution is mixed with barium chloride solution, a white precipitate of barium sulphate (BaSO4) is formed immediately due to mutual exchange of ions (Double displacement).

Board Exam Tip: Always write the state symbol (s) or downward arrow (↓) to indicate a precipitate in the board exam.
Q5 2 Marks CBSE 2022, 2020 Short Answer

Why are oil and fat containing food items flushed with nitrogen gas? Define rancidity and mention any two other methods to prevent it.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Reason for Nitrogen Flushing:

Nitrogen is an unreactive (inert) gas. Flushing potato chip packets with nitrogen prevents fats and oils from coming in contact with atmospheric oxygen, thereby preventing oxidation and rancidity.

Definition of Rancidity:

When fats and oils are oxidised, their smell and taste change. This condition is known as rancidity.

Two other methods to prevent rancidity:
  1. Adding antioxidants (e.g., BHA, BHT) to foods containing fats and oils.
  2. Keeping food in airtight containers or refrigerating it to slow down oxidation.
Board Exam Tip: Keep points distinct: 1 mark for nitrogen reason, 1 mark for rancidity definition and prevention methods.
Q6 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Reasoning & Practical

In the electrolysis of water:

  1. Name the gas collected at the cathode and the anode.
  2. Why is the volume of gas collected at one electrode double that of the other?
  3. Why are a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid added to pure water before electrolysis?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Gases at electrodes:
  • Cathode (-ve electrode): Hydrogen gas (H2)
  • Anode (+ve electrode): Oxygen gas (O2)
2. Double volume reason:

Water (H2O) consists of 2 parts of hydrogen and 1 part of oxygen by volume. On decomposition, 2 volumes of hydrogen gas are produced for every 1 volume of oxygen gas according to the equation:

2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) [Cathode] + O2(g) [Anode]
3. Role of dilute sulphuric acid:

Pure water is a very poor conductor of electricity. Adding a few drops of dilute H2SO4 ionises water, providing free H+ and SO42- ions to conduct electricity easily.

Board Exam Tip: Mnemonic: 'C'athode collects 'H'ydrogen (Consonant with C/H) and Anode collects Oxygen. H2 is double of O2.
Q7 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Chemical Equation

Write balanced chemical equations for the following reactions and identify the type of reaction in each case:

  1. Iron reacts with steam to form iron(II,III) oxide and hydrogen gas.
  2. Ferrous sulphate crystals are heated strongly in a dry test tube.
  3. A shiny brown metal 'X' on heating in air turns black. Name 'X' and the black compound.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Iron with steam (Displacement / Redox):
3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
2. Heating Ferrous Sulphate (Thermal Decomposition):
2FeSO4(s) → Fe2O3(s) [Ferric oxide, reddish brown] + SO2(g) + SO3(g)

Observation: Green crystals lose water, turn white, then brown with choking burning sulphur smell.

3. Shiny brown metal:

Metal 'X' is Copper (Cu). The black compound is Copper(II) oxide (CuO).

2Cu(s) + O2(g) → 2CuO(s) [Black] (Combination / Oxidation)
Board Exam Tip: In 3Fe + 4H2O -> Fe3O4 + 4H2, steam must be written with state symbol (g).
Q8 3 Marks CBSE 2022, 2019 Redox Analysis

Identify the following in the given chemical reaction:

MnO2 + 4HCl → MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2
  1. The substance oxidised
  2. The substance reduced
  3. The oxidising agent
  4. The reducing agent
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Let us analyze the loss and gain of oxygen/hydrogen/electrons:

  • 1. Substance oxidised: HCl (loss of hydrogen and oxidation of Cl- to Cl2)
  • 2. Substance reduced: MnO2 (loss of oxygen to form MnCl2)
  • 3. Oxidising agent: MnO2 (supplies oxygen and oxidises HCl)
  • 4. Reducing agent: HCl (removes oxygen from MnO2)
Board Exam Tip: Substance oxidised is ALWAYS the reducing agent. Substance reduced is ALWAYS the oxidising agent.
Q9 5 Marks CBSE 2024 Sample Paper, 2023 Competency / Case-Based

A student dropped a few pieces of marble chips (calcium carbonate) into dilute hydrochloric acid contained in a test tube.

  1. Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction that takes place.
  2. What gas is evolved? How will you test for this gas in the laboratory? Write the chemical equations involved.
  3. What happens when the evolved gas is passed through lime water for a prolonged time? Explain with chemical equations.
  4. Classify this reaction as exothermic or endothermic with justification.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Balanced Chemical Equation:
CaCO3(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g) ↑
2. Gas evolved and laboratory test:

The gas evolved is Carbon dioxide (CO2). When CO2 is passed through lime water [Ca(OH)2], the lime water turns milky due to the formation of insoluble calcium carbonate:

Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) → CaCO3(s) [White ppt / Milky] + H2O(l)
3. Prolonged passage of CO2:

When excess CO2 is passed, the milkiness disappears because insoluble calcium carbonate converts into soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate [Ca(HCO3)2]:

CaCO3(s) + H2O(l) + CO2(g) → Ca(HCO3)2(aq) [Soluble in water, clear solution]
4. Thermochemical classification:

This reaction is exothermic because when acid reacts with carbonate, bond formation in products releases energy, causing the temperature of the test tube to rise slightly.

Board Exam Tip: Excess CO2 passing through lime water is one of CBSE's top 3 most repeated questions. Always write Ca(HCO3)2 reaction.
Q10 5 Marks CBSE 2020, 2017 Long Answer
  1. Differentiate between displacement reaction and double displacement reaction with one balanced example of each.
  2. Define the following types of decomposition reactions with one balanced chemical equation each:
    • (i) Thermal decomposition
    • (ii) Electrolytic decomposition
    • (iii) Photochemical decomposition
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Difference between Displacement and Double Displacement:
  • Displacement Reaction: A reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound solution.
    Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) [Blue] → ZnSO4(aq) [Colourless] + Cu(s) [Reddish-brown]
  • Double Displacement Reaction: A reaction in which two ionic compounds exchange ions to form two new compounds.
    AgNO3(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) ↓ [White ppt] + NaNO3(aq)
2. Types of Decomposition Reactions:
  • (i) Thermal Decomposition: Decomposition carried out by heating.
    CaCO3(s) →Δ CaO(s) + CO2(g)
  • (ii) Electrolytic Decomposition: Decomposition carried out by passing electric current.
    2NaCl(l) →Electricity 2Na(s) + Cl2(g)
  • (iii) Photochemical Decomposition: Decomposition caused by absorption of light energy (sunlight).
    2AgCl(s) [White] →Sunlight 2Ag(s) [Grey] + Cl2(g)

    Application: Used in black and white photography.

Board Exam Tip: Photochemical decomposition of AgCl and AgBr and its use in black & white photography is a guaranteed 1-mark or 2-mark question.
