Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

A cross between a pure tall pea plant (TT) and a pure dwarf pea plant (tt) resulted in progeny that were all tall because:

  • (A) Tallness is the dominant trait while shortness is recessive
  • (B) Shortness is the dominant trait
  • (C) The plants fused to form intermediate height
  • (D) Both traits are codominant
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (A)

All F1 progeny inherit genotype Tt. Since the tall allele 'T' is dominant over the short dwarf allele 't', only tallness is physically expressed in heterozygous condition (Law of Dominance).

Board Exam Tip: Phenotype = physical appearance (tall). Genotype = genetic makeup (Tt).
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2019 MCQ

In human beings, the sex of the child is determined by:

  • (A) The chromosome contributed by the mother
  • (B) The chromosome contributed by the father
  • (C) Environmental temperature during gestation
  • (D) The size of the ovum
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Women produce only one type of gamete (egg) carrying an X chromosome. Men produce two types of sperms: 50% carrying X and 50% carrying Y. If an X-carrying sperm fertilises the egg → XX (Girl). If a Y-carrying sperm fertilises → XY (Boy). Thus, the father's sperm determines sex.

Board Exam Tip: Mother is homogametic (XX), father is heterogametic (XY). Father determines child's sex.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Short Answer

Differentiate between Inherited traits and Acquired traits by giving two suitable examples of each.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Comparison:
  • Inherited Traits: Traits caused by changes in the DNA/genes of germ cells (gametes) that are passed from parents to offspring across generations.
    Examples: Eye colour, ear lobe shape (attached/free), blood group, height.
  • Acquired Traits: Characteristics developed by an individual during their lifetime due to environmental influences or practice, involving only non-reproductive somatic tissues; they cannot alter germ cell DNA and hence cannot be passed to offspring.
    Examples: Muscular physique from exercise, cut tail of a mouse, knowledge of swimming/languages.
Board Exam Tip: Acquired traits do not change the DNA of germ cells, which is why they are never inherited.
Q4 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Monohybrid Cross

Mendel crossed pure tall pea plants (TT) with pure dwarf pea plants (tt).

  1. What did he observe in the F1 generation?
  2. When F1 plants were self-pollinated, what phenotypes were observed in the F2 generation?
  3. Show the cross using a Punnett square and state the phenotypic and genotypic ratios of the F2 generation.
  4. Which law of inheritance did Mendel deduce from this monohybrid cross?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. F1 Generation:

All plants were Tall (Genotype: Tt).

2. Punnett Square for Selfing of F1 (Tt × Tt):
×Tt
TTT (Tall)Tt (Tall)
tTt (Tall)tt (Dwarf)
3. Ratios in F2 Generation:
  • Phenotypic Ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (3 : 1)
  • Genotypic Ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1 : 2 : 1)
4. Deduced Laws:

Law of Segregation: Alleles for a trait separate during gamete formation so that each gamete carries only one allele. Also establishes the Law of Dominance.

Board Exam Tip: Always state both ratios: Phenotypic ratio (3:1) and Genotypic ratio (1:2:1).
Q5 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Dihybrid Cross

Explain Mendel's dihybrid cross between pea plants with Round Yellow seeds (RRYY) and Wrinkled Green seeds (rryy).

  1. What was the phenotype and genotype of the F1 generation?
  2. What four combinations of seeds appeared in the F2 generation?
  3. State the phenotypic ratio of the F2 generation.
  4. What law of inheritance did this cross establish? State it.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. F1 Generation:

All seeds were Round and Yellow (Genotype: RrYy).

2. F2 Phenotypes (on selfing RrYy × RrYy):
  1. Round Yellow (Parental combination)
  2. Round Green (Recombinant)
  3. Wrinkled Yellow (Recombinant)
  4. Wrinkled Green (Parental combination)
3. Phenotypic Ratio in F2:

9 : 3 : 3 : 1
(9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green)

4. Law of Independent Assortment:

When two pairs of contrasting traits are combined in a hybrid, the inheritance/segregation of one pair of characters is completely independent of the other pair during gamete formation.

Board Exam Tip: Dihybrid F2 ratio 9:3:3:1 is a hallmark of the Law of Independent Assortment.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2017 Sex Determination

Explain with the help of a genetic flow diagram how sex is determined in human beings. Why is it scientifically incorrect to blame the mother for giving birth to a girl child?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Mechanism of Sex Determination:

Human cells contain 23 pairs of chromosomes. 22 pairs are autosomes, and the 23rd pair are sex chromosomes:

  • Females have two matching X chromosomes: 44 + XX
  • Males have one X and one smaller Y chromosome: 44 + XY
Mother (XX) × Father (XY)
Gametes: Mother produces all (X) eggs | Father produces (X) and (Y) sperms
• X (egg) + X (sperm) → XX → Female Child (50% probability)
• X (egg) + Y (sperm) → XY → Male Child (50% probability)
Why Blaming the Mother is Scientifically Baseless:

The mother can donate only an X chromosome to all her eggs. It is entirely the father's sperm (which carries either an X or a Y chromosome) that decides whether the baby will be a girl or a boy. Thus, sex determination is purely biological and dependent on the father's sperm type, with an equal 50% mathematical chance for either gender.

Board Exam Tip: Draw the XX and XY branching tree diagram. It earns instant 3 marks in board evaluations.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2023 Sample Paper Case-Based / Genetics Reasoning

"A trait may be inherited, but may not be expressed." Justify this statement with the help of Mendel's breeding experiment with pea plants. Also explain how genes control traits in living organisms.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Justification with Mendel's Experiment:
  • When Mendel crossed pure tall pea plants (TT) with dwarf pea plants (tt), only tall plants (Tt) appeared in the F1 generation. The dwarf trait was absent in appearance.
  • However, when these F1 tall plants were self-pollinated, the dwarf trait reappeared in the F2 generation in 25% of the offspring (ratio 3:1).
  • This proves that the recessive allele 't' for dwarfness was inherited in F1, remained hidden in the presence of dominant 'T', and reappeared unchanged in F2. Thus, a trait can be inherited without being expressed.
2. How Genes Control Traits:

A gene is a section of cellular DNA that provides the code for making a specific protein:

Gene (DNA sequence) → Messenger RNA → Specific Enzyme (Protein) → Biochemical Hormone/Reaction → Physical Trait

Example: A gene controls the synthesis of a plant growth hormone. If the gene functions efficiently, plenty of growth hormone is produced, making the plant tall. If the gene has an alteration, less enzyme is produced, leading to a dwarf plant.

Board Exam Tip: Always link: Gene -> Specific Enzyme/Protein -> Hormone amount -> Expression of Trait.
