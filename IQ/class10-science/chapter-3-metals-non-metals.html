Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2021 MCQ

Which of the following pairs of oxide are amphoteric in nature?

  • (A) Na2O and K2O
  • (B) Al2O3 and ZnO
  • (C) CO2 and SO2
  • (D) CaO and MgO
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Amphoteric oxides are metal oxides that react with both acids as well as bases to produce salt and water. Aluminium oxide (Al2O3) and Zinc oxide (ZnO) are amphoteric oxides.

Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O (Acts as base)
Al2O3 + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO2 [Sodium aluminate] + H2O (Acts as acid)
Board Exam Tip: Equations of Al2O3 with NaOH to form sodium aluminate (NaAlO2) are frequently tested in board exams.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

Which metal does not react with either cold water or hot water, but reacts vigorously with steam?

  • (A) Sodium
  • (B) Calcium
  • (C) Iron
  • (D) Copper
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

Sodium and potassium react violently with cold water. Calcium and magnesium react with hot water. Iron, aluminium, and zinc react neither with cold nor hot water, but react only with steam to form metal oxides and hydrogen gas.

3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) [Steam] → Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
Board Exam Tip: Copper, silver, and gold do not react with water or steam at all.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Reasoning

Give scientific reasons for the following:

  1. Sodium and potassium metals are kept immersed under kerosene oil.
  2. Calcium starts floating when dropped into water.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Sodium kept in kerosene:

Sodium is highly reactive. When exposed to open air, it reacts vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and moisture, catching fire and exploding. It does not react with kerosene, hence kept safely immersed.

2. Calcium floats in water:

Calcium reacts with water less violently. The bubbles of hydrogen gas (H2) formed stick to the surface of the calcium metal pieces, making them buoyant and causing them to float.

Ca(s) + 2H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + H2(g) ↑
Board Exam Tip: Mention: 'Hydrogen gas bubbles stick to the surface of the metal' for why Calcium and Magnesium float.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Electron Dot Structure

(a) Show the formation of Magnesium Chloride (MgCl2) by the transfer of electrons.
(b) Why do ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points?
(c) Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity in molten state or aqueous solution but not in solid state?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
(a) Formation of MgCl2:
  • Mg (Atomic no. 12: 2, 8, 2) loses 2 electrons:
    Mg → Mg2+ + 2e-
  • Two Cl atoms (Atomic no. 17: 2, 8, 7) each accept 1 electron:
    2Cl + 2e- → 2[Cl]-
  • Resulting ions attract by strong electrostatic forces:
    [Mg]2+ + 2[:Cl:·]- → MgCl2
(b) High melting and boiling points:

Ionic compounds consist of oppositely charged ions held together by extremely strong electrostatic forces of attraction. A considerable amount of heat energy is required to break these strong ionic bonds.

(c) Electrical conductivity:

In solid state, ions are locked in rigid crystal lattice positions and cannot move. In molten state or solution, the electrostatic forces weaken, ions become free to move and carry electric current.

Board Exam Tip: Clearly show electron dots, electron transfer arrows, and final cation/anion charges.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Metallurgy / Equations

Differentiate between Roasting and Calcination with suitable chemical equations using Zinc Blende (ZnS) and Calamine (ZnCO3) as examples.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Roasting:
  • Process of heating sulphide ores strongly in the presence of excess air to convert them into metal oxides.
  • Example: Roasting of Zinc Blende (ZnS):
    2ZnS(s) + 3O2(g) →Heat 2ZnO(s) + 2SO2(g) ↑
Calcination:
  • Process of heating carbonate ores strongly in the absence or limited supply of air to convert them into metal oxides.
  • Example: Calcination of Calamine (ZnCO3):
    ZnCO3(s) →Heat ZnO(s) + CO2(g) ↑

Next Step: The metal oxide is then reduced using Carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO

Board Exam Tip: Mnemonic: 'C'arbonate ore = 'C'alcination. Sulphide ore = Roasting in air.
Q6 3 Marks CBSE 2022, 2017 Special Reaction

What is the Thermite process?

  1. Write the balanced chemical equation.
  2. Why is this reaction extremely useful in heavy engineering?
  3. Why is it classified as a highly exothermic displacement reaction?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Definition & Balanced Equation:

The reduction of iron(III) oxide (Fe2O3) by aluminium powder is called the Thermite reaction:

Fe2O3(s) + 2Al(s) → Al2O3(s) + 2Fe(l) + Enormous Heat
2. Engineering Application:

The amount of heat evolved is so high that the iron metal produced is in the molten liquid state. This molten iron is poured into the gap to weld broken railway tracks or cracked heavy machine parts.

3. Classification:

It is a displacement reaction because more reactive aluminium displaces less reactive iron from its oxide, and strongly exothermic because massive thermal energy is released.

Board Exam Tip: Notice that iron in the product has state symbol (l) for liquid because the reaction temperature exceeds 2000°C!
Q7 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2019 Electrolytic Refining

Describe the process of electrolytic refining of copper.

  1. What materials are used as anode, cathode, and electrolyte?
  2. Write the chemical reactions taking place at the anode and cathode.
  3. What is 'anode mud'?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Cell Setup:
  • Anode (+): Thick block of impure copper metal
  • Cathode (-): Thin strip of pure copper metal
  • Electrolyte: Acidified copper sulphate (CuSO4) solution
2. Electrode Reactions:
  • At Anode (+): Copper atoms lose electrons and dissolve as Cu2+ ions into the solution:
    Cu(s) [Impure] → Cu2+(aq) + 2e-
  • At Cathode (-): An equivalent amount of pure Cu2+ ions from solution gain electrons and deposit on the cathode:
    Cu2+(aq) + 2e- → Cu(s) [Pure deposited]
3. Anode Mud:

The insoluble impurities (like gold, silver, platinum) settle down at the bottom of the electrolytic cell below the anode as anode mud.

Board Exam Tip: Pure copper always deposits at the negative cathode. Anode mud contains valuable metals (Au, Ag, Pt).
Q8 5 Marks CBSE 2024, 2018 Corrosion & Prevention
  1. What is rusting of iron? What are the two essential conditions required for rusting to occur? Describe a simple experiment to prove them.
  2. Explain the following methods used to prevent corrosion:
    • (a) Galvanisation
    • (b) Alloying (Give composition of Stainless steel, Brass, and Bronze)
    • (c) Anodising
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Rusting and Conditions:

Rusting is the oxidation of iron into hydrated iron(III) oxide (Fe2O3·xH2O). Essential conditions are Air (Oxygen) and Water (Moisture).

Three Test Tubes Experiment:

  • Tube A: Nails + Water + Air → Nails rust heavily.
  • Tube B: Nails + Boiled distilled water + layer of oil (no dissolved oxygen) → Nails do NOT rust.
  • Tube C: Nails + Anhydrous calcium chloride (absorbs all moisture) + dry air → Nails do NOT rust.
  • Conclusion: Both oxygen and moisture together are mandatory for rusting.
2. Prevention Methods:
  • (a) Galvanisation: Coating iron and steel articles with a thin protective layer of Zinc. Even if the zinc coating is scratched, zinc corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection).
  • (b) Alloying: Mixing molten metal with other metals/non-metals:
    • Stainless Steel: Fe + Ni + Cr + Carbon (doesn't rust, hard)
    • Brass: Copper (Cu) + Zinc (Zn)
    • Bronze: Copper (Cu) + Tin (Sn)
    • Solder: Lead (Pb) + Tin (Sn) (low melting point for welding wires)
  • (c) Anodising: Forming a thick protective oxide layer on aluminium electrolytically.
Board Exam Tip: Alloy compositions (Brass = Cu+Zn, Bronze = Cu+Sn, Solder = Pb+Sn) are tested in 1-mark and 2-mark questions repeatedly.
Q9 5 Marks CBSE 2023, 2021 Long / Reactivity & Acids
  1. Why is hydrogen gas usually not evolved when metals react with dilute nitric acid (HNO3)? Name two metals that do react with very dilute HNO3 to evolve H2 gas.
  2. What is Aqua Regia? State its composition and why it can dissolve gold and platinum.
  3. A metal 'M' reacts vigorously with cold water. Another metal 'N' reacts only with steam. Metal 'P' does not react even with steam. Arrange M, N, and P in increasing order of their reactivity and identify them.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Nitric Acid with Metals:

Nitric acid (HNO3) is a strong oxidising agent. As soon as hydrogen gas is produced, HNO3 oxidises it to water (H2O) and is itself reduced to nitrogen oxides (NO2, NO, or N2O).

Exceptions: Very dilute (1%) HNO3 reacts with Magnesium (Mg) and Manganese (Mn) to evolve hydrogen gas:
Mg + 2HNO3(very dil) → Mg(NO3)2 + H2 ↑

2. Aqua Regia:

Aqua Regia (Latin for 'royal water') is a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated Hydrochloric acid (HCl) and concentrated Nitric acid (HNO3) in the ratio of 3 : 1 by volume. It releases nascent chlorine which dissolves noble metals like Gold (Au) and Platinum (Pt).

3. Metal Reactivity Order:
  • Metal 'M' (reacts with cold water): E.g., Sodium (Na) or Potassium (K)
  • Metal 'N' (reacts only with steam): E.g., Iron (Fe) or Zinc (Zn)
  • Metal 'P' (unreactive even with steam): E.g., Copper (Cu) or Silver (Ag)
  • Increasing order of reactivity: P < N < M (e.g., Cu < Fe < Na)
Board Exam Tip: Aqua regia ratio: Conc. HCl : Conc. HNO3 = 3:1. Do not swap the numbers!
