Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

The SI unit of electrical resistivity (ρ) is:

  • (A) Ohm (Ω)
  • (B) Ohm-metre (Ω·m)
  • (C) Ohm / metre (Ω/m)
  • (D) Watt / metre
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

From the formula R = ρ × (L / A), we have ρ = (R × A) / L. Substituting units: (Ω × m2) / m = Ω·m (Ohm-metre).

Board Exam Tip: Resistance = Ohm (Ω); Resistivity = Ohm-metre (Ω·m). Resistivity depends ONLY on material and temperature, not on length or area.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2021 MCQ

Two resistors of resistances 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is:

  • (A) 9 Ω
  • (B) 4.5 Ω
  • (C) 2 Ω
  • (D) 18 Ω
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

For parallel combination: 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 = 1/6 + 1/3 = (1 + 2)/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 → Rp = 2 Ω.

Board Exam Tip: Equivalent resistance in parallel is ALWAYS smaller than the smallest individual resistor.
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Ohm's Law & Factors
  1. State Ohm's Law and express it mathematically. What is the shape of the V-I graph for an ohmic conductor?
  2. On what factors does the resistance of a cylindrical conductor depend?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Ohm's Law:

At constant temperature, the electric current (I) flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends.

V ∝ I → V = I × R (where R is Resistance)

The V-I graph is a straight line passing through the origin, whose slope equals resistance (R).

2. Factors Affecting Resistance:
  1. Length (L): R ∝ L (doubling length doubles resistance).
  2. Area of cross-section (A): R ∝ 1/A (thicker wire has less resistance).
  3. Nature of material: Represented by resistivity (ρ).
  4. Temperature: Increases with temperature in pure metals.
R = ρ × (L / A)
Board Exam Tip: Condition 'at constant temperature' is compulsory when stating Ohm's Law.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Reasoning / Domestic Circuits

Give reasons for the following:

  1. Why is a parallel circuit arrangement used for domestic wiring rather than a series circuit? State three reasons.
  2. Why are the heating elements of electric toasters and irons made of alloys (like Nichrome) rather than pure metals?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Advantages of Parallel Domestic Circuits:
  1. Independent Operation: Each appliance has its own dedicated switch and can be turned on/off independently without affecting others. In series, if one appliance fails, the entire circuit breaks.
  2. Same Full Voltage (220 V): Every appliance receives the full rated line voltage of 220 V, operating at its designed power rating.
  3. Independent Current Demand: Appliances of different wattage (e.g., 15 W bulb vs 1500 W heater) draw current according to their individual resistance.
  4. Lower Total Resistance: Total circuit resistance decreases, reducing power loss.
2. Heating Elements Made of Alloys:
  • Alloys (e.g., Nichrome) have a much higher electrical resistivity than pure metals, generating high heat (H = I2Rt).
  • Alloys do not undergo oxidation (burning) even at high temperatures (red hot state).
Board Exam Tip: Domestic circuits are ALWAYS parallel so each appliance gets 220 V.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Wire Stretching Numerical

A piece of wire having resistance R = 20 Ω is drawn out (stretched) so that its length is doubled. Calculate its new resistance. How many times has the resistance increased?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Initial State:

R1 = ρ (L1 / A1) = 20 Ω

During Stretching:

When the wire is stretched, its volume remains constant (V = A1 × L1 = A2 × L2).

Given: New length L2 = 2L1 → New cross-sectional area A2 = A1 / 2.

Calculation of New Resistance (R2):
R2 = ρ × (L2 / A2) = ρ × (2L1 / (A1/2)) = 4 × [ρ (L1 / A1)] = 4 × R1
R2 = 4 × 20 = 80 Ω

The new resistance is 80 Ω, which is 4 times the original resistance.

Board Exam Tip: Crucial rule: If length is increased 'n' times by stretching, new resistance becomes n^2 times original resistance!
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Circuit Numerical

Three resistors of 5 Ω, 10 Ω, and 30 Ω are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery.

  1. Calculate the total equivalent resistance of the circuit.
  2. Calculate the current flowing through each individual resistor.
  3. Calculate the total current drawn from the battery.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Equivalent Resistance (Rp):
1/Rp = 1/5 + 1/10 + 1/30 = (6 + 3 + 1) / 30 = 10 / 30 = 1/3
Rp = 3 Ω
2. Current through each resistor (I = V / R):

Since connected in parallel, potential difference across each resistor is V = 12 V:

  • I1 = 12 V / 5 Ω = 2.4 A
  • I2 = 12 V / 10 Ω = 1.2 A
  • I3 = 12 V / 30 Ω = 0.4 A
3. Total Current (Itotal):
Itotal = I1 + I2 + I3 = 2.4 + 1.2 + 0.4 = 4.0 A
(Verification: I = V / Rp = 12 / 3 = 4.0 A)
Board Exam Tip: In parallel circuits: Voltage is the same across all branches, but branch currents divide.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2024 Sample Paper, 2021 Commercial Energy & Cost Calculation

An electric refrigerator rated 400 W operates for 8 hours/day and two electric fans rated 80 W each operate for 12 hours/day. Also, four LED bulbs rated 10 W each operate for 6 hours/day.

  1. Calculate the total electrical energy consumed in commercial units (kWh) in the month of June (30 days).
  2. Find the total cost of electricity consumed if the rate is £6.50 per commercial unit (kWh).
  3. State Joule's Law of Heating and express it mathematically.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Energy Consumed Per Day:
  • Refrigerator: 400 W × 8 h = 3200 Wh
  • Two Fans: (2 × 80 W) × 12 h = 1920 Wh
  • Four Bulbs: (4 × 10 W) × 6 h = 240 Wh
  • Total Energy per day: 3200 + 1920 + 240 = 5360 Wh = 5.36 kWh (units)
Energy Consumed in June (30 days):
Total Energy (E) = 5.36 kWh/day × 30 days = 160.8 kWh (Units)
2. Total Electricity Bill:
Cost = 160.8 units × ₹6.50 = ₹1045.20
3. Joule's Law of Heating:

The heat (H) produced in a resistor is directly proportional to: (i) the square of current (I2), (ii) the resistance (R), and (iii) the time (t) for which current flows:

H = I2 × R × t (Joules)
Board Exam Tip: 1 unit of electricity = 1 kWh = 3.6 x 10^6 Joules. Always multiply by number of days in that month!
