Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2021 MCQ

In which of the following organisms does multiple fission occur as a method of asexual reproduction during unfavourable conditions?

  • (A) Amoeba
  • (B) Plasmodium (Malarial parasite)
  • (C) Leishmania
  • (D) Yeast
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Plasmodium (the malarial parasite) reproduces by multiple fission, where the parent cell divides into numerous daughter cells simultaneously inside a protective cyst. (Note: Leishmania reproduces by binary fission in a definite orientation relative to its whip-like flagellum).

Board Exam Tip: Plasmodium = Multiple fission. Leishmania = Binary fission in longitudinal orientation. Yeast = Budding.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

Which of the following parts of a flower develop into the seed and fruit respectively after fertilisation?

  • (A) Stigma and Style
  • (B) Anther and Ovary
  • (C) Ovule and Ovary
  • (D) Petal and Sepal
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

After fertilisation, the zygote divides repeatedly to form an embryo inside the ovule. The ovule develops a tough protective coat and converts into a seed. The ovary grows rapidly and ripens to form the fruit, while petals, sepals, and stamens shrivel and fall off.

Board Exam Tip: Mnemonic: Ovule -> Seed; Ovary -> Fruit.
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Reasoning & DNA Copying

Why is DNA copying an essential part of the process of reproduction? What is the importance of variations that arise during reproduction?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Importance of DNA Copying:

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid) in the cell nucleus carries genetic blueprints for body design and functions. DNA copying ensures that genetic information is faithfully transmitted from parents to offspring, maintaining body design features of the species.

Significance of Variations:

No biochemical copying process is 100% accurate; minor inaccuracies during replication lead to subtle variations in the offspring.

  • Variations are crucial for the survival of species over time in changing environmental niches.
  • Example: If water temperature in a bacterial pond suddenly rises due to global warming, most bacteria will die, but a few variants with heat resistance will survive and multiply.
  • Variations form the raw material for biological evolution.
Board Exam Tip: Keywords: 'survival of species in changing niches' and 'raw material for evolution'.
Q4 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Asexual Modes

Explain the following modes of asexual reproduction with one example and a diagrammatic description for each:

  1. Budding in Hydra
  2. Spore formation in Rhizopus (Bread mould)
  3. Regeneration in Planaria
  4. Vegetative propagation in Bryophyllum
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Budding in Hydra:

Hydra uses regenerative cells for reproduction. A small bud develops as an outgrowth due to repeated cell division at one specific site. The bud grows, develops tiny tentacles and mouth, and when fully mature, detaches from parent body to become an independent individual.

2. Spore Formation in Rhizopus:

The bread mould (Rhizopus) consists of thread-like hyphae bearing tiny blob-on-a-stick structures called sporangia. Inside sporangia, numerous microscopic spores are formed, protected by thick walls until they land on moist bread and germinate.

3. Regeneration in Planaria:

If Planaria is cut into several pieces, each piece can grow and regenerate into a complete organism using specialised proliferated cells.

4. Vegetative Propagation in Bryophyllum:

Buds produced in the notches along leaf margins of Bryophyllum fall into moist soil and develop adventitious roots and shoots to form independent new plants.

Board Exam Tip: Bryophyllum propagates vegetatively through leaf margin notches. Rhizopus reproduces via spores in sporangia.
Q5 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Human Reproductive Anatomy
  1. Why are the testes in human males located outside the abdominal cavity in the scrotum?
  2. State the two functions performed by the testes.
  3. What is the function of the secretions of seminal vesicles and prostate gland?
  4. Name the site of fertilisation in human females, and the site where the embryo implants.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Location of Testes in Scrotum:

Sperm formation requires a temperature 2–2.5°C lower than normal human core body temperature (37°C). The scrotum hangs outside the abdomen to maintain this lower optimal temperature for spermatogenesis.

2. Two Functions of Testes:
  • Production of male gametes (sperms).
  • Secretion of the male sex hormone Testosterone (regulates secondary sexual characteristics like facial hair, deep voice, and sperm development).
3. Seminal Vesicles & Prostate Gland:

They secrete fluids that: (i) Provide nourishment (fructose) to sperms, and (ii) Provide a fluid medium that makes sperm transport easier and neutralises vaginal acidity.

4. Female Sites:
  • Site of Fertilisation: Fallopian Tube (Oviduct)
  • Site of Implantation: Endometrium lining of the Uterus (womb)
Board Exam Tip: Fertilisation happens in the Fallopian tube (oviduct), NOT in the uterus. Implantation happens in the uterus.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Placenta & Menstruation

(a) What is placenta? Describe its structure and state its major functions during pregnancy.
(b) What happens in the female reproductive system when the released egg is not fertilised?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
(a) Structure and Functions of Placenta:

The placenta is a disc-shaped vascular organ embedded in the uterine wall that connects the developing embryo to the mother's bloodstream.

  • Structure: It contains finger-like villi on the embryo's side. On the mother's side are blood spaces that bathe the villi, providing a large surface area for exchange.
  • Functions: (i) Provides glucose, amino acids, and oxygen from mother to embryo. (ii) Removes carbon dioxide and nitrogenous metabolic wastes generated by embryo into mother's blood.
(b) When Egg is Not Fertilised (Menstruation):

If the egg released from the ovary is not fertilised by a sperm, it lives for about one day. The thick, spongy uterine lining prepared for pregnancy breaks down along with its ruptured blood capillaries, resulting in discharge of blood and mucus through the vagina for 3–5 days, a process known as menstruation (occurs roughly every 28 days).

Board Exam Tip: Placenta villi maximize surface area for diffusion of nutrients and removal of wastes.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2024, 2022 Contraception & Health
  1. What is contraception? Explain four different methods of contraception used for birth control with one example each.
  2. What are Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)? Give two examples of bacterial STDs and two examples of viral STDs. How can STDs be prevented?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Contraceptive Methods:

Contraception is the intentional prevention of conception (pregnancy) through artificial methods.

  1. Barrier Methods: Physical barriers prevent sperms from meeting egg. E.g., Condoms on penis or diaphragms in vagina. (Condoms also prevent STDs).
  2. Chemical Methods (Hormonal Pills): Oral pills containing progesterone/estrogen prevent ovaries from releasing eggs.
  3. Intrauterine Devices (IUDs): Loop or Copper-T inserted into uterus by doctors to prevent implantation of embryo.
  4. Surgical Methods (Sterilisation): Permanent methods:
    • Vasectomy: In males, small portion of vas deferens is cut and tied.
    • Tubectomy: In females, fallopian tubes are cut and tied.
2. Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs):

Infections transmitted through sexual contact:

  • Bacterial STDs: Gonorrhoea and Syphilis.
  • Viral STDs: Warts and HIV-AIDS.
  • Prevention: Use of barrier contraceptives like condoms during intercourse, avoiding multiple partners, and maintaining hygiene.
Board Exam Tip: Copper-T does NOT prevent fertilisation; it prevents implantation. Only barrier methods (condoms) protect against STDs.
