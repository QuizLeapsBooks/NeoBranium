Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

Which plant hormone is primarily responsible for cell elongation and causes phototropism by moving towards the shaded side of a growing shoot?

  • (A) Gibberellin
  • (B) Cytokinin
  • (C) Auxin
  • (D) Abscisic acid
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

Auxin synthesised at the shoot tip diffuses towards the shaded side of the shoot. Higher concentration of auxin on the shaded side stimulates cells there to grow longer than cells on the illuminated side, causing the shoot to bend towards light (positive phototropism).

Board Exam Tip: Auxin = cell elongation & phototropism. Cytokinin = cell division. Abscisic acid = growth inhibitor & wilting.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2021 MCQ

Posture and balance of the human body is maintained and coordinated by which part of the brain?

  • (A) Cerebrum
  • (B) Cerebellum
  • (C) Medulla oblongata
  • (D) Pons
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

The cerebellum (part of hindbrain) is responsible for precision of voluntary actions (such as walking in a straight line, picking up a pen, riding a bicycle) and maintaining the posture and equilibrium (balance) of the body.

Board Exam Tip: Cerebrum = thinking, memory, intelligence. Cerebellum = balance, posture. Medulla = involuntary actions (BP, vomiting).
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Neuron Structure & Synapse

(a) Name the parts of a neuron where:
  (i) Information is first acquired.
  (ii) Through which information travels as an electrical impulse.
  (iii) Where this impulse is converted into a chemical signal for onward transmission.
(b) What is a synapse? How does a nerve impulse cross a synapse?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
(a) Neuron Components:
  • (i) Information acquired: Dendritic tip
  • (ii) Electric impulse transmission: Along the Axon
  • (iii) Chemical conversion: At the Nerve ending (Axon terminal)
(b) Synapse and Transmission:

A synapse is a microscopic junction or gap between the nerve ending of one neuron and the dendrite of the next adjacent neuron.

When an electrical impulse reaches the axonal end, it triggers the release of minute chemical substances called neurotransmitters into the synapse. These chemicals diffuse across the gap and bind to receptors on the dendrite of the next neuron, setting off an identical electrical impulse.

Board Exam Tip: Impulses travel in one direction only across a synapse because neurotransmitters are released only from the axon terminal.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2020 Reflex Arc

What is a reflex action? Trace the pathway of a nerve impulse in a reflex arc when you suddenly touch a hot plate.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Definition of Reflex Action:

A reflex action is an extremely rapid, involuntary, and unconscious automatic response to an external stimulus, executed directly by the spinal cord to protect the body from injury without prior thinking by the brain.

Sequence in Reflex Arc:
  1. Receptor (Thermoreceptors in skin): Detects intense heat stimulus.
  2. Sensory Neuron: Conducts the electrical impulse to the spinal cord.
  3. Relay Neuron (Interneuron in Spinal Cord): Processes signal instantly and relays command directly to motor neuron (saving crucial time).
  4. Motor Neuron: Carries executive motor impulse to the effector muscle.
  5. Effector (Biceps muscle of arm): Contracts instantly, withdrawing the hand from the hot plate.
Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory Neuron → Spinal Cord (Relay Neuron) → Motor Neuron → Effector → Response
Board Exam Tip: Reflex arc route: Receptor -> Sensory neuron -> Spinal cord -> Motor neuron -> Effector (muscle).
Q5 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2018 Plant Tropisms

What are tropic movements? Name and explain with examples four types of directional plant growth movements in response to external stimuli.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Tropic Movement (Tropism): A directional growth movement of a plant part in response to an external directional environmental stimulus.

  • 1. Phototropism (Stimulus: Light): Shoot grows towards light (+ve phototropism) due to auxin accumulation on shaded side; roots grow away (-ve phototropism).
  • 2. Geotropism (Stimulus: Gravity): Roots grow downwards towards gravity (+ve geotropism) to anchor plant and absorb water; shoots grow upwards away from gravity (-ve geotropism).
  • 3. Hydrotropism (Stimulus: Moisture/Water): Roots bend and grow towards water sources even against gravity.
  • 4. Chemotropism (Stimulus: Chemical): Growth of the pollen tube through the style towards the ovule attracted by chemical secretions from the ovary during fertilization.
Board Exam Tip: Pollen tube growth towards ovule = Chemotropism. Shoot bending towards light = Phototropism.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2021 Endocrine Hormones

Name the hormone secreted by the following endocrine glands, state their primary function, and describe the disorder caused by their deficiency or oversecretion:

  1. Thyroid gland
  2. Pancreas
  3. Adrenal gland
  4. Pituitary gland
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Thyroid Gland – Thyroxin:

Function: Regulates carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism for balanced body growth. Requires Iodine for synthesis.

Disorder: Deficiency of iodine causes enlargement of thyroid gland in neck known as Goitre.

2. Pancreas – Insulin:

Function: Regulates and lowers blood sugar (glucose) levels by promoting cellular uptake of glucose.

Disorder: Deficiency causes Diabetes Mellitus (high blood sugar levels requiring insulin injections).

3. Adrenal Gland – Adrenaline:

Function: 'Fight or Flight' hormone secreted during emergency/fear. Increases heartbeat, dilates pupils, rushes blood to skeletal muscles, increases breathing rate.

4. Pituitary Gland – Growth Hormone (GH):

Function: Regulates overall growth and skeletal development.

Disorder: Deficiency in childhood causes Dwarfism; oversecretion leads to Gigantism.

Board Exam Tip: Why iodised salt is advised: Iodine is essential for thyroid gland to make thyroxin, preventing goitre.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2022, 2017 Brain Structure & Feedback Mechanism
  1. Name the three major divisions of the human brain and state two functions of each part.
  2. Explain the feedback mechanism of hormonal regulation with the example of blood sugar level control by the pancreas.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Three Divisions of Human Brain:
  • Forebrain (Cerebrum, Olfactory lobes): (i) Main thinking and intellectual part; processes memory, sensory analysis (sight, hearing, smell). (ii) Controls voluntary motor actions.
  • Midbrain: Controls auditory reflexes, visual reflexes, and pupil size adjustments.
  • Hindbrain:
    • Cerebellum: Coordinates precision voluntary movements and maintains body posture/balance.
    • Medulla Oblongata: Controls vital involuntary functions like heartbeat, blood pressure, salivation, and vomiting reflex.
    • Pons: Assists in regulation of respiration.
2. Feedback Mechanism:

The timing and amount of hormone released by endocrine glands are regulated by accurate feedback mechanisms so hormones are produced only in required quantities.

Example: Blood Sugar Regulation:

  • When blood sugar levels rise (e.g., after a meal), beta cells of the pancreas detect this increase and respond by secreting more insulin into blood.
  • Insulin facilitates cellular uptake and storage of glucose as glycogen in liver, causing blood sugar to fall to normal.
  • As blood glucose drops back to normal, the stimulus stops, and insulin secretion from the pancreas is automatically reduced (negative feedback).
Board Exam Tip: Negative feedback ensures stability (homeostasis). When sugar drops, insulin production decreases automatically.
