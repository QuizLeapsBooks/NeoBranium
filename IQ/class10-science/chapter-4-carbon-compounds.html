Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

What is the general formula of the homologous series of alkynes, and what is the IUPAC name of the first stable member?

  • (A) CnH2n+2, Methane
  • (B) CnH2n, Ethene
  • (C) CnH2n-2, Ethyne (Acetylene)
  • (D) CnH2n-1, Propyne
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

The general formula for alkynes (containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond C≡C) is CnH2n-2. The first member must have at least 2 carbon atoms (n = 2), giving C2H2 named Ethyne (common name acetylene).

Board Exam Tip: Alkanes: CnH2n+2 (single bond); Alkenes: CnH2n (double bond); Alkynes: CnH2n-2 (triple bond).
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

Conversion of ethanol into ethanoic acid in the presence of alkaline KMnO4 is an example of:

  • (A) Reduction reaction
  • (B) Oxidation reaction
  • (C) Substitution reaction
  • (D) Addition reaction
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Alkaline potassium permanganate (KMnO4) or acidified potassium dichromate (K2Cr2O7) act as strong oxidising agents. They add oxygen to ethanol (CH3CH2OH) to convert it into ethanoic acid (CH3COOH):

CH3CH2OH + 2[O] →Alkaline KMnO4 + Δ CH3COOH + H2O
Board Exam Tip: Alkaline KMnO4 or Acidified K2Cr2O7 are designated oxidising agents in Class 10 organic chemistry.
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Reasoning & Versatility

Carbon cannot form C4+ cation or C4- anion to complete its octet. Explain why. What type of bond does carbon form? State two unique properties of carbon responsible for the large number of carbon compounds.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Why Carbon cannot form C4+ or C4-:
  • Cannot form C4-: Carbon nucleus has 6 protons. Holding 10 electrons (6 original + 4 gained) is difficult for 6 protons due to strong inter-electronic repulsion.
  • Cannot form C4+: Removing 4 electrons requires a colossal amount of energy that cannot be compensated.
  • Conclusion: Carbon overcomes this by sharing electrons to form covalent bonds.
Two Unique Properties of Carbon:
  1. Catenation: The unique ability of carbon atoms to form strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms to form long chains, branched chains, or closed rings. Carbon-carbon bonds are very strong due to small atomic size.
  2. Tetravalency: Carbon has a valency of 4, capable of bonding with 4 other atoms of carbon or other monovalent elements (H, Cl, O, N, S).
Board Exam Tip: Small size of carbon allows its nucleus to hold shared pairs of electrons firmly, making carbon-carbon bonds exceptionally strong.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Homologous Series

What is a homologous series? State any four characteristics of a homologous series. Write the molecular formula and name of the second and third members of the alcohol homologous series.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Definition:

A series of organic compounds having the same functional group, same chemical properties, and in which successive members differ by a -CH2- unit is called a homologous series.

Four Characteristics:
  1. All members can be represented by a common general formula.
  2. Successive members differ by -CH2- in formula and 14 u in molecular mass.
  3. They show similar chemical properties due to the same functional group.
  4. They show a gradual gradation in physical properties (boiling point, melting point, density) as molecular mass increases.
Alcohol Series:
  • General Formula: CnH2n+1OH
  • 2nd member (n=2): CH3CH2OH / C2H5OH – Ethanol
  • 3rd member (n=3): CH3CH2CH2OH / C3H7OH – Propanol
Board Exam Tip: Difference in molecular mass between two consecutive members is always 14 u (C=12 + 2H=2).
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2022, 2017 Addition & Substitution
  1. What is an addition reaction? Write the chemical equation for the industrial hydrogenation of vegetable oils, specifying the catalyst used.
  2. What is a substitution reaction? Write the chemical equation for the chlorination of methane in sunlight.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Addition Reaction (Hydrogenation of Oils):

Unsaturated hydrocarbons add hydrogen in the presence of catalysts such as Nickel (Ni) or Palladium (Pd) to give saturated hydrocarbons:

R2C = CR2 [Vegetable oil / Liquid unsaturated] + H2 →Ni Catalyst / Δ R2CH—CHR2 [Vanaspati Ghee / Solid saturated]

Health Aspect: Vegetable oils (unsaturated) are healthier than animal fats (saturated).

2. Substitution Reaction:

A reaction in which one or more hydrogen atoms of an alkane are replaced one by one by other atoms (like chlorine) in the presence of sunlight:

CH4 + Cl2 →Sunlight (hν) CH3Cl [Chloromethane] + HCl
Board Exam Tip: Nickel catalyst is required for hydrogenation. Vegetable oils are unsaturated; vanaspati ghee is saturated.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Cleansing Action of Soap

Explain the mechanism of the cleansing action of soaps.

  1. Describe the structure of a soap molecule with hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts.
  2. What is a micelle? How does it entrap dirt and remove it from fabric?
  3. Why are soaps not suitable for washing in hard water?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Soap Molecule Structure:

Soap is a sodium or potassium salt of long-chain fatty acids (e.g., C17H35COO-Na+).

  • Ionic end (COO-Na+): Hydrophilic (water-loving), dissolves in water.
  • Long hydrocarbon tail (C17H35-): Hydrophobic (water-fearing), dissolves in oil/grease/dirt.
2. Micelle Formation & Dirt Removal:

When soap is dissolved in water, the hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails cluster inward towards the oily dirt droplet, while the ionic heads project outward interacting with surrounding water molecules. This spherical cluster is called a micelle.

When agitated, the oil droplet is trapped inside the micelle emulsion and gets washed away with rinsing water. Micelles do not precipitate together due to ion-ion electrostatic repulsion between negative heads.

3. Soaps in Hard Water:

Hard water contains dissolved Ca2+ and Mg2+ salts. Soaps react with these ions to form an insoluble sticky white curdy precipitate called scum, wasting soap and sticking to clothes.

Board Exam Tip: Always mention: ionic end faces outwards into water, hydrocarbon tail faces inwards into the oil droplet.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Reactions of Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid

Write balanced chemical equations and observations for the following reactions of Ethanol and Ethanoic acid:

  1. Ethanol reacts with Sodium metal.
  2. Ethanol is heated with concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K.
  3. Ethanoic acid reacts with Ethanol in presence of concentrated H2SO4 (Esterification).
  4. An ester is treated with sodium hydroxide solution (Saponification).
  5. Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Ethanol + Sodium:
2CH3CH2OH + 2Na → 2CH3CH2ONa [Sodium ethoxide] + H2 ↑

Observation: Effervescence of hydrogen gas (burns with a pop sound).

2. Dehydration of Ethanol:
CH3CH2OH →Conc. H2SO4 at 443 K CH2=CH2 [Ethene] + H2O

Role of Conc. H2SO4: Acts as a dehydrating agent removing water.

3. Esterification:
CH3COOH + C2H5OH →Conc. H2SO4 CH3COOC2H5 [Ethyl ethanoate / Ester] + H2O

Observation: Production of a sweet fruity smell. Esters are used in perfumes and flavourings.

4. Saponification (Soap making):
CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa [Sodium ethanoate / Soap] + C2H5OH
5. Reaction with NaHCO3:
CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 ↑

Observation: Brisk effervescence of CO2 gas which turns lime water milky. This distinguishes carboxylic acids from alcohols.

Board Exam Tip: The esterification test (fruity smell) and NaHCO3 test (brisk CO2 effervescence) are standard laboratory distinction tests.
Q8 5 Marks CBSE 2023 Sample Paper Case-Based / Organic Identification

An organic compound 'A' is a constituent of wine and has molecular formula C2H6O. On heating with alkaline potassium permanganate, 'A' gets converted to compound 'B' which turns blue litmus red.

  1. Identify compounds 'A' and 'B'.
  2. Write the chemical equation for the conversion of 'A' into 'B'.
  3. When 'A' and 'B' are warmed together in the presence of concentrated H2SO4, a sweet-smelling compound 'C' is formed. Name 'C' and write the reaction.
  4. What happens when 'B' is treated with baking soda? Name the gas evolved and give its test.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Identification:
  • Compound 'A': Ethanol / Ethyl alcohol (CH3CH2OH)
  • Compound 'B': Ethanoic acid / Acetic acid (CH3COOH)
  • Compound 'C': Ethyl ethanoate (an ester) (CH3COOC2H5)
2. Oxidation of 'A' into 'B':
CH3CH2OH + 2[O] →Alkaline KMnO4 + Δ CH3COOH + H2O
3. Formation of 'C' (Esterification):
CH3COOH + C2H5OH →Conc. H2SO4 CH3COOC2H5 + H2O
4. Reaction with Baking Soda:
CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 ↑

Gas evolved is Carbon dioxide (CO2). When passed through lime water, it turns lime water milky.

Board Exam Tip: C2H6O is always Ethanol. C2H4O2 is Ethanoic acid. Esters are recognized by sweet fruity odor.
