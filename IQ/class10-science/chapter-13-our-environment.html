Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

Which synthetic chemical compound is primarily responsible for the depletion of the protective ozone layer in the stratosphere?

  • (A) Carbon dioxide (CO2)
  • (B) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  • (C) Methane (CH4)
  • (D) Sulphur dioxide (SO2)
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), widely used in refrigerators, air conditioners, and aerosol sprays, release active chlorine atoms under solar UV radiation in the stratosphere, which catalytically break down protective ozone (O3) into molecular oxygen (O2).

Board Exam Tip: Montreal Protocol (1987) by UNEP successfully froze CFC production at 1986 levels.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

In a food chain consisting of Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small Fish → Fish-eating Bird, which organism will accumulate the highest concentration of non-biodegradable pesticides?

  • (A) Phytoplankton
  • (B) Zooplankton
  • (C) Small Fish
  • (D) Fish-eating Bird
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (D)

Due to Biological Magnification, non-biodegradable toxic chemicals (like DDT) cannot be metabolised or excreted and progressively accumulate at higher concentrations at each successive trophic level. The organism at the top trophic level (Fish-eating Bird) accumulates the maximum concentration.

Board Exam Tip: Top carnivore / highest trophic level ALWAYS has maximum concentration of pesticides (Biomagnification).
Q3 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 10% Law & Numericals

(a) State the 10 Percent Law of energy transfer in an ecosystem.
(b) Why are food chains generally limited to only 3 or 4 trophic levels?
(c) Numerical: If 20,000 J of solar energy falls on green plants in a terrestrial ecosystem, calculate the amount of energy available to the tiger in the following food chain: Plants → Deer → Tiger.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
(a) 10 Percent Law (Lindeman, 1942):

Only about 10% of the energy entering a particular trophic level is available for transfer to the next higher trophic level. The remaining 90% is lost as metabolic heat, respiration, and excretion.

(b) Why Food Chains are Short (3–4 levels):

Since 90% of energy is lost at each step, after 3–4 trophic levels the amount of usable energy left becomes so negligibly small that it cannot sustain any further population of organisms.

(c) Numerical Solution:
  1. Green plants trap only 1% of total sunlight falling on their leaves:
    Energy trapped by Plants = 1% of 20,000 J = 200 J
  2. Deer (Herbivore) gets 10% of plant energy:
    Energy to Deer = 10% of 200 J = 20 J
  3. Tiger (Carnivore) gets 10% of deer energy:
    Energy to Tiger = 10% of 20 J = 2 J

The energy available to the tiger is 2 Joules.

Board Exam Tip: Common error: Plants capture only 1% of incident solar energy, NOT 10%! 10% applies between subsequent trophic levels.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Ozone Formation & Destruction

What is ozone and how does it protect the biosphere? Explain the two-step chemical reaction involved in the formation of ozone in the upper stratosphere. What was the Montreal Protocol?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Ozone & Protection:

Ozone is a triatomic molecule made of three oxygen atoms (O3). It is deadly poisonous at ground level, but in the stratosphere, it forms a protective shield that absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation from the Sun, protecting humans from skin cancer, cataracts, and immune disorders.

Formation Reactions:
  1. High energy UV rays split molecular oxygen (O2) into free nascent oxygen atoms (O):
    O2 →UV radiation O + O
  2. These reactive oxygen atoms combine with molecular oxygen to form ozone:
    O + O2 → O3 (Ozone)
Montreal Protocol (1987):

An international treaty signed under the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) where world nations agreed to freeze and phase out the production of ozone-depleting CFCs.

Board Exam Tip: Write both reactions: (1) O2 -> O + O, and (2) O + O2 -> O3.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2019 Decomposers & Ecosystem

What is the role of decomposers in an ecosystem? What will happen if all decomposers (bacteria and fungi) are completely eliminated from the biosphere?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Role of Decomposers:
  • Decomposers (microorganisms like bacteria and fungi) break down complex organic matter from dead plants, animal carcasses, and excreta into simple inorganic substances.
  • These inorganic nutrients are returned to the soil, water, and air, where they are reabsorbed by producers (plants).
  • They act as natural cleaners of the environment by disposing of organic debris.
Consequences of Eliminating Decomposers:
  1. Dead bodies of plants and animals and waste heaps would pile up indefinitely across the planet, causing severe environmental contamination.
  2. Biogeochemical nutrient cycling (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus) would cease completely. The soil would lose its fertility, depriving green plants of essential minerals and ultimately leading to the collapse of all food webs and life on Earth.
Board Exam Tip: Decomposers replenish natural nutrient cycles and maintain soil fertility.
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Solid Waste & Eco-friendly Alternatives
  1. Differentiate between biodegradable and non-biodegradable wastes with two examples of each.
  2. Why did the Indian Railways' initiative to replace plastic disposable cups with clay cups (kulhads) on trains run into problems? Why are disposable paper cups now preferred?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Biodegradable vs Non-Biodegradable:
  • Biodegradable Waste: Substances that can be broken down into non-toxic simple substances by the action of biological microorganisms over time.
    Examples: Fruit and vegetable peels, paper, cotton cloth, cow dung.
  • Non-Biodegradable Waste: Synthetic substances that cannot be broken down by biological saprophytes due to lack of specific enzymes, persisting in nature for hundreds of years.
    Examples: Plastic bags, DDT, glass bottles, metal cans.
2. The Kulhad vs Paper Cup Issue:
  • Problems with Kulhads (Clay cups): Making millions of clay cups daily required enormous quantities of fertile topsoil, stripping agricultural fields of their productive soil layer and leading to topsoil loss.
  • Why Disposable Paper Cups are Preferred: They are biodegradable, can be recycled, are lightweight to transport, and do not consume agricultural topsoil.
Board Exam Tip: Kulhads cause topsoil depletion. Paper cups are biodegradable, recyclable, and environmentally sustainable.
