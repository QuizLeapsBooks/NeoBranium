Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2021 MCQ

A student tests dry HCl gas with dry blue litmus paper and then with moist blue litmus paper. What will be the observation?

  • (A) No colour change with dry litmus; turns red with moist litmus
  • (B) Turns red with dry litmus; no change with moist litmus
  • (C) Turns red with both dry and moist litmus papers
  • (D) Turns blue in both cases
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (A)

Dry HCl gas does not contain hydrogen ions (H+). Hydrogen ions are only produced in the presence of water (forming hydronium ions, H3O+):

HCl + H2O → H3O+ + Cl-

Since acidic properties are exhibited only by H+(aq) / H3O+ ions, dry litmus shows no change, but moist litmus turns red.

Board Exam Tip: CBSE loves asking about the role of moisture/water in displaying acidic character of dry HCl gas.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2019 MCQ

An aqueous solution turns red litmus paper blue. Excess addition of which of the following solution would reverse the change?

  • (A) Baking powder
  • (B) Lime water
  • (C) Ammonium hydroxide solution
  • (D) Hydrochloric acid
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (D)

Red litmus turns blue in a basic solution. To reverse this change (turn blue litmus back to red), an acid must be added in excess to neutralise the base and make the solution acidic. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is a strong acid.

Board Exam Tip: Baking powder, lime water, and ammonium hydroxide are all basic. Only HCl is acidic.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Reasoning

Why is it recommended that acid should be added to water slowly with constant stirring, and never water to acid?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

The process of dissolving an acid in water is a highly exothermic reaction.

  • If water is added to concentrated acid, the sudden heat generated may cause the acid mixture to splash out and cause severe chemical burns.
  • The glass container may also break due to excessive local heating.
  • When acid is added slowly to water with constant stirring, the heat released is absorbed by the large volume of water gradually and safely.
Board Exam Tip: Keywords: 'highly exothermic', 'splashing out can cause burns', 'breaking of glass container due to excessive heat'.
Q4 2 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Short Answer

Why does an aqueous solution of an acid conduct electricity, whereas glucose and alcohol solutions do not conduct electricity?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Electric current is carried through solutions by moving free ions.

  • When an acid dissolves in water, it dissociates into hydrogen ions (H+) or hydronium ions (H3O+) and corresponding anions, which conduct electricity:
    HCl + H2O → H3O+ + Cl-
  • Glucose (C6H12O6) and alcohol (C2H5OH) are covalent compounds. Although they contain hydrogen atoms, they do not ionise in water to produce H+ ions, hence they do not conduct electricity.
Board Exam Tip: Merely having hydrogen is not enough; formation of free ions in aqueous solution is essential for electrical conductivity.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2022 Preparation & Equation

What is Plaster of Paris (POP)?

  1. Write its chemical name and chemical formula.
  2. How is it prepared from gypsum? Write the balanced chemical equation with temperature condition.
  3. Why should POP be stored in a moisture-proof container?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Chemical Name and Formula:

Chemical Name: Calcium sulphate hemihydrate
Chemical Formula: CaSO4·½H2O (or 2CaSO4·H2O)

2. Preparation from Gypsum:

POP is prepared by heating Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) at 373 K (100°C). On heating, it loses three-fourths of its water of crystallisation:

CaSO4·2H2O →373 K CaSO4·½H2O + 1½ H2O

Note: Above 373 K, it loses all water and becomes dead burnt plaster.

3. Moisture-proof storage:

POP absorbs moisture from the atmosphere and sets into a hard solid mass (Gypsum) again, rendering it useless:

CaSO4·½H2O + 1½ H2O → CaSO4·2H2O [Hard Gypsum]
Board Exam Tip: Always specify 373 K or 100°C. If temperature exceeds this, dead burnt plaster forms without setting property.
Q6 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Industrial Process

Explain the Chlor-alkali process.

  1. Why is it called the 'Chlor-alkali' process?
  2. Write the balanced chemical equation.
  3. Name the gases evolved at the anode and cathode, and write one important use of each product.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Why called Chlor-alkali:

It is called the Chlor-alkali process because of the products formed: 'chlor' for chlorine gas and 'alkali' for sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

2. Chemical Equation:
2NaCl(aq) [Brine] + 2H2O(l) →Electricity 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) ↑ + H2(g) ↑
3. Products at electrodes & their uses:
  • At Anode (+): Chlorine gas (Cl2) – Used in water treatment (disinfectant), manufacturing PVC, CFCs, and bleaching powder.
  • At Cathode (-): Hydrogen gas (H2) – Used as rocket fuel, in making ammonia for fertilisers, and margarine.
  • Near Cathode: Sodium hydroxide solution (NaOH) – Used for de-greasing metals, soap and detergent making, and paper manufacturing.
Board Exam Tip: Anode = Cl2 (gas), Cathode = H2 (gas), NaOH forms near cathode. This is a favorite 3-mark CBSE question.
Q7 3 Marks CBSE 2022, 2018 Daily Life Application

Explain the role of pH in the following daily life situations:

  1. Tooth decay and how toothpaste prevents it.
  2. Digestive system during indigestion and how antacids provide relief.
  3. Self-defence by honeybee sting or stinging hair of nettle leaves.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Tooth Decay (pH < 5.5):

Tooth enamel (calcium hydroxyapatite) corrodes when pH in the mouth falls below 5.5. Bacteria degrade sugar/food debris to produce acids. Using basic toothpastes neutralises the excess acid and prevents decay.

2. Digestive System & Acidity:

Our stomach produces HCl (pH ~ 1.2 to 2) for digestion. During indigestion, excess acid causes pain and irritation. Mild bases called antacids (e.g., Milk of Magnesia - Mg(OH)2, Sodium hydrogen carbonate - NaHCO3) neutralise excess acid.

3. Bee sting & Nettle plant:

A bee sting or nettle hair injects methanoic acid (formic acid) causing burning pain. Applying a mild base like baking soda (for bee sting) or rubbing dock plant leaf (basic sap) neutralises the acid.

Board Exam Tip: Remember exact pH values: tooth decay begins below pH 5.5; human blood and body functions ideally between pH 7.0 to 7.8.
Q8 3 Marks CBSE 2020, 2019 Salts & Water of Crystallisation

What is meant by 'water of crystallisation'? Describe an experiment using copper sulphate crystals to demonstrate that salts contain water of crystallisation.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Definition:

Water of crystallisation is the fixed number of water molecules chemically bonded in one formula unit of a salt. E.g., in blue vitriol (CuSO4·5H2O), there are 5 molecules of water per formula unit.

Demonstration Experiment:
  1. Take a few blue crystals of copper sulphate in a dry test tube and heat gently over a burner flame.
  2. Observation 1: The blue colour disappears and the crystals turn white (anhydrous CuSO4). Tiny water droplets condense on the cooler inner walls of the test tube.
  3. Reaction:
    CuSO4·5H2O(s) [Blue] →Heat CuSO4(s) [White] + 5H2O(g)
  4. Observation 2: When 2–3 drops of water are added back to the white anhydrous powder, the blue colour is immediately restored.
Board Exam Tip: Formula of copper sulphate is CuSO4.5H2O, Washing soda is Na2CO3.10H2O, and Gypsum is CaSO4.2H2O.
Q9 5 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Long / Comprehensive

Give the chemical formula, preparation equation, and two major industrial uses for each of the following common salts:

  1. Baking Soda (Sodium hydrogen carbonate)
  2. Washing Soda (Sodium carbonate decahydrate)
  3. Bleaching Powder (Calcium oxychloride)
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Baking Soda – NaHCO3:

Preparation: Produced on a large scale using the Solvay process by reacting cold concentrated brine with ammonia and carbon dioxide:

NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 → NH4Cl + NaHCO3 ↓
  • Uses: (i) Ingredient in baking powder (releases CO2 on heating to make cakes soft and spongy). (ii) Antacid to neutralise stomach acidity. (iii) In soda-acid fire extinguishers.
2. Washing Soda – Na2CO3·10H2O:

Preparation: Recrystallisation of anhydrous sodium carbonate (soda ash):

Na2CO3 + 10H2O → Na2CO3·10H2O
  • Uses: (i) Cleansing agent for domestic laundry. (ii) In glass, soap, and paper industries. (iii) For removing permanent hardness of water.
3. Bleaching Powder – CaOCl2:

Preparation: Action of chlorine gas on dry slaked lime [Ca(OH)2]:

Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 → CaOCl2 + H2O
  • Uses: (i) Bleaching cotton and linen in textile industry and wood pulp in paper factories. (ii) Disinfectant for sterilising drinking water.
Board Exam Tip: In baking powder, baking soda is mixed with edible tartaric acid to neutralise bitter-tasting sodium carbonate produced on heating.
Q10 5 Marks CBSE 2023 Sample Paper Case-Based / Identification

A compound 'X' of sodium is used as an antacid and in fire extinguishers. When 'X' is heated strongly, it gives a compound 'Y', water, and a gas 'Z'. Gas 'Z' turns lime water milky.

  1. Identify compound 'X', 'Y', and gas 'Z'.
  2. Write the balanced chemical equation for the thermal decomposition of 'X'.
  3. How is 'Y' converted into washing soda? Write the chemical equation.
  4. What happens when 'X' is added to dilute hydrochloric acid? Write the reaction.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Identification:
  • Compound 'X': Sodium hydrogen carbonate / Baking soda (NaHCO3)
  • Compound 'Y': Anhydrous sodium carbonate / Soda ash (Na2CO3)
  • Gas 'Z': Carbon dioxide (CO2)
2. Thermal Decomposition of 'X':
2NaHCO3(s) →Δ Na2CO3(s) + H2O(l) + CO2(g) ↑
3. Conversion of 'Y' into Washing Soda:

Recrystallisation of sodium carbonate from water gives washing soda:

Na2CO3 + 10H2O → Na2CO3·10H2O (Washing soda)
4. Reaction with dilute HCl:
NaHCO3(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g) ↑

Brisk effervescence of CO2 gas is observed.

Board Exam Tip: Identification questions testing 'X', 'Y', 'Z' conversions are worth 4 to 5 marks and appear in almost every CBSE board paper.
