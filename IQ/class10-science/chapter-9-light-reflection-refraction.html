Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

A convex lens has a focal length of 20 cm. Its optical power in dioptres (D) is:

  • (A) +2 D
  • (B) -5 D
  • (C) +5 D
  • (D) +0.05 D
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (C)

Power of a lens is given by P = 1 / f (in metres). Given f = +20 cm = +0.20 m.

P = 1 / (+0.20) = +5 D (Dioptres)
Board Exam Tip: Always convert focal length in cm into metres before calculating power in dioptres (P = 1/f).
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

To obtain a real, inverted image of the same size as the object using a convex lens, where should the object be placed?

  • (A) At the principal focus F1
  • (B) At twice the focal length 2F1
  • (C) Between optical centre O and focus F1
  • (D) At infinity
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

When an object is placed at 2F1 (centre of curvature) in front of a convex lens, a real, inverted image of the same size is formed at 2F2 on the opposite side (magnification m = -1).

Board Exam Tip: Object at 2F1 -> Image at 2F2, real, inverted, same size, m = -1.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Laws & Reasoning
  1. State the two laws of refraction of light.
  2. State Snell's Law and express it mathematically.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Two Laws of Refraction:
  1. The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal to the interface of two transparent media at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.
  2. Snell's Law: The ratio of the sine of angle of incidence (i) to the sine of angle of refraction (r) is a constant for a light of given colour and for a given pair of media.
    sin(i) / sin(r) = constant = n21 (Refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1)
Board Exam Tip: Don't forget to state: 'for a light of given colour and for a given pair of media' when stating Snell's Law.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2018 Mirrors / Practical Applications

Give reasons for the following:

  1. Why are convex mirrors used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles?
  2. Why are concave mirrors used by dentists and in shaving mirrors?
  3. Why are concave mirrors used in solar furnaces and car headlights?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Convex mirror as rear-view mirror:

(i) It always forms an erect, diminished virtual image of vehicles behind. (ii) It curves outwards, providing a much wider field of view than a plane mirror.

2. Concave mirror for dentists/shaving:

When an object is placed between the pole (P) and principal focus (F) of a concave mirror, it produces an erect, virtual, and highly magnified image, allowing dentists to see clear enlarged views of teeth.

3. Solar furnaces & headlights:
  • Solar furnaces: Large concave mirrors focus parallel rays of sunlight to the focal point, generating extreme concentrated heat.
  • Headlights: The bulb is placed at the principal focus of the concave reflector to produce a powerful parallel beam of light.
Board Exam Tip: Dentist mirror uses the specific case: Object between P and F -> erect, virtual, magnified.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Numerical (Mirror Formula)

An object 4.0 cm in size is placed at a distance of 25.0 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15.0 cm. At what distance from the mirror should a screen be placed in order to obtain a sharp image? Find the nature and size of the image.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Given Data (Sign Convention):
  • Height of object, h = +4.0 cm
  • Object distance, u = -25.0 cm
  • Focal length of concave mirror, f = -15.0 cm
Step 1: Calculate Image Distance (v):

Using mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f

1/v = 1/f - 1/u = 1/(-15) - 1/(-25) = -1/15 + 1/25 = (-5 + 3) / 75 = -2 / 75
v = -75 / 2 = -37.5 cm

The screen should be placed at 37.5 cm in front of the mirror (negative sign indicates on the same side as object).

Step 2: Calculate Height of Image (h'):
m = -v / u = h' / h
h' = - (v / u) × h = - [(-37.5) / (-25)] × 4.0 = - (1.5) × 4.0 = -6.0 cm
Nature of Image:

The image is real, inverted (negative h'), and magnified (height = 6.0 cm).

Board Exam Tip: Remember sign conventions: Concave mirror has negative focal length (f = -15 cm). Object distance u is always negative.
Q6 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2019 Numerical (Lens & Power)

A convex lens forms a real and inverted image of a needle at a distance of 50 cm from it. Where is the needle placed in front of the convex lens if the image is equal to the size of the object? Also, find the power of the lens.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Step 1: Finding Object Distance (u):

Given: Image is real, inverted, and of the same size as the object → Magnification m = -1.

For a lens, m = v / u = -1 → v = -u → u = -v

Since image distance v = +50 cm (real image on opposite side), object distance u = -50 cm.

The needle is placed at 50 cm in front of the lens (at 2F1).

Step 2: Finding Focal Length (f):

Since object is at 2F, 2f = 50 cm → f = 25 cm = +0.25 m.
(Or using lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u = 1/50 - 1/(-50) = 2/50 = 1/25 → f = +25 cm)

Step 3: Calculating Power of Lens (P):
P = 1 / f (in metres) = 1 / (+0.25 m) = +4.0 D

The power of the convex lens is +4.0 Dioptres.

Board Exam Tip: For convex lens: real inverted same-size image means v = 2f = 50 cm, hence f = 25 cm = 0.25 m.
Q7 4 Marks CBSE 2024, 2017 Refraction / Glass Slab

Describe the refraction of light through a rectangular glass slab.

  1. Explain why the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray.
  2. What is lateral displacement?
  3. State three factors on which lateral displacement depends.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Why Emergent Ray is Parallel to Incident Ray:

At the first air-glass interface, the ray of light travels from an optically rarer medium (air) to a denser medium (glass), bending towards the normal (angle of refraction r1 < i). At the second glass-air interface, it bends away from the normal by an equal amount (angle of emergence e = angle of incidence i). Since the two opposing refracting faces are parallel, the emergent ray is parallel to the original incident ray.

2. Lateral Displacement:

The perpendicular distance between the original path of the incident ray and the emergent ray coming out of the glass slab is called lateral displacement.

3. Factors Affecting Lateral Displacement:
  1. Thickness of the glass slab: Directly proportional to thickness (thicker slab = greater displacement).
  2. Angle of incidence: Increases with increase in angle of incidence.
  3. Refractive index of glass: Directly proportional to refractive index.
  4. Wavelength: Inversely proportional to wavelength (violet light suffers more lateral shift than red light).
Board Exam Tip: Angle of incidence (i) equals angle of emergence (e). Lateral displacement increases with slab thickness.
Q8 5 Marks CBSE 2024 Sample Paper, 2021 Lens Numerical & Ray Diagram

A concave lens has a focal length of 15 cm. At what distance should an object be placed from the lens so that it forms an image at 10 cm from the lens? Also, calculate the magnification produced by the lens and state the nature of the image formed.

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Given Data:
  • A concave lens always forms a virtual, erect image on the same side as the object → Image distance v = -10 cm
  • Focal length of concave lens is always negative → f = -15 cm
  • Object distance u = ?
Step 1: Lens Formula:

1/v - 1/u = 1/f → 1/u = 1/v - 1/f

1/u = 1/(-10) - 1/(-15) = -1/10 + 1/15 = (-3 + 2) / 30 = -1 / 30
u = -30 cm

The object should be placed at a distance of 30 cm in front of the lens.

Step 2: Magnification (m):
m = v / u = (-10 cm) / (-30 cm) = +1/3 = +0.33
Nature of Image:

Positive magnification (m > 0) and m < 1 confirms that the image is virtual, erect, and diminished to one-third of the object's size.

Board Exam Tip: In concave lens, both v and f are ALWAYS negative. Magnification is positive and less than 1.
