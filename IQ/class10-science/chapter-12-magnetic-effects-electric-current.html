Q1 1 Mark CBSE 2024, 2022 MCQ

The magnetic field inside a long straight current-carrying solenoid is:

  • (A) Zero at all points
  • (B) Decreases as we move towards its ends
  • (C) Increases as we move towards its ends
  • (D) Uniform and the same at all interior points
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (D)

Inside a long current-carrying solenoid, magnetic field lines are straight, parallel, and equidistant lines. This indicates that the magnetic field is uniform throughout the interior of the solenoid.

Board Exam Tip: Magnetic field pattern inside a solenoid resembles that of a bar magnet. Inside lines are uniform parallel lines.
Q2 1 Mark CBSE 2023, 2020 MCQ

To find the direction of the magnetic field produced around a straight current-carrying conductor, which rule is applied?

  • (A) Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
  • (B) Right-Hand Thumb Rule (Maxwell's Corkscrew Rule)
  • (C) Fleming's Right-Hand Rule
  • (D) Lenz's Law
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:

Correct Option: (B)

The Right-Hand Thumb Rule gives the direction of concentric magnetic field lines around a straight current-carrying conductor. Imagine holding the wire in your right hand with the thumb pointing in current direction – curled fingers point in magnetic field direction.

Board Exam Tip: Right-Hand Thumb Rule = Field around wire. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule = Force on wire in magnetic field.
Q3 2 Marks CBSE 2024, 2018 Properties of Field Lines

State four important characteristics of magnetic field lines. Why can no two magnetic field lines ever intersect each other?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Four Characteristics:
  1. They emerge from the North pole and merge at the South pole outside the magnet; inside the magnet, they travel from South to North, forming continuous closed loops.
  2. The degree of closeness (density) of lines represents the relative strength of the magnetic field (stronger near the poles).
  3. The tangent drawn at any point on a field line gives the direction of the magnetic field at that point.
  4. They never cross or intersect each other.
Why Lines Never Intersect:

If two field lines were to intersect at a point, a compass needle placed at that point of intersection would have to point in two different directions simultaneously, which is physically impossible.

Board Exam Tip: Reason why field lines never cross is a guaranteed 1-mark or 2-mark question in boards.
Q4 3 Marks CBSE 2023, 2019 Solenoid & Electromagnet
  1. What is a solenoid? Draw the pattern of magnetic field lines of a current-carrying solenoid.
  2. How can a solenoid be converted into an electromagnet? What core material is used and why?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Solenoid & Field Pattern:

A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder is called a solenoid.

Its magnetic field lines resemble the magnetic field of a bar magnet, with one end behaving as a North pole and the other as a South pole. Inside the solenoid, the field lines are parallel and equidistant (uniform field).

2. Making an Electromagnet:

A strong magnetic field inside a solenoid is used to magnetise a piece of magnetic material like Soft Iron placed inside the coil.

  • Why Soft Iron Core is used: Soft iron has high magnetic permeability and loses its magnetism completely as soon as current is turned off (temporary magnet). Steel is not used because it retains permanent magnetism.
Board Exam Tip: Soft iron core makes a temporary electromagnet (demagnetises when current stops). Steel makes a permanent magnet.
Q5 3 Marks CBSE 2024, 2020 Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

State Fleming's Left-Hand Rule. An electron enters a uniform magnetic field directed vertically downwards at right angles to the field from west to east. What will be the direction of force experienced by the electron?

Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
Statement of Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:

Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of your left hand mutually perpendicular to each other:

  • Forefinger: Points in the direction of the Magnetic Field (B)
  • Middle finger: Points in the direction of conventional Electric Current (I)
  • Thumb: Points in the direction of Motion / Force (F) acting on the conductor.
Application to Electron:
  • Electron travels from West to East. Since conventional current is taken opposite to the flow of electrons, current direction (middle finger) is from East to West.
  • Magnetic field (forefinger) is directed Vertically Downwards.
  • Applying Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the thumb points towards the South.
  • Result: The electron experiences a magnetic force directed towards the South.
Board Exam Tip: Be careful: Current is OPPOSITE to electron movement. For an electron moving East, current is West!
Q6 4 Marks CBSE 2023, 2017 Domestic Circuit Safety
  1. What are the three types of wires used in domestic electrical circuits? Give the standard insulation colour code and voltage for each.
  2. What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it essential to earth metallic appliances like refrigerators and electric irons?
  3. Differentiate between short-circuiting and overloading.
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Wire Types & Colours:
  • Live Wire (Phase): Red or Brown insulation (Maintains +220 V potential).
  • Neutral Wire: Black or Blue insulation (Maintains 0 V potential).
  • Earth Wire: Green or Yellow insulation (Ground potential 0 V).
2. Function of Earth Wire:

The green earth wire is connected to a metal plate buried deep in the ground. In appliances with metallic bodies (geyser, iron, cooler), the body is connected to this wire. If insulation fails and live wire touches the metal casing, current immediately flows safely to the ground through the low-resistance earth wire, triggering the fuse and protecting the user from a fatal electric shock.

3. Short-Circuiting vs Overloading:
  • Short-Circuiting: Occurs when live and neutral wires come into direct contact due to faulty insulation or appliance defect. Circuit resistance drops to near zero, causing an enormous surge of current that can cause electric fires.
  • Overloading: Occurs when too many high-power appliances (AC, heater, iron) are connected simultaneously to a single socket, drawing current beyond the safe carrying limit of the wires.
Board Exam Tip: Earthing provides a low-resistance path for leakage current to prevent electric shocks.
Q7 5 Marks CBSE 2024, 2021 Electric Motor / Principle & Working

Explain the principle, construction, and working of an Electric Motor.

  1. State the fundamental principle on which an electric motor works.
  2. What is the function of the split-ring commutator?
  3. What is the function of the carbon brushes?
Step-by-Step Solution & Marking Scheme:
1. Principle:

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. It works on the principle that when a current-carrying rectangular coil is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences equal and opposite forces on its two parallel arms (given by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule), creating a torque that rotates the coil continuously.

2. Function of Split Rings (Commutator):

The split ring acts as a commutator – a device that reverses the direction of current flowing through the coil after every half-rotation (180°). Reversing the current reverses the direction of forces on the arms, ensuring that the coil continues to rotate in the same direction continuously.

3. Function of Carbon Brushes:

Stationary carbon brushes maintain sliding electrical contact with the rotating split rings, feeding current from the external battery into the rotating armature coil without tangling wires.

Board Exam Tip: Commutator reverses current every half rotation, ensuring unidirectional continuous rotation.
